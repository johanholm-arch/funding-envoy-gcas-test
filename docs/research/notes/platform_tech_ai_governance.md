Research method note: findings below come from web searches run on 26 Oct 2026. Direct page fetches to vendor and regulator domains (veeva.com, steeprockinc.com, iqvia.com, envisionpharmagroup.com, fda.gov, efpia.eu, cov.com etc.) were blocked by the session's egress proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), and the shared web-search budget was exhausted mid-task, so several items could only be captured from search summaries rather than full documents. ## KQ1. Competitive landscape: which platforms pharma/medtech use for grants, donations, sponsorships and IIT/IIS management

### Takeaway
The pharma-specific segment is small and consolidating: MediSpend (merged with RLDatix Life Sciences in Sept/Oct 2025, which had earlier absorbed Porzio Life Sciences), IQVIA Grants & Funding Management (on OCE, launched Nov 2021), SteepRock GGS, Envision Pharma's iEnvision (Corporate Funding / Medical Education / ISR modules) and the brand-new Veeva Grants (announced 2025, "Early", zero customers on the Sept 2026 product sheet). Big-pharma public portals are frequently run on generic corporate-grantmaking tools (Pfizer on Bonterra/CyberGrants) or in-house portals (Novartis NGCS, being replaced by a new platform launching 16 Nov 2026). No source found documented an AI feature in any pharma-specific grants module as of Oct 2026.

**Requestor and coordinator pain points (documented)**
- A 2007 CME-provider survey found grant application procedures the most-cited challenge, with respondents citing pharma's move to online grant processes, "long decision times, the lack of direct access to grant reviewers, the varying and inconsistent policies of grantors" — [MM+M Med Ed Report 2007](https://www.mmm-online.com/med-ed-report-cmes-open-gesture/article/24765/)
- Vendor framing (CyberGrants) of the coordinator problem: manual tracking in multiple systems makes it "difficult to gain a holistic view… constantly hopping from one system to another, which leaves room for error" — [CSRwire](https://csrwire.com/?p=42071)
- Pfizer "may demand a CME grant recipient return funding if the data it submits is inaccurate" (Sunshine-era contract changes) — [Pink Sheet](https://pink.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/PS074944/Sunshine-Act-Prompts-Pharma-Companies-To-Alter-Grant-Contracts)
- Prior-art patents on pharma grant systems describe requestor self-service profiles with status tracking and exclusion-list screening as core functions — [US8589308](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8589308); [US7885900](https://patents.justia.com/patent/7885900)

### Inferences
- The pharma-specific vendor set is effectively four-to-five players (MediSpend/RLDatix-Porzio, IQVIA, SteepRock, Envision, Veeva-new); all are positioned as compliance-first workflow tools, none advertises AI in the grants module, and the largest sponsors still run generic corporate-giving platforms (Bonterra/CyberGrants) or home-grown portals. A new entrant's differentiators are plausibly (a) requestor UX across sponsors, (b) AI-assisted triage with auditable human oversight, (c) multi-tenant managed operations for mid-size companies.
- Public portal requirements converge on a standard document set (letter of request on letterhead, needs assessment, agenda, itemized budget, accreditation/tax-exempt proof, W-9/tax ID, outcomes plan) and 60–120-day lead times; this is a de-facto schema a multi-tenant intake form can standardize.
- Novartis' migration off NGCS (cutover Nov 2026) signals active RFP/replacement activity among large sponsors in 2026.

### Gaps
- No pricing for SteepRock, MediSpend, IQVIA, Envision, or Veeva Grants is public; no G2/Capterra review pages for MediSpend or SteepRock were found. Fluxx/SmartSimple/Bonterra review-site weakness queries could not be run (search budget exhausted).
- No source names which vendor powers the Lilly, J&J, AbbVie, Gilead or new Novartis portals.
- No public pharma conference talk or RFP write-up on grants-system weaknesses was found; the only provider-complaint evidence is from 2007.
- "GlobalGrants" (Porzio) could not be confirmed as a product. Advarra/Polaris IIT module, Reed Tech, Diligent, Submittable and Medidata grants capabilities: no relevant sources surfaced.

## KQ3. Security and assurance expected by pharma procurement for SaaS handling HCO/HCP data

### Takeaway
Pharma TPRM typically requests SOC 2 Type II and/or ISO 27001, with HITRUST reserved for high-risk PHI vendors, and uses the Shared Assessments SIG/SIG Lite (140–200 questions) as questionnaire backbone; 21 CFR Part 11 formally applies to GxP records, so a grants system is normally non-GxP, but FDA's audit-trail expectations (who/what/when/why, protected from modification) are the pattern customers ask for anyway.

### Cited Findings
- Assurance artifacts to request from vendors: SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001, HITRUST CSF, HIPAA assessments — [AccountableHQ pharmacy vendor checklist](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/pharmacy-vendor-security-assessment-checklist-how-to-evaluate-third-party-risk-and-compliance)
- HITRUST is "the strongest option for high-risk PHI vendors", healthcare-tailored, accepts SOC 2/ISO 27001 as evidence, but is "rigorous but resource-intensive"; SOC 2 "is not healthcare-specific"; ISO 27001 "needs supplementary healthcare-specific standards" — [Censinet](https://censinet.com/perspectives/hitrust-certification-third-party-risk-assessment-role)
- SIG Lite is typically 140–200 questions; "most enterprise clients will accept a clean SOC 2 in lieu of a filled-out SIG Lite", SIG remains the fallback for vendors without SOC 2 — [Macktez SIG Lite](https://macktez.com/university/sig-lite); SIG 2024 framework description — [SmartSuite SIG 2024](https://www.smartsuite.com/frameworks/shared-assessments-sig-2024-standardized-information-gathering-questionnaire)
- Pharma vendor reviews historically centered on GxP/quality; cybersecurity is now core due diligence, with sample questions on documented ISMS, encryption, penetration testing, incident notification — [Freyr Solutions](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/index%2Ephp/blog/cyber-risk-considerations-in-pharma-vendor-assessments)
- Sample third-party vendor security assessment questionnaire (community health centers) — [CCALAC sample questionnaire PDF](https://ccalac.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/Sample-Third-Party-Vendor-Security-Assessment-Questionnaire.pdf)
- FDA's Part 11 scope guidance ("Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and Application") is the authoritative scoping document — [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/part-11-electronic-records-electronic-signatures-scope-and-application)
- Part 11 requirements cover "GxP computer systems that create, modify, maintain, archive, retrieve or transmit electronic records", excluding systems merely incidental to paper records — [GMPSOP computer system standard](https://gmpsop.com/?p=9333)
- Part 11 may still apply where records support GxP activities or regulatory decisions even if not submitted to FDA — [InformaIT Part 11 assumptions](https://informait.com/news/21-cfr-part-11-the-compliance-assumptions-pharma-teams-make-that-fda-keeps-challenging)
- FDA audit-trail expectations: capture changes, who made them, when, and ideally why; protect audit trails from modification and from being disabled; predicate rules still apply — [Technology Networks audit trail article](https://www.technologynetworks.com/biopharma/articles/audit-trail-requirements-for-a-digitalized-regulated-laboratory-401729)
- Record retention reference points: federal grant records "up to seven years from the expiration of the grant" depending on agency; FDA-regulated study records 2 years after last marketing approval (21 CFR 312.62) — [U. Illinois OPRS](https://oprs.research.illinois.edu/node/124685); Medicare Part D records 10 years (42 CFR 423.505) — [AccountableHQ medication records](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/how-long-should-medication-records-be-kept-legal-requirements-and-best-practices)

## KQ4. AI governance for an agentic grants platform

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations have been deferred by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I), while Article 50 transparency and GPAI obligations stayed on 2 Aug 2026; US state law has fragmented (Colorado repealed/re-enacted as an ADMT statute effective 1 Jan 2027; Texas TRAIGA effective 1 Jan 2026 without the Colorado-style impact-assessment regime; California CCPA ADMT rules phase in 2026–2027); DOJ's Sept 2024 ECCP asks how companies govern their own AI use; ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable management-system standard and NIST AI RMF + AI 600-1 the voluntary reference. Enterprise HITL patterns combine action-type/risk tiers with confidence bands and policy rules rather than confidence alone.

### Cited Findings

**EU AI Act timeline (post-Omnibus)**

**Frameworks**
- ISO/IEC 42001 is certifiable by accredited bodies (ideally meeting ISO/IEC 42006); NIST AI RMF is voluntary with no certification; NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile, 2024) extends the RMF; the two are complementary (RMF = risk substance, 42001 = auditable management system requiring AI policy, internal audit records, management review); neither establishes EU AI Act compliance by itself; sources conflict on Annex A control counts (38/39 vs "nine") — [SecureW2](https://securew2.com/blog/nist-ai-rmf-vs-iso-42001); [CASRAI AI 600-1](https://casrai.org/news/nist-ai-rmf-generative-ai-profile-ai-600-1-explained); [CASRAI ISO 42001](https://casrai.org/wp/?p=1468); [IS Partners](https://www.ispartnersllc.com/blog/iso-42001-vs-nist-ai-rmf/); [Elevate](https://elevateconsult.com/insights/nist-ai-rmf-vs-iso-42001-for-teams-building-ai-controls/)

**Human-in-the-loop / autonomy-dial patterns**
- Confidence scores alone should not control approval; combine action type, transaction value, account sensitivity, data completeness and policy exceptions (example rule set with a 0.90 confidence floor) — [DZone HITL enterprise agents](https://dzone.com/articles/human-in-the-loop-approval-enterprise-ai-agents)
- Models are "often most confident when it has misunderstood", so pure thresholds let the worst mistakes through — [Xano](https://www.xano.com/blog/the-human-in-the-loop-isnt-a-constraint-its-how-you-trust-autonomy/)
- Tiered routing example (LangGraph-style): auto-execute ≥0.9, human approval 0.6–0.9, full handoff <0.6 — [liziu HITL patterns](https://liziu.com/ai-agents/human-in-the-loop.html)
- Require approval for irreversible, costly, sensitive or hard-to-undo actions; over-gating overloads reviewers who then rubber-stamp; actions repeatedly approved without modification are candidates for controlled automation; approval packets should include proposed action, confidence components, evidence and the policy reason — [Rioja](https://alejandrorioja.com/human-in-the-loop-ai-agents-when-to-build-an-approval-gate/); [GAAS](https://gaas.it.com/how-to-implement-human-in-the-loop-approvals); [Tray.ai](https://tray.ai/blog/human-in-the-loop-ai-agents/)
- Gateway-layer enforcement: an AI gateway can block or pause outbound requests to an MCP server/internal API until an approval token is attached — [Gravitee](https://www.gravitee.io/corpus/gen-1762/the-nine-billion-names-of-god-collection/human-in-the-loop-controls-and-approval-gates-for-high-risk-ai-agent-actions.html)

### Inferences
- A grants platform's AI (triage, completeness checks, drafting, risk flags) interacts with natural persons (requestors), so Article 50 transparency duties (disclose AI interaction; label AI-generated content where applicable) apply from 2 Aug 2026 regardless of risk tier. Whether funding-decision support for organisations is "high-risk" under Annex III is not settled in sources found; Annex III's listed areas (employment, education, essential services/credit, etc.) do not obviously include corporate grant decisions to organisations, so limited-risk treatment is the more likely reading, but a documented classification assessment per tenant deployment is prudent (gap below).
- Pharma customers will map vendor AI controls to DOJ ECCP expectations (own-use AI risk management, monitoring) and to ISO 42001 artefacts: model inventory, intended-use statements, human-oversight design, logging of prompts/outputs, evaluation sets and drift monitoring, and contractual no-training-on-customer-data terms.
- Practical autonomy dial: per-tenant, per-action policy table (observe → suggest → act-with-approval → act-and-notify) keyed on action reversibility and monetary/compliance impact, plus confidence bands as a secondary signal.

### Gaps
- Could not source Annex III text analysis for "assessing funding requests from organisations", Utah AI Policy Act details, California SB 53, Illinois law specifics, or any published pharma internal AI-governance/vendor requirement (model inventory, prompt logging, no-training clauses) — searches not run due to budget exhaustion.

## KQ5. Identity and user management (SSO, SCIM, external identities, MFA, roles, tenant isolation)

### Takeaway
Enterprise-side identity follows a settled pattern (SAML/OIDC SSO with Entra ID/Okta plus SCIM provisioning keyed on a stable externalId); for a many-tenant SaaS, a multi-tenant app registration or a managed auth provider is recommended over per-customer IdP wiring; for data isolation on Postgres, AWS guidance mandates RLS on every tenant table in the pool model with tenant context set per request.

### Cited Findings
- SCIM keeps the Entra directory and the SaaS in sync; SAML sign-in is a separate prerequisite configured on the Microsoft side — [Buf SCIM/Entra docs](https://buf.build/docs/bsr/admin/instance/scim/azure-saml/); Palantir Entra SCIM docs — [Palantir](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/authentication/scim-entra-id)
- Map the Entra object ID to SCIM externalId as the stable identifier; most schema errors trace to that mapping — [SSOJet Entra SCIM](https://ssojet.com/docs/en/enterprise-sso/directory-sync/integrations/entra-id-scim)
- A SaaS accepting Entra SAML from many customers should "use a managed authentication provider unless you have a strong reason to own the SAML, SCIM, certificate, and XML-validation burden yourself" — [Clerk Entra SAML part 2](https://clerk.com/articles/microsoft-entra-id-saml-integration-for-saas-apps-2.md); Stytch multi-tenant SCIM with Entra — [Stytch docs](https://stytch.com/docs/multi-tenant-auth/enterprise-ready/scim/microsoft-entra.md)
- Community (Apr 2026) answer: for B2B SaaS, a multi-tenant workforce app registration that external Entra tenants consent to scales better than a custom OIDC IdP per customer — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/a/12723289)
- Postgres pool model: "Row-level security (RLS) is required to maintain tenant data isolation in a pool model"; enable RLS on all tenant tables; set tenant context at runtime rather than creating a DB user per tenant; silo/bridge/pool are the three partitioning models — [AWS Prescriptive Guidance RLS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/saas-multitenant-managed-postgresql/rls.html); [AWS best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/saas-multitenant-managed-postgresql/best-practices.html)
- RLS should be an "infrastructure safety net, not your primary authorization gate"; resolve tenant from a validated JWT claim and keep app-level filters — [ClickHouse multi-tenant Postgres guide](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/multi-tenant-saas-postgres-architecture)
- Each joined table needs its own RLS policy; stale tenant GUC under connection pooling causes leaks — reset session variables on checkout; index tenant_id; automate policy checks in CI — [Edana RLS guide, June 2026](https://edana.ch/en/2026/06/25/ensuring-multi-tenant-data-isolation-with-postgresql-row-level-security-rls/); [OneUptime RLS, Jan 2026](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-01-25-row-level-security-postgresql/view)
- Schema-per-tenant gives stronger isolation but harder migrations/patching and more expensive cross-tenant analytics — [Edana](https://edana.ch/en/2026/06/25/ensuring-multi-tenant-data-isolation-with-postgresql-row-level-security-rls/)
- Unverified micro-benchmark: 100k rows/1,000 tenants, RLS added ~0.4 ms (3.2 vs 3.6 ms) — [Skyline Codes Substack](https://skylinecodes.substack.com/p/how-to-architect-a-multi-tenant-saas)

### Inferences
- Recommended design: OIDC/SAML federation per tenant for internal users (Entra/Okta/Ping) with SCIM 2.0 provisioning and group→role mapping; a separate B2C-style identity realm (Entra External ID, Auth0 or similar) for requestors using email magic links/passkeys with MFA step-up; RBAC with tenant-scoped roles plus ABAC attributes (therapeutic area, country, request type, amount band) for routing/visibility; pool-model Postgres with RLS as defence-in-depth behind application-level tenant scoping, tenant-scoped encryption keys for documents.

## KQ6. Audit trail standards (immutable, hash-chained, AI actions as first-class events, WORM, exportability)

### Takeaway
The converged design is an application-side hash chain (each entry commits to the previous hash) plus periodic chain-head anchoring to WORM storage (S3 Object Lock, compliance mode) and optionally RFC 3161 timestamps, with continuous automated verification and export-then-tombstone retention; AI-action events should be recorded in the same chain with model/prompt/sources/output/decision fields.

### Cited Findings
- Hash chain: each entry's hash includes the previous entry's hash; a verify walk detects the first divergence — [DEV: tamper-evident audit log in NestJS](https://dev.to/elwin_ernst/building-a-tamper-evident-audit-log-in-nestjs-with-hash-chains-4l76)
- A chain is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: an attacker with full DB access can recompute it, so an external anchor (hash published to an immutable location at intervals) is needed — [Archman auditing & tamper-evident logs](https://archman.dev/docs/data-architecture-and-persistence/data-lifecycle-and-compliance/auditing-and-tamper-evident-logs)
- S3 Object Lock prevents overwrite/deletion for a retention period, even for the account owner; compliance mode is stronger than governance mode when operators are untrusted — [Gravitee tamper-evident logging](https://www.gravitee.io/corpus/gen-1025/title-44-of-the-united-states-code/tamper-evident-logging-for-content-changes.html); [OneUptime immutable audit pipeline, Feb 2026](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-02-06-immutable-audit-log-pipeline-otel/view)
- RFC 3161 trusted timestamps on batch boundaries provide a time-stamped commitment to chain state; seal segments frequently to limit rollback windows; replicate to a separate security account; run continuous automated verification — [Gravitee](https://www.gravitee.io/corpus/gen-1025/title-44-of-the-united-states-code/tamper-evident-logging-for-content-changes.html)
- Deleting rows breaks the chain; retention must use export-then-tombstone; GDPR erasure must be handled without breaking integrity — [DEV NestJS article](https://dev.to/elwin_ernst/building-a-tamper-evident-audit-log-in-nestjs-with-hash-chains-4l76)
- Ship audit events to a dedicated append-only sink (Postgres table with UPDATE/DELETE revoked, WORM object store, or managed audit service), separate from console logs — [OneUptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-02-06-immutable-audit-log-pipeline-otel/view)
- FDA audit-trail expectation (who/what/when/why, protected from modification/disabling) as the regulatory benchmark customers reference — [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/biopharma/articles/audit-trail-requirements-for-a-digitalized-regulated-laboratory-401729)

### Inferences
- AI audit event schema (first-class, same chain): event_id, tenant_id, actor (agent id + model name/version + prompt/template version), inputs (request id, retrieved sources with hashes), prompt and output (or content hashes with retrievable blobs), confidence/score components, policy evaluated and gate outcome, human decision (accept/modify/reject, who, when, reason), downstream action ids. Exports: per-tenant signed JSON/CSV with chain-verification manifest for auditors.

### Gaps
- No standards body document specifically prescribing AI-action audit fields was found; the schema above is an inference from HITL and audit-log sources.

## KQ7. Database, scale and NLQ analytics architecture

### Takeaway
Hundreds of concurrent users and ~500k+ rows is modest for Postgres with tenant-indexed RLS, partition pruning and read replicas; the hard problem is NLQ: raw-schema text-to-SQL accuracy is low on realistic benchmarks (Spider 2.0 ~10–21% for general models; BIRD ~55% for GPT-4 with evidence), while vendor-reported semantic-layer approaches claim 87–98%+ on curated metrics — so NLQ should run over a governed semantic layer with RLS propagated from the warehouse/DB.

### Inferences
- Architecture: Postgres (RLS + tenant_id-leading composite indexes; optional list/hash partitioning by tenant for the largest tables; read replicas for reporting), an OpenSearch/pg_trgm search index for free-text, and a governed semantic layer (Cube / dbt Semantic Layer / equivalent) exposing metrics and dimensions to an NLQ agent via a constrained query API rather than free SQL; propagate tenant and role context into the semantic layer's security context so NLQ cannot bypass RLS; log every NLQ (question, generated query, result hash) to the audit chain; show the generated query/metric definitions to the user for verifiability.

## KQ8. Integration patterns (Salesforce, DocuSign, SAP, Coupa/Workday/Oracle, Adobe Sign, Dynamics; iPaaS)

## KQ9. Workflow/SOP-driven configuration, form builders, versioning, SOP→workflow generation, multi-tenant theming

### Takeaway
Camunda (BPMN, business-readable, SaaS from ~$99/mo, tenant-id or engine-per-tenant isolation) and Temporal (code-first durable execution, MIT-licensed, Cloud from $25/mo + $25 per million actions) are the two reference engines; LLM SOP-to-workflow generation is research-grade in 2025–2026 (JPMorgan SOPStruct DAGs, BPMN collaboration models from SOPs, FLOW-BENCH), with fine-tuning and few-shot CoT giving the best control-flow accuracy; no production "SOP→BPMN" product was found.

### Cited Findings
- Camunda is BPMN-based and suits cases where non-developers must read the process; Temporal is code-first (Go, Java, TypeScript, Python) for technical audiences; Camunda has closed-source components with only Zeebe source-available; Temporal is MIT; Camunda SaaS from $99/month; Temporal Cloud from $25/month + $25 per million actions (March 2026 snapshot) — [AutomationAtlas Camunda vs Temporal 2026](https://automationatlas.io/answers/camunda-vs-temporal-2026/); [Temporal community thread](https://community.temporal.io/t/temporal-vs-camunda-8/10570); [Akka Temporal alternatives](https://akka.io/blog/temporal-alternatives)
- Camunda multi-tenancy: one engine per tenant, or one engine with tenant identifiers stored in a column — [Camunda multi-tenancy docs](https://docs.camunda.org/manual/user-guide/process-engine/multi-tenancy/)
- SOPStruct (JPMorgan AI Research, Mar 2025): converts SOPs to decision-tree DAGs (not BPMN) via segmentation → structure generation → evaluation (PDDL planner soundness + LLM-judged completeness) — [arXiv 2504.00029](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00029)
- BPMN collaboration modeling from real-world SOPs (Springer, listed 2026): participant extraction → entity extraction → collaboration modeling, including message flows and data perspective — [Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/bpmn-based-business-process-collaboration-modeling-using-large-l/51773402)
- LLM process-model generation (IJCNLP-AACL 2025): fine-tuning gave best accuracy; few-shot CoT handled nested gateways/parallel branches and incomplete inputs better — [ACL Anthology author page](https://aclanthology.org/people/zhongsheng-wang/)
- FLOW-BENCH / FLOW-GEN (EMNLP 2025 Industry): conversational generation of enterprise workflows with benchmark — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2025.emnlp-industry.100)

### Inferences
- Pattern: store workflow definitions (BPMN or JSON DSL) and form schemas (JSON Schema + UI schema) as versioned, tenant-scoped artefacts; running requests pin the definition version they started on; LLM assists by drafting a workflow/form from an uploaded SOP into the DSL for human review, with diff + validation (soundness checks) before publish — consistent with the SOPStruct evaluation approach.
- Theming: per-tenant design tokens (colors, typography, logo, email templates) rendered as CSS variables, with white-label requestor portals on tenant domains.

### Gaps
- Workflow versioning specifics (Camunda 8 vs Temporal), Form.io/JSON Forms/formly comparison, and design-token/white-label references could not be searched (budget exhausted).

## KQ10. Accessibility, localization and e-signature legality

### Takeaway
The European Accessibility Act applied from 28 June 2025; EN 301 549 v3.2.1 currently maps to WCAG 2.1 AA (a WCAG 2.2 update is expected but unverified), B2B-only SaaS scope is contested, and overlays do not count; UK e-signatures rest on the Electronic Communications Act 2000 and retained UK eIDAS with a permissive approach where higher tiers carry little special status; ESIGN is the US statute (15 U.S.C. §7001) and does not govern UK/EU contracts.

### Cited Findings
- EAA compliance required by 28 June 2025 — [Allyant EAA checklist](https://allyant.com/eaa-compliance-checklist-key-steps-to-meeting-2025-accessibility-requirements/)
- EN 301 549 v3.2.1 "fully based on WCAG 2.1" / maps services to WCAG 2.1 AA; some vendor blogs say it incorporates 2.2; a WCAG 2.2-referencing update expected in the OJ "around late 2026" (unverified) — [Accessibility.works SaaS EAA guide](https://www.accessibility.works/blog/saas-eaa-compliance-european-accessibility-act-en-301-549-requirements/); [Sota EAA guide](https://sota.io/blog/european-accessibility-act-2025-saas-developer-compliance-wcag22-api-accessibility-enforcement-penalties-guide)
- Scope: pure B2B with only professional users generally outside direct scope, but self-serve B2B with consumer-facing sales may be in scope; another source argues all SaaS EU citizens can access is covered — [BuildMVPFast EAA decision tree 2026](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-saas-decision-tree-2026); [Flexmade](https://flexmade.com/insights/what-the-european-accessibility-act-means)
- WCAG 2.2 AA criteria relevant to SaaS: focus not obscured by sticky headers; accessible authentication (no cognitive tests like CAPTCHA-only login) — [Pretius WCAG/EAA](https://pretius.com/?p=19654)
- Overlay widgets do not count as conformance; European Commission rejected overlays for EN 301 549 — [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/?p=11022390)
- UK: e-signatures recognised under Electronic Communications Act 2000 and retained UK eIDAS (amended 2019); simple/advanced/qualified tiers "have little significance at present" as almost no UK legislation requires the higher tiers; parties may specify by contract — [DocuSign UK legality](https://www.docusign.com/en-gb/how-it-works/legality/global/the-united-kingdom)
- QES = qualified certificate from a qualified trust service provider + qualified signature creation device; rarely used in UK (no national ID cards); Registers of Scotland uses QES for conveyancing — [LexisNexis QES glossary](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/glossary/qualified-electronic-signatures); [Cryptomathic](https://www.cryptomathic.com/blog/qualified-digital-signing-the-electronic-execution-of-documents-in-england-wales)
- UK Law Commission 2019: e-signatures valid for the great majority of business transactions — [Lightico UK e-signature](https://www.lightico.com/blog/legal-considerations-of-esignatures-in-the-uk/); eIDAS has little practical effect on Scots/English signature law — [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/technology/eidas-a-new-framework-for-electronic-signatures/)
- Non-EU companies dealing with EU counterparties may need to consider eIDAS (vendor guide, 2026) — [Certyneo](https://certyneo.com/en/blog/eidas-regulation-non-eu-companies)

### Inferences
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA now (superset of 2.1 AA, which is the legally referenced level), publish an accessibility statement/VPAT per tenant portal, avoid CAPTCHA-only login for requestors (passkeys/magic links), and support RTL/multilingual content with tenant-managed translations. For attestations and letters of agreement, simple/advanced e-signatures (DocuSign/Adobe Sign) suffice in US/UK/most EU grant contexts; offer QES as an option for jurisdictions/customers that require it.

### Gaps
- Official EU harmonised-standard status of EN 301 549 vs WCAG 2.2 unverified; US ESIGN/UETA specifics, eIDAS 2.0 (EU Digital Identity Wallet) timelines, and multilingual CMS patterns were not researched (budget exhausted).
