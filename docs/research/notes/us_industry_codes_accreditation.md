## 1. PhRMA Code on Interactions with Health Care Professionals (current version effective 1 Jan 2022) — IME grants, institutional grants, charitable donations, speaker programs, "no influence" rules, separation from sales

### Takeaway
The PhRMA Code (first 2002; revised 2009, 2019, and in Aug 2021 with effect 1 Jan 2022) requires that CME support go only to the CME provider (never to individual HCPs or for attendee expenses), that the company have no control over content/faculty/venue and give no advice to the provider, and that grant-making be structurally separated from sales and marketing with objective criteria; the 2022 revision extended these principles to virtual events and tightened speaker-program rules (no alcohol, no repeat attendance, speaker selection by defined criteria, FMV pay unrelated to prescribing).

### Cited Findings
- Version history: "Originally published in 2002, the PhRMA Code on Interactions with Health Care Professionals (PhRMA Code) was updated in 2009, 2019, and again in 2021 (effective January 1, 2022)." — [IntuitionLabs compliance guide (secondary)](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/pharma-funding-physician-education-compliance); effective date Jan 2022 confirmed by [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-effect-january-2022) and [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-into-effect-january-2022/). The current Code PDF is hosted on PhRMA's CDN — [PhRMA Code (effective 1 Jan 2022) PDF](https://cdn.aglty.io/phrma/global/resources/PhRMA%20Code%20-%20Final.pdf).
- Scope extended to virtual events in the 2022 revision: the principles for company support of CME and third-party scientific/educational conferences and professional meetings "apply to virtual events as well as in-person," though the Code gives little further guidance on virtual programs — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-into-effect-january-2022/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-effect-january-2022).
- CME funding channel: law-firm summary states the Code provides that a company is "not to fund continuing medical education in any manner other than direct funding to the CME organization" — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-effect-january-2022). The same point appears in the Code text as reproduced by UCSF: financial support should be given directly to the CME provider, not to individual clinicians — [UCSF copy of PhRMA Code](https://meded.ucsf.edu/sites/meded.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/PhRMACode.pdf).
- No influence on content: summary of the Code reports that "the company should not provide any advice or guidance to the CME provider, even if asked by the provider" — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-effect-january-2022). The CME provider/organizer should control content, faculty and venue, and a supporting manufacturer should have "no control or input over these facets of a CME event" — [McGuireWoods 2008 alert on the 2009 Code](https://mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/Alerts/2008/7/PhRMA-Code-on-Interactions-with-Healthcare-Professionals); a 2008-era compliance guide summarises the Code as stating a company has "no responsibility or control" over faculty selection or content — [IntuitionLabs (secondary)](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/pharma-funding-physician-education-compliance).
- Purpose test: support for CME "should be intended to educate on a full range of treatment options and not to promote a particular medicine" — [UCSF copy of PhRMA Code](https://meded.ucsf.edu/sites/meded.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/PhRMACode.pdf).
- Separation from sales/marketing + objective criteria: the Code's CME section says a company "should separate its CME grant-making functions from its sales and marketing departments," and companies should "develop and apply objective criteria for making grant decisions" — [McGuireWoods 2008 alert](https://mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/Alerts/2008/7/PhRMA-Code-on-Interactions-with-Healthcare-Professionals); the same separation requirement appears in [OIG testimony to the Senate Special Committee on Aging, 29 Jul 2009](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/testimony/157/20090729_-_Morris.pdf), which says separating grant-making from sales and marketing "helps ensure grant funding is not influenced by sales or marketing motivations."
- Grants not tied to prescribing: trade-press summary of the Code: "no grants, subsidies, or similar support should be offered to a health care professional in exchange for prescribing products or for a commitment to continue prescribing products" — [Relias Media news brief](https://www.reliasmedia.com/articles/65965-news-briefs-phrma-adopts-code-on-marketing-relationships).
- Section inventory (2011-edition table of contents, cited by the US Supreme Court docket): sections include "Scholarships and Educational Funds" (Section 9) and "Independence and Decision Making" (Section 13) — [PhRMA Code copy on supremecourt.gov](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/URLs_Cited/OT2011/11-204/11-204-2.PDF). A crosswalk prepared by AdvaMed lists the PhRMA Code's treatment of "Educational & Research Grants, Charitable Donations, and Commercial Sponsorships" with subsections on third-party programs, independent research and charitable programs — [AdvaMed crosswalk, AdvaMed Code 2020 vs PhRMA Code 2009](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/crosswalk-advamed-code-phrma-code.pdf).
- Scholarship model in practice (company policy guided by the Code): Lilly's grant terms state "the sponsoring organization shall have sole discretion over the selection of the scholarship recipients and distribution of scholarship funds" — [Lilly Grant Office FAQ](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/faq).
- Speaker programs (2022 revision): PhRMA says speaker programs "are distinct from continuing medical education programs" and "companies and speakers should be clear about this distinction" — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2021/09/phrma-code-2022-speaker-programs-enter-a-prohibition-era). Alcohol should not be served; "Repeat attendance at a substantially similar event is not appropriate"; speakers should be selected on defined criteria independent of revenue generated — [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/revisions-to-the-phrma-code-on-interactions-with-health-care-professionals); [4As Health](https://4ashealth.org/2021/08/20/phrmas-updated-code-for-industry-addresses-speaker-programs-meals/). Payments to speakers and HCP consultants "should be fair market value, and should not take account of past or future product usage" — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2021/09/phrma-code-2022-speaker-programs-enter-a-prohibition-era). The Code "merely cautions against 'high-end' restaurants and focuses on the extravagance of the location," which is looser than the OIG Special Fraud Alert — [Corporate Compliance Insights](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/pharma-speaker-program/).
- The PhRMA Code is voluntary, but several states incorporate it by law (see Question 8) — [Hyman Phelps FDA Law Blog, Jan 2022](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2022/01/revised-phrma-code-took-effect-on-january-1-2022-and-certain-state-obligations-follow/).

### Inferences
- For a grants platform, the PhRMA rules translate into: (a) payee must be the accredited/organising entity (block individual HCP payees and attendee-expense line items); (b) a hard "no-contact" rule — no content/faculty/venue inputs, and the platform should not even expose a field for funder advice to the provider; (c) workflow role-separation enforced by system permissions (sales/marketing roles have no view or vote on grant decisions); (d) objective, documented review criteria captured as structured scoring; (e) attestation from requestor that support is not tied to prescribing/purchasing; (f) support for virtual activities handled identically to in-person.
- The 2022 revision's speaker-program rules are relevant to grants platforms only insofar as the platform must keep speaker programs (promotional, company-controlled) categorically separate from IME grants; a request that looks like a speaker program should be rejected from the grants channel.

### Gaps
- Exact verbatim 2022 text and section numbering for the CME section (believed to be Section 3 "Continuing Medical Education" in the 2009/2019 editions, with "Educational and Practice-Related Items", "Scholarships and Educational Funds", "Independence and Decision Making" as separate sections) could not be fetched; verify against the [PhRMA Code PDF](https://cdn.aglty.io/phrma/global/resources/PhRMA%20Code%20-%20Final.pdf).
- Unverified recollection (needs primary check): the CME section also states that "financial support should not be offered for the costs of travel, lodging, or other personal expenses of non-faculty healthcare professionals attending CME," that companies may provide "modest meals" only through the CME provider (not directly), and that grant decisions should be made by a function with no sales/marketing input. The search results confirmed the first and third points only through secondary summaries.
- No evidence found of any PhRMA Code revision after the 1 Jan 2022 edition (through Oct 2026); treat the 2022 edition as current but confirm on phrma.org.
- The Code does not itself prescribe documentation (budgets, reconciliation); such documentation expectations derive from OIG guidance and ACCME standards (see Questions 3 and 6).

## 2. AdvaMed Code of Ethics on Interactions with U.S. Health Care Professionals (Revised and Restated, effective 1 June 2023; no later revision found) — research/educational grants, charitable donations, sales exclusion, third-party conference grants, satellite symposia, documentation, HCO-run programs

### Takeaway
The 2023 AdvaMed Code consolidates all support of third-party programs into Section IV (educational grants, commercial sponsorships, research grants, charitable donations) and requires that sales personnel not be involved in educational-grant decisions and not control or unduly influence research-grant or donation decisions; grants go to the organiser (or an organiser-designated training institution), never directly to an individual HCP, and the organiser controls faculty, attendee selection and content, with grant funds usable only for Code-permissible items (modest meals, educational items), not gift baskets, entertainment or branded non-educational items.

### Cited Findings
- Version and date: "Revised and Restated AdvaMed U.S. Code of Ethics | Effective June 1, 2023" — [AdvaMed Code of Ethics PDF (file dated Sept 2025 upload)](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf); the "Educational Grant" definition header in the Code shows the June 1, 2023 effective date, and an earlier edition carried a June 1, 2022 date — [Globus Medical copy of 2022 Code](https://www.globusmedical.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022-AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Digital-002.pdf); 2023 copy mirrored by a member company — [Cardinal/CPO2 copy of 2023 AdvaMed Code](https://www.cpo2.com/siteassets/us/documents/pdf/2023-advamed-code-of-ethics-202501162.pdf). A member company (Exact Sciences) republished its own HCP code in November 2025 referencing the AdvaMed Code, suggesting the 2023 Code remained the operative baseline in late 2025 — [Exact Sciences Code of Conduct, Nov 2025](https://s22.q4cdn.com/877809405/files/doc_governance/2025/11/exact-sciences-code-of-conduct-on-interactions-with-health-care-professionals-november-2025.pdf).
- Structure: Section IV covers "Educational and Research Grants, Charitable Donations, and Commercial Sponsorships," with subsections on third-party programs, other third-party programs, independent research, and charitable programs — [AdvaMed 2023 Code PDF](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf). The consolidated section "covers companies may support 'Third-Party Programs' through 'Educational Grants' and/or 'Commercial Sponsorships'" and "sets forth new guidelines for supporting independent third-party research and supporting charitable programs" — [Gardner Law](https://gardner.law/news/does-your-compliance-program-meet-the-revised-advamed-code); the section "expands and clarifies the requirements for supporting independent research grant requests or charitable donations" — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/advamed-updates-code-ethics-interactions-health-care-professionals).
- Educational grant definition: support to a third party "to reduce the cost of providing education," and grants "are not offered for commercial sponsorship opportunities" — [EBG summary / Code definition](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/advamed-revises-code-of-conduct).
- Payee rules: a grant "can go directly to the Third-Party Program Organizer or, in some instances, to a training institution or other entity designated by the Third-Party Program Organizer. Support may not go directly to an individual Health Care Professional" — [AdvaMed Code excerpt via search](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf).
- Sales exclusion (educational grants): "In all cases, sales personnel may not be involved in grantmaking decisions" — [AdvaMed 2023 Code excerpt](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf).
- Sales controls (research grants): "A Company should establish controls for reviewing requests for research grants in order to ensure sales personnel do not control or influence selection of recipient organizations," and "the recipient should retain independent control over the research" — [AdvaMed Code flashcards (older edition)](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/advamed-code-flashcards.pdf).
- Charitable donations: "must be for bona fide charitable purposes and made only to bona fide charitable organizations"; "sales personnel are permitted to provide input into this process, though they cannot control or unduly influence the decision" — [AdvaMed Code excerpt](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf) and [Deloitte summary](https://www2.deloitte.com/us/en/pages/regulatory/articles/medical-device-code-of-ethics.html).
- Commercial sponsorship (distinguished from grants): the fee "should be a commercially reasonable fee in exchange for the marketing and promotional benefits received by the Company, such as advertising, signage, or exhibit space" — [AdvaMed Code excerpt](https://www.cpo2.com/siteassets/us/documents/pdf/2023-advamed-code-of-ethics-202501162.pdf).
- Review checklist: Section IV "includes a checklist that companies can use to review requests for educational grants" — [AdvaMed Code excerpt](https://www.cpo2.com/siteassets/us/documents/pdf/2023-advamed-code-of-ethics-202501162.pdf).
- Permitted/prohibited use of grant funds: a third-party organiser may use grant funds for items "permissible under the Code, such as modest meals, refreshments, and educational items"; prohibited items include "gift baskets, entertainment or recreational activities, and branded, non-edu[cational items]" — [AdvaMed Code excerpt](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf).
- Faculty selection: AdvaMed FAQ (earlier edition): "The Code does not preclude a Member from recommending a knowledgeable faculty member. The ultimate selection should be made by the conference sponsor." — [AdvaMed FAQ via search (older edition)](https://www.interamericancoalition-medtech.org/ethics/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/05/advamed_en.pdf).
- Satellite symposia definition (2023): "a Company-organized and funded program that is appended to a Third-Party Program agenda but that the Third-Party Organizer does not control," often held during meal breaks — [AdvaMed 2023 Code excerpt](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf). Company-conducted training/education is a separate section ("applies to Company-conducted training, education, or other business meetings") — same source. Older FAQ: it is appropriate to sponsor a sales/promotional/business meeting "scheduled at approximately the same time as an educational conference but ... conducted at a separate location" — [AdvaMed 2018-era FAQ](https://www.interamericancoalition-medtech.org/ethics/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/05/advamed_en.pdf).
- Global reach pointer: APACMed's 2023 Code of Ethical Conduct contains analogous satellite-symposium and third-party-program provisions (covered by other agents) — [APACMed Code 2023](https://apacmed.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/APACMed-LEC-Code-2023.pdf). A multilateral crosswalk of AdvaMed vs. other regional codes exists — [AdvaMed multilateral crosswalk 2020](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/advamed-code-crosswalk-multilateral-2020.pdf).
- Difference vs PhRMA: a law-firm comparison notes Connecticut makes AdvaMed provisions effectively mandatory for device makers and that the two codes differ in structure — [Kendall PC](https://kendallpc.com/differences-between-phrma-code-advamed/).

### Inferences
- Platform rules derivable from the Code: (1) request types must be mutually exclusive — educational grant vs commercial sponsorship vs research grant vs charitable donation vs company-conducted training — because each has a different decision-maker and different sales-involvement rule (no involvement for educational grants; input-but-no-control for donations; controls-to-prevent-influence for research); (2) payee validation: organiser or organiser-designated training institution only; (3) budget line-item validation: block gift baskets, entertainment, recreation, branded non-educational items; allow modest meals/refreshments, educational items; (4) requestor attestation that the organiser selects faculty/attendees and controls content; (5) a satellite symposium is not a grant and must be routed to a commercial/sponsorship channel with a "company-controlled" flag.
- The embedded educational-grant checklist in Section IV should be reproduced as a structured reviewer form (see Gaps for exact items).

### Gaps
- The verbatim Section IV text, including the educational-grant review checklist items, the research-grant "controls" language, and whether the 2023 Code includes specific guidance on grants to HCO-run (e.g., hospital-conducted) training programs, could not be retrieved; open the [2023 Code PDF](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf) Section IV and the AdvaMed FAQs.
- Unverified recollection: the Code's educational-grant checklist asks whether the organiser is a bona fide entity with objective criteria for selecting attendees, whether the program has an educational purpose, whether the grant is reasonable in amount and not tied to purchases, and whether the company has documented the request and decision; AdvaMed also permits grants to HCOs for "training and education" of HCPs where the HCO selects the trainees. These need primary confirmation.
- No evidence of an AdvaMed Code revision after June 2023 was found in search results through Oct 2026; confirm on advamed.org.
- Documentation-retention and written-agreement requirements under the AdvaMed Code were not visible in excerpts (the Code generally expects written agreements for consulting; whether it mandates written grant agreements must be checked).

## 3. ACCME Standards for Integrity and Independence in Accredited Continuing Education (released Dec 2020; mandatory from 1 Jan 2022; current PDF file dated 28 Oct 2024) — Standards 3, 4, 5; "ineligible company"; commercial support documentation and PARS reporting; Joint Accreditation and specialty accreditors

### Takeaway
The Standards replaced the Standards for Commercial Support on 1 Jan 2022 and are adopted across medicine (ACCME, AAFP, AOA), nursing (ANCC), pharmacy (ACPE), dentistry (ADA CERP, from June 2026) and Joint Accreditation; Standard 4 requires a written agreement executed before the activity, provider control of all receipt/disbursement decisions, no direct payment of expenses by the ineligible company, no support to individual learners, a record of amount/nature/use producible on request, and pre-activity learner disclosure of supporter name and nature of in-kind support without logos; Standard 3 requires 24-month look-back disclosure with no dollar threshold and exclusion of ineligible-company owners/employees from content control; Standard 5 requires a 30-minute/space separation of marketing and non-accredited education; PARS requires per-activity reporting of each supporter and monetary amount, with in-kind flagged by source but not valued.

### Cited Findings
- Dates: "The Standards were released in December 2020 and went into effect on January 1, 2022, replacing the Standards for Commercial Support" — [UCF CPD policy page](https://med.ucf.edu/continuous-professional-development/policies/standards-for-integrity-and-independence/); the former Standards for Commercial Support "expired 12/31/21" — [ACCME archived SCS PDF](https://accme.org/sites/default/files/2020-12/174_20201210_ACCME_Standards_for_Commercial_Support.pdf). Current official PDF — [ACCME Standards PDF (file stamp 20241028)](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/884_20241028_standardsforintegrityandindependenceinaccreditedcontinuingeducation-1.pdf); information package — [ACCME info package (2022)](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/881_20220623_Standards-for-Integrity-and-Independence-in-Accredited-CE-Information-Package.pdf); toolkit — [ACCME toolkit](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/883_20201210_standards_for_integrity_and_independence_toolkit.pdf); identification/mitigation tools — [ACCME Key Steps tool](http://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/885_20201210_Tools-for-Identifying-Mitigating-and-Disclosing-Relevant-Financial-Relationships.pdf).
- Ineligible company definition: "Companies that are ineligible to be accredited in the ACCME System (ineligible companies) are those whose primary business is producing, marketing, selling, re-selling, or distributing healthcare products used by or on patients" — [ACCME Standards (Weill Cornell copy)](https://teach.weill.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/accme_standards_for_integrity_and_independence_in_accredited_continuing_education.pdf); ACCME's eligibility page lists examples including device makers and dietary supplement sellers — [ACCME Eligibility rule](https://accme.org/rule/eligibility/); institutional policy example — [WUSTL Ineligible Company Policy](https://cme.wustl.edu/planners/ineligible-company-policy/).
- Standard 3 (Identify, Mitigate, Disclose): look-back "within the prior 24 months (changed from the current requirement of 12 months)" — [ACCME info package](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/881_20220623_Standards-for-Integrity-and-Independence-in-Accredited-CE-Information-Package.pdf); "There is no minimum financial threshold; individuals must disclose all financial relationships, regardless of the amount, with ineligible companies" — [ACCME Standard 3 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/identify-mitigate-and-disclose-relevant-financial-relationships/); Standard 3.2: "Owners or employees of ineligible companies must be excluded from controlling content. Includes exceptions to this exclusion" — [ACCME toolkit](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/883_20201210_standards_for_integrity_and_independence_toolkit.pdf). Changes from the old SCS included dropping the spouse/partner disclosure requirement and exempting non-clinical activities from identify/mitigate/disclose — [ISMA summary](https://www.ismanet.org/PDF/OneSource/UpdatedStandardsCriteria-WhatYouNeedtoKnow.pdf). Example 2026 disclosure form in use — [AHN 2026 Disclosure Form](https://cme.ahn.org/sites/default/files/media/2025-10/2026_Disclosure_Form.pdf).
- Standard 4 applicability: "applies only to accredited education that receives financial or in-kind support from ineligible companies" — [ACCME Standard 4 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/).
- Standard 4.1 (Decision-making and disbursement): the provider "must make all decisions regarding the receipt and disbursement of the commercial support"; "Ineligible companies must not pay directly for any of the expenses related to the education or the learners"; support may be used for honoraria or travel for planners and faculty "but not for individual learners"; the provider "may use commercial support to defray or eliminate the cost of the education for all learners" — [ACCME Standard 4 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); [AAFP credit eligibility requirements (adopting same text)](https://www.aafp.org/cme/credit-system/eligibility-requirements.html); [Marshall University summary](https://jcesom.marshall.edu/continuing-medical-education/accme-standards-for-integrity-and-independence/).
- Standard 4.2 (Agreement): "the terms and purposes of the support must be recorded in a written agreement between the company and the provider," and the agreement "must be executed prior to the start of the accredited education" — [ACCME Standard 4 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/). ACCME expects the agreement to be between the accredited provider and the commercial supporter, executed by both before the activity; if a joint provider or third party receives/disburses funds the agreement should name it; third parties may be included but cannot sign in the provider's place — [ACCME Standard 4 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/). The superseded SCS 3.4–3.6 similarly required the written agreement to include the provider even when funds go to an educational partner, name the commercial interest, and be signed by both supporter and provider — [ACCME archived SCS](https://accme.org/sites/default/files/2020-12/174_20201210_ACCME_Standards_for_Commercial_Support.pdf).
- Standard 4.3 (Accountability): the provider "must keep a record of the amount or kind of commercial support received and how it was used, and must produce that accounting, upon request" — [ACCME Standard 4 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); [FACS Commercial Support Guidelines](https://www.facs.org/for-medical-professionals/education/cme-resources/commercial-support-guidelines/).
- Standard 4.4 (Disclosure to learners): "The accredited provider must disclose to the learners the name(s) of the ineligible company(ies) that gave the commercial support, and the nature of the support if it was in-kind, prior to the learners engaging in the education," and the disclosure must not include corporate or product logos, trade names or product-group messages — [ACCME Standard 4 rule page](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); [PAMED guidelines (rev. Mar 2025)](https://www.pamedsoc.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/cme/accreditation-info-center/additional-resources/cme-planning-resources/guidelines-for-financial-support-and-commercial-promotion-in-conjunction-with-a-cme-activity.pdf?Status=Master&sfvrsn=9f918a8_1).
- Standard 5 (Ancillary activities): marketing, exhibits and non-accredited education developed by or with influence from an ineligible company "must not occur in the educational space within 30 minutes before or after an accredited education activity"; non-accredited parts of an event "must be clearly labeled and communicated as such"; Standard 5 applies only when ineligible-company marketing or non-accredited education is tied to the accredited activity — [UCF CPD policy](https://med.ucf.edu/continuous-professional-development/policies/standards-for-integrity-and-independence/); [AOE Consulting synopsis](https://www.aoeconsulting.com/cme-news-item/synopsis-of-accmes-standards-for-integrity-and-independence/).
- Institutional LOA practice: TTUHSC requires the CME office and the commercial supporter's representative to sign and accepts either the school's or the supporter's letter of agreement "provided it is an unrestricted grant and meets ACCME requirements" — [TTUHSC policy 60-05](https://www.ttuhsc.edu/medicine/continuing-medical-education/documents/cme_policies/60-05-Appropriate-Use-of-Commercial-Support.pdf); a university template includes a mutual statement that supporter and provider will comply with ACCME standards and that the provider will furnish "documentation of receipts and expenditures on request" — [USC agreement for commercial support form](https://app.smartsheet.com/b/form/12f1e9b26866493284d51f7f4e8d3877); UConn publishes its LOA for commercial support and exhibitor/advertising rules — [UConn Health LOA page](https://health.uconn.edu/continuing-medical-education/overview/required-activity-forms/letter-of-agreement-for-commercial-support).
- PARS reporting: commercial support is "any monetary or in-kind contributions given by an ACCME-defined ineligible company that is used to pay all or part of the costs of a CME activity"; providers "must report commercial support information, including the commercial support source(s) and amount received, for each individual activity that received commercial support" — [ACCME data reporting FAQ](https://accme.org/data-reporting/data-reporting-quick-answers/); in-kind: select/type the supporter name and mark in-kind; "you do not need to provide additional information about the nature of the in-kind support" in PARS — [ACCME PARS CME Activity Tutorial, Nov 2021](https://accme.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/936_20211116_CME%20Activity%20Tutorial.pdf); ACCME announced in 2011 it "will no longer require providers to quantify the dollar value of in-kind commercial support" — [Policy & Medicine, Jul 2011](https://policymed.com/2011/07/accme-news-in-brief.html); older in-kind categories (durable equipment, facilities/space, disposable supplies, tissue, other) and advice not to estimate dollar value — [ISMA Commercial Support presentation](https://www.ismanet.org/pdf/OneSource/CommercialSupport.pdf); ACCME FAQ "How do we report in-kind support?" — [ACCME FAQ](https://accme.org/node/6144).
- 2026 ACCME activity: ACCME posted a Compliance Check titled "Disclosing In-Kind Commercial Support" dated 26 Aug 2026, repeated in its September 2026 newsletter — [ACCME Announcements](http://accme.org/news-publications/announcements/). ACCME launched certificate programs in 2025, one covering implementation of the Standards for Integrity and Independence — [ACCME Certificate Programs packet (2025)](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACCME-Certificate-Programs-Enrollment-Packet-1.pdf). ACCME's May 2024 "Funding Playbook" addresses bringing financial support into accredited education — [ACCME Funding Playbook (May 2024)](http://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/1038_20240514_Funding_Playbook.pdf).
- Joint Accreditation (ACCME/ACPE/ANCC): Standard 2 requires that "all decisions related to the planning, faculty selection, delivery, and evaluation of accredited education are made without any influence or involvement from the owners and employees of an ineligible company," bars marketing in accredited education, and prohibits sharing learner contact information with ineligible companies without explicit consent; Standard 3 requires 24-month disclosure with no minimum threshold — [Joint Accreditation FAQ](https://jointaccreditation.org/faq/); glossary definition of ineligible companies — [Joint Accreditation Guide to the Accreditation Process (Aug 2023)](https://jointaccreditation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/1014_20230829_guide_for_providers_to_the_joint_accreditation_process.pdf); jointly accredited providers must follow ADA CERP and ANCC credit requirements when awarding those credits — [Joint Accreditation credit requirements](https://jointaccreditation.org/credit-requirements-ipce-credit-mark/).
- Joint providership: an ACCME-defined ineligible company "cannot take the role of the non-accredited entity in a joint provider relationship" — [CME Outfitters Joint Providership FAQ](https://www.cmeoutfitters.com/joint-providership-faq/).
- AAFP: requires CME to "fully comply with the ACCME Standards for Integrity and Independence," with the provider making all receipt/disbursement decisions and a written agreement signed before the education — [AAFP CME Credit System Eligibility Requirements](https://www.aafp.org/cme/credit-system/eligibility-requirements.html); [AAFP Credit System Policy](https://www.aafp.org/about/policies/aafp-creditsystem).
- AOA: "a commercial interest is not eligible for AOA accreditation"; commercial support defined as monetary or in-kind contributions used to pay all or part of the costs of a CME activity — [AOA Accreditation Requirements for Category 1 CME Sponsors (2023)](https://osteopathic.org/wp-content/uploads/Accreditation-Requirements-2023.pdf); AOA guide for commercial supporters: not permissible to use commercial funds for "travel, lodging, registration fees, honoraria, or personal expenses for non-faculty attendees" — [AOA Guide for Commercial Supporters (copy at UNE)](https://www.une.edu/sites/default/files/AOA_cme-commercial-sponsors-guide.pdf).

### Inferences
- Minimum data model for an ACCME-compliant commercial-support record in a grants platform: supporter legal name (ineligible company), activity identifier/title, dates, monetary amount OR in-kind flag + nature description, written agreement (both signatures, execution date must precede activity start), payee = accredited provider (or named joint provider/third party as disbursing agent, never as signatory substitute), budget and actual use ledger (to satisfy 4.3 "produce that accounting upon request"), learner disclosure text (name only, no logos), and a Standard 5 attestation that any exhibits/non-accredited sessions are separated by 30 minutes/space.
- Because every major US health-profession accreditor has adopted the same Standards (medicine, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry from 2026, Joint Accreditation), a single rule set can be applied to all "accredited CE" requests, with accreditor-specific credit statements captured as metadata rather than different compliance logic.
- The Aug 2026 ACCME compliance check on in-kind support indicates in-kind disclosure is an active audit theme; the platform should force a textual "nature of in-kind support" field even though PARS no longer requires it, because Standard 4.4 learner disclosure does.

### Gaps
- Verbatim text of Standards 1 and 2 and of 4.1–4.4/5.1–5.3 could not be downloaded (proxy block); numbering above (4.1 decision-making, 4.2 agreement, 4.3 accountability, 4.4 learner disclosure; 5.1 separation, 5.2 labeling, 5.3 learner-information sharing) follows the ACCME rule page structure and the UCF/AOE summaries but should be verified in the [official PDF](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/884_20241028_standardsforintegrityandindependenceinaccreditedcontinuingeducation-1.pdf).

- ACCME annual data report figures (total commercial support dollars and share of provider income for 2023/2024/2025) were not retrieved; the ACCME Data Report series should be consulted.

## 4. FDA's 1997 "Guidance for Industry: Industry-Supported Scientific and Educational Activities" and its continuing influence on "independence" tests

### Takeaway
FDA's final guidance (62 FR 64074, 3 Dec 1997, Docket 92N-0434) sets out twelve non-dispositive factors FDA weighs in deciding whether an industry-supported activity is independent (and therefore outside labeling/advertising regulation); although parts were found unconstitutional as applied in Washington Legal Foundation v. Friedman (D.D.C. 1998–99), the factor list (control of content and selection of presenters/moderators, disclosures, focus of program, provider–supporter relationship, provider involvement in sales/marketing, etc.) remains the template for industry "independence" criteria in grant policies.

### Cited Findings
- Identity and purpose: FDA published a final guidance "Industry-Supported Scientific and Educational Activities" that "describes how industry may support scientific and educational activities without being subject to regulation under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act" — [Federal Register, 3 Dec 1997 (62 FR 64073)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-12-03/pdf/97-31741.pdf); FDA lists it as December 1997, Docket 92N-0434, preceded by the 1992 "Draft Policy Statement on Industry-Supported Scientific and Educational Activities" — [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/industry-supported-scientific-and-educational-activities); copy on regulations.gov — [Guidance PDF (FDA-1992-N-0007-0005)](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FDA-1992-N-0007-0005/attachment_1.pdf); also mirrored at HHS — [HHS guidance listing](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/industry-supported-scientific-and-educational-activities-guidance-industry).
- Core distinction: FDA separates company-produced promotional materials from activities "otherwise independent from the promotional influence of the supporting company"; only truly independent, non-promotional activities fall outside FDA regulation — [Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-12-03/pdf/97-31741.pdf).
- Twelve factors, holistic application: a court opinion describes the guidance as identifying "twelve factors to be used in determining the propriety of manufacturer sponsorship of CME and similar programs" — [Washington Legal Foundation v. Friedman, 36 F. Supp. 2d 16 (D.D.C. 1999)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/36/16/2413102/); the guidance states "These factors will be considered as part of an overall evaluation of an activity; no individual factor is likely by itself to stimulate an action based on lack of independence" — [Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-12-03/pdf/97-31741.pdf).
- Factor content confirmed from the FR preamble: the draft's "Supporting Company Involvement in Content" discussion was folded into the factor "Control of Content and Selection of Presenters and Moderators"; a factor was added that "when an activity includes discussion of unapproved uses, there should be general disclosure of that fact"; the draft's "Focus on a Single Product" discussion was incorporated into "Focus of the Program" — [Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-12-03/pdf/97-31741.pdf). FDA looks at whether the provider kept full control over content and speaker/moderator choice and whether the company scripted presentations or targeted points for emphasis — same source.
- Factor questions as summarised in a peer-reviewed table: "Disclosure: Has funding for the program and any legal or business relationships between the company, providers, and presenters been disclosed, particularly for an off-label discussion?"; "Provider's sales or marketing activities: Are provider employees also producing marketing or promotional programs?"; "was the audience generated by a sales or marketing department?" — [P&T journal table (PMC2799128)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2799128/table/t2-ptj34_8p428).
- Themes: a law-firm presentation summarises the guidance's focus as "(1) Balance; (2) Objectivity; (3) Independency; and (4) Scientific Rigor" — [FDLI presentation (Russell/Gillian, 2022)](https://www.fdli.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Russell-Gillian.pdf); FDA permits industry-supported CME when independent of company influence and bars promotion of off-label uses in programs that are not truly independent — same source.
- Legal status: in WLF v. Friedman the court held the FDA guidances on industry-supported activities restricted manufacturers' ability to discuss off-label uses and found them unconstitutional (as applied); the 1999 opinion refers to the guidances as "one of those found unconstitutional by the Court last July" — [Justia, WLF v. Friedman](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/36/16/2413102/). Industry nonetheless continues to treat the 1997 factors as the operative independence test — [Policy & Medicine, "Role of the FDA" (2014)](https://policymed.com/2014/01/transforming-professional-medical-education-role-of-the-fda.html).
- Later FDA statements: FDA's 2023–2024 draft/final guidance on "Communications From Firms to Health Care Providers Regarding Scientific Information on Unapproved Uses of Approved/Cleared Medical Products" (SIUU) addresses firm-initiated scientific communications, not independent CME — [FDA SIUU guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/184871/download); an FDLI 2021 presentation on "Dissemination of Non-Promotional Information" situates the 1997 guidance within that framework — [FDLI Banuelos 2021](https://www.fdli.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Banuelos-Heather.pdf).

### Inferences
- A grants platform's "independence" screen can mirror the FDA factors as requestor attestations/reviewer checks: (1) provider controls content and selects presenters/moderators; (2) disclosure of funding source and relationships and of off-label discussion; (3) program not focused on a single product; (4) provider is not a sales/marketing arm of the supporter and provider staff don't also produce promotional programs; (5) audience not selected by sales; (6) opportunity for discussion/questions; (7) dissemination controlled by provider; (8) no ancillary promotional activities tied to the program; (9) no history of complaints. These map cleanly onto ACCME Standards 1–5.

### Gaps
- The verbatim titles of all twelve factors could not be fetched. Unverified recollection of the list: (1) Control of content and selection of presenters and moderators; (2) Disclosures; (3) The focus of the program; (4) Relationship between provider and supporting company; (5) Provider involvement in sales or marketing; (6) Provider's demonstrated failure to meet standards; (7) Multiple presentations; (8) Audience selection; (9) Opportunities for discussion; (10) Dissemination; (11) Ancillary promotional activities; (12) Complaints. Verify against [62 FR 64074](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-12-03/pdf/97-31741.pdf).
- No FDA statement after 1997 that formally withdraws, reaffirms or updates the guidance was found; FDA's guidance page still lists it as a final guidance (as of the search), but current legal effect post-WLF should be confirmed by counsel.

## 5. Outcomes measurement conventions in grant reporting (Moore levels 1–7) and typical reconciliation data

### Takeaway
Moore, Green & Gallis (2009, JCEHP) is the standard seven-level outcomes framework (1 participation, 2 satisfaction, 3A/3B declarative/procedural knowledge, 4 competence, 5 performance, 6 patient health, 7 community health; levels 1–5 provider-level, 6–7 patient/community), updated by Moore et al. in Medical Teacher (2018); funders routinely require an outcomes/assessment plan at application and a post-activity reconciliation (typically within 60 days) covering participation, budget-vs-actual and return of unused funds, but no funder-wide template dictating which Moore level must be reported was found.

### Cited Findings
- Framework origin: Moore, Green & Gallis, "Achieving Desired Results and Improved Outcomes: Integrating Planning and Assessment Throughout Learning Activities," J Contin Educ Health Prof (2009) — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227627769_Achieving_Desired_Results_and_Improved_Outcomes_Integrating_Planning_and_Assessment_Throughout_Learning_Activities); the authors "inserted the 4 levels of an assessment framework developed by Miller ... into the middle of the original CME outcomes framework" — [AO Foundation outcomes document](https://images.aofoundation.org/CIP/asset/download/Gallery/7291); in the seven-level form Competence is Level 4 and Performance Level 5 — [Comparison table](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Comparison-of-an-Expanded-Outcomes-Framework-with-the-Original-Framework-for-Planning_tbl1_298348302).
- Level structure: "Levels 1 to 5 are provider-level outcomes (participation, satisfaction, knowledge, competence, and performance) while levels 6 and 7 are patient- and community-level outcomes" — [Annals of Family Medicine 2022 supplement abstract](https://www.annfammed.org/content/20/Supplement_1/3106); Level 3 split into declarative (3A) and procedural (3B) knowledge, Level 4 "Learning: Competence (Shows How)" — [Charter Care outcomes measures chart](https://www.chartercare.org/globalassets/chartercare/pdfs/subsites/rwmc/outcomes_measures.pdf); Level 1 data source is "the activity records" — [NKF outcomes/objectives document (2016)](https://kidney.org/sites/default/files/Outcomes%20Objectives%20and%20Content%20Revised%205%2017%202016.docx_0.pdf); a provider policy codifies the "Seven-Level Outcomes Model" — [Academy for Continued Healthcare Learning Policy 6.2 (rev. Oct 2021)](https://academycme.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/6.2-The-Seven-level-Outcomes-Model.pdf).
- Measurement methods: chart reviews are one method to assess Level 5 (performance) — [PMC: Using chart reviews to evaluate a CME program](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10548990); sample evaluation tools for levels 2–7 — [Medical Society of Virginia sample evaluation methods](https://www.msv.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/3-doc_8_2016_published_sample_eval_meth_outcomes.pdf); CME professionals are "generally comfortable with data sources such as attendance records, questionnaires, pre- and posttests, observations and self-reported results, which are required for levels of Participation, Satisfaction, Knowledge and Competence (Levels 1–4)," with higher levels needing more complex data — [ACC "Sources of Data in CE"](https://www.acc.org/~/media/Non-Clinical/Files-PDFs-Excel-MS-Word-etc/Meetings/2017/Course%20PDFs/Emerging%20Faculty/Supplemental%20Docs/1%20Sources%20of%20Data%20in%20CE.pdf).
- 2018 update: Moore, Chappell, Sherman & Vinayaga-Pavan, "A conceptual framework for planning and assessing learning in continuing education activities designed for clinicians in one profession and/or clinical teams," Medical Teacher (2018), adds an expanded instructional-design approach aimed at improved clinical performance and patient health — [Moore et al. 2018 PDF](https://s9193.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Moore-Chappell-A-conceptual-framework-for-planning-and-assessing-learning-in-cE-activities-designed-for-clinicians-in-one-profession-and-or-clinical-teams.pdf); a 2021 critique notes the update focused on health professionals and interprofessional practice — [Teaching and Learning in Medicine 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/10401334.2021.1950540); ACEhp commentary "What's Next for the Outcomes Pyramid?" — [ACEhp Almanac](https://almanac.acehp.org/Outcomes/Outcomes-Article/outcomes-pyramid); CE vendor framing ("which of these is this activity actually trying to produce?") — [Oasis LMS](https://oasis-lms.com/post/cme-outcomes).
- Funder application and reconciliation requirements (examples, 2024–2026 funder pages): Daiichi Sankyo requires "a Letter of Request (LOR), Needs Assessment or Statement of Need, Educational Objectives, Program Agenda, Outcomes or Assessment Plan, budget for the program and any necessary tax documentation for your organization (i.e., W-9)" — [Daiichi Sankyo Independent Grants](https://daiichisankyo.us/independent-grants); Blueprint Medicines requires "an activity and budget reconciliation within 60 days after the end date of the Educational Grant activity detailing overall participation and the use of the funds" and "the Educational Grant recipient will be required to return any unused funds" and does not fund "Individual healthcare professionals" — [Blueprint Medicines IME](https://www.blueprintmedicines.com/pipeline/independent-medical-education/); argenx: "All requests for grant funding must be submitted at least 60 days in advance of the activity start date. Any change of scope must be submitted at least 30 days in advance" — [argenx grants](https://argenx.com/grants); Acadia: requests "60 days or more in advance" — [Acadia IME grants](https://acadia.com/en-us/healthcare-professionals/resources/independent-medical-education-grants); Boehringer Ingelheim requires every applicant organisation to provide a Conflict of Interest policy — [BIPI Medical Education Grants Tutorial](https://docs.boehringer-ingelheim.com/misc/BIPI-Medical-Education-Grants-Tutorial.pdf); LEO Pharma requires tax-exempt status — [LEO Pharma grant portal](https://leo-portal.idea-point.com/Grants.aspx); device-side example — [Boston Scientific IME program grants](https://www.bostonscientific.com/en-US/corporate-responsibility/giving/grants-donations-exhibits/educational-conference.html); other funder pages — [Apellis IME](https://apellis.com/about-us/corporate-giving/independent-medical-education/), [Phathom IME grants](https://www.phathompharma.com/our-science/ime-grants/).
- ACCME accountability record (drives reconciliation content): provider must keep "a record of the amount or kind of commercial support received and how it was used, and must produce that accounting, upon request" — [ACCME Standard 4](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/).

### Inferences
- A reconciliation module should capture, per grant: actual expenditures by budget category vs approved budget; variance explanation; unused-funds amount and return/repurpose status; participation counts (registered/attended/completed, by profession and specialty) = Moore Level 1; evaluation summary = Level 2; pre/post knowledge/competence data = Levels 3–4; any performance/patient data = Levels 5–6; evidence of acknowledgement of support (copies of disclosure slide/syllabus text consistent with ACCME 4.4, i.e., supporter name without logo); and copies of final agenda/faculty list with disclosures.
- Typical timelines to encode: application lead time 60 days before activity (common), scope-change notice 30 days, reconciliation due 60 days after activity end (common), with returned-funds workflow.
- Funders typically ask applicants to state at application which Moore level(s) the outcomes plan targets; a dropdown of Levels 1–7 (with 3A/3B) plus method field (attendance records, evaluation survey, pre/post test, case vignettes, chart audit, registry) is the industry convention, though not mandated by any code or accreditor.

### Gaps
- No funder-industry standard specifying which Moore level must be reported (e.g., "Level 4 minimum") was found; the ACCME does not require Moore levels. Treat "Level 3–4 expected; Level 5+ encouraged" as practice lore unless a funder's own guidelines are cited.
- Could not retrieve reconciliation templates from large funders (Pfizer, Lilly, J&J, Medtronic) showing exact data elements; the Blueprint/Daiichi/argenx examples are the concrete evidence available.

## 6. Industry norms: fair market value, grants to individual physicians, travel/lodging, meals, "unrestricted" grant language, letters of agreement, needs assessments, grants-committee separation, requestor eligibility, retention

### Takeaway
Across the PhRMA/AdvaMed codes, OIG guidance and ACCME, the consistent norms are: no grants to individual physicians; funds paid only to the organising/accredited entity; no funder payment of attendee travel, lodging or registration; meals only as modest and controlled by the provider; grants documented by a written agreement executed before the activity; applications supported by needs assessment, objectives, agenda, budget and tax documentation; decisions made by a committee outside sales/marketing on objective criteria; eligibility limited to bona fide organisations (tax-exempt or accredited providers); and the provider must retain an accounting of use of funds producible on request.

### Cited Findings
- No grants to individual HCPs: AdvaMed — "Support may not go directly to an individual Health Care Professional" — [AdvaMed Code](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf); PhRMA — support given directly to the CME provider, not individual clinicians — [UCSF copy of PhRMA Code](https://meded.ucsf.edu/sites/meded.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/PhRMACode.pdf); funder practice — Blueprint does not fund individual HCPs — [Blueprint Medicines](https://www.blueprintmedicines.com/pipeline/independent-medical-education/).
- Attendee travel/lodging/registration: ACCME — ineligible companies "must not pay directly for any of the expenses related to the education or the learners," support may cover honoraria/travel for planners and faculty "but not for individual learners" — [ACCME Standard 4](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); AOA — not permissible to use commercial funds for "travel, lodging, registration fees, honoraria, or personal expenses for non-faculty attendees" — [AOA Guide for Commercial Supporters](https://www.une.edu/sites/default/files/AOA_cme-commercial-sponsors-guide.pdf).
- Meals: AdvaMed grant funds may cover "modest meals, refreshments, and educational items" but not gift baskets, entertainment or recreation — [AdvaMed Code](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf); PhRMA 2022 speaker-program rules bar alcohol and repeat attendance — [EBG](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/revisions-to-the-phrma-code-on-interactions-with-health-care-professionals).
- Fair market value: PhRMA — speaker/consultant payments "should be fair market value, and should not take account of past or future product usage" — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2021/09/phrma-code-2022-speaker-programs-enter-a-prohibition-era); AdvaMed — commercial sponsorship fees must be "a commercially reasonable fee in exchange for the marketing and promotional benefits received" — [AdvaMed Code excerpt](https://www.cpo2.com/siteassets/us/documents/pdf/2023-advamed-code-of-ethics-202501162.pdf).
- "Unrestricted" grant language and LOAs: TTUHSC accepts a supporter's LOA "provided it is an unrestricted grant and meets ACCME requirements" — [TTUHSC 60-05](https://www.ttuhsc.edu/medicine/continuing-medical-education/documents/cme_policies/60-05-Appropriate-Use-of-Commercial-Support.pdf); ACCME requires the written agreement to be executed before the activity and between the provider and the supporter, naming any joint provider/third-party disbursing agent — [ACCME Standard 4](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); template clause: supporter and provider "will comply with ACCME standards," provider will furnish "documentation of receipts and expenditures on request" — [USC agreement form](https://app.smartsheet.com/b/form/12f1e9b26866493284d51f7f4e8d3877).
- Needs assessment and application package: LOR, needs assessment/statement of need, educational objectives, agenda, outcomes plan, budget, W-9 — [Daiichi Sankyo](https://daiichisankyo.us/independent-grants); COI policy required — [BIPI tutorial](https://docs.boehringer-ingelheim.com/misc/BIPI-Medical-Education-Grants-Tutorial.pdf); CMS-style alignment of reporting with goals/outputs/outcomes is the general grant-reporting convention — [Foundant grant reporting guide](https://www.foundant.com/blog/grant-reporting/).
- Separation of grants function from commercial: PhRMA — separate CME grant-making from sales and marketing; objective criteria — [McGuireWoods](https://mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/Alerts/2008/7/PhRMA-Code-on-Interactions-with-Healthcare-Professionals); OIG — same recommendation — [OIG testimony 2009](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/testimony/157/20090729_-_Morris.pdf); AdvaMed — sales personnel may not be involved in educational-grant decisions; may give input but not control donations; controls to prevent sales influence on research-grant recipient selection — [AdvaMed Code](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf), [AdvaMed flashcards](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/advamed-code-flashcards.pdf).
- Requestor eligibility: LEO requires tax-exempt organisations with appropriate federal status — [LEO Pharma](https://leo-portal.idea-point.com/Grants.aspx); AdvaMed donations only to "bona fide charitable organizations" — [AdvaMed Code](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf); ineligible companies cannot be accredited or act as the non-accredited joint provider — [ACCME Eligibility](https://accme.org/rule/eligibility/), [CME Outfitters FAQ](https://www.cmeoutfitters.com/joint-providership-faq/); ANCC: an ineligible company "is not eligible to award NCPD contact hours" — [ANCC NCPD FAQ](https://www.nursingworld.org/organizational-programs/accreditation/ncpd/faqs/).
- Learner data: Joint Accreditation Standard 2 prohibits sharing learner contact information with ineligible companies without explicit consent — [Joint Accreditation FAQ](https://jointaccreditation.org/faq/).
- Retention/accounting: provider must keep a record of amount/kind of support and use and produce it on request — [ACCME Standard 4](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); California requires the company's compliance program and annual declaration to be posted on its website with a toll-free number — [Cal. Health & Safety Code §119402](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_health_and_safety_code_section_119402).
- Virtual activities: PhRMA Code principles apply equally to virtual CME and third-party meetings — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-into-effect-january-2022/).

### Inferences
- Requestor eligibility rules to encode (derived from above): organisation types allowed — accredited CE providers (ACCME/JA/ANCC/ACPE/ADA CERP/CAPCE/AAFP/AOA), medical societies, hospitals/health systems, academic institutions, 501(c)(3) patient and charitable organisations; blocked — individual HCPs, individual physician practices requesting personal support, ineligible companies, medical communication companies acting without an accredited provider (route to non-accredited channel with extra scrutiny, see Question 7).
- Required attestations at submission (consolidated): organiser controls content/faculty/venue; no funder input will be sought or accepted; funds will not pay learner travel/lodging/registration; meals modest; no entertainment; support will be acknowledged by name without logos; written agreement signed before activity; unused funds returned; reconciliation within funder's deadline; learner data not shared; no linkage to purchasing/prescribing; disclosure of any other industry support for the same activity.
- Record retention: ACCME accreditation cycles (4 years standard) imply at least one full cycle of retention; combine with state law posting requirements for funder-side records. (Retention period itself is not specified by the Standards in the material retrieved.)

### Gaps

## 7. Accreditation-equivalent and professional standards for other requestor types: nursing (ANCC), pharmacy (ACPE), dental (ADA CERP), EMS (CAPCE), and non-accredited medical education

### Takeaway

## 8. 2024–2026 updates, enforcement and public controversies; state laws requiring adoption/certification of the PhRMA/AdvaMed Codes (California, Nevada, Massachusetts, Connecticut)

### Takeaway
No new edition of the PhRMA Code (2022), AdvaMed Code (June 2023) or ACCME Standards (2022) was found through Oct 2026, but there is continuing activity: ADA CERP's 2026 standards adopting the SII; ACCME's Aug 2026 in-kind disclosure compliance check; OIG's Nov 2020 Special Fraud Alert on speaker programs and ongoing FCA settlements (Novartis $642M 2020, Biogen ~$900M 2022, Gilead $202M April 2025); and state laws — California H&S §119402 (annual written declaration, posted compliance program incorporating the PhRMA Code and OIG 2003 guidance, annual gift cap), Nevada NRS 639.570 (annual 1 June filing certifying adoption of the current PhRMA/AdvaMed code plus an annual audit), Massachusetts M.G.L. c.111N / 105 CMR 970 (state code incorporating PhRMA/AdvaMed plus additional restrictions) and Connecticut P.A. 10-117 (compliance program adhering to PhRMA or AdvaMed Code at minimum).

### Inferences
- A funder-side platform should maintain a "code version adopted" attribute (PhRMA 2022 / AdvaMed 2023 / company-specific) and an annual certification calendar: California annual declaration (company-chosen date, commonly 1 July), Nevada filing by 1 June with annual audit evidence, plus Massachusetts/Connecticut compliance-program attestations; grant records should be exportable to support these audits.
- The enforcement pattern (speaker programs, meals/venue monitoring failures) implies platforms should keep meal-cap and venue data for any education-adjacent spend and keep IME grants structurally separate from promotional speaker spend to avoid commingling in audits.
