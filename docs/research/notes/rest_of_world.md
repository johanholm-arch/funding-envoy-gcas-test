## 1. Canada — IMC Code of Ethical Practices, Medtech Canada Code, voluntary disclosure, Ontario HSPTA, CFPOA/Quebec

### Takeaway
Canada has no in-force statutory sunshine law: Ontario's Health Sector Payment Transparency Act, 2017 received Royal Assent but was never proclaimed and its regulations were shelved in 2018; transparency rests on the Innovative Medicines Canada (IMC) Code (which separates grants, donations, sponsorships and collaborations/partnerships and bars funding to individuals) plus a voluntary 2017 aggregate-disclosure framework that only ~8-10 of ~48 IMC members use. Platform must encode IMC funding-category rules, Annex A patient-group transparency, agenda/venue review of third-party conferences, and aggregate annual disclosure in three categories.

### Cited Findings
- The IMC Code (revision effective 2020) "more clearly defines the categories of funding that IMC member companies can provide to stakeholders, including grants, donations, sponsorships, collaborations and partnerships" and adds "a new section (Section 13) devoted to collaborations and partnerships," with standards for each category — [Smart & Biggar](https://www.smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/updates-to-the-imc-code-of-ethical-practices)
- The same revision prohibits members "from providing payments or other benefits, directly or indirectly, to a patient's prescribing HCP for services provided in the context of PSPs and MPAs" (patient support programs / market access programs) — [Fasken](https://fasken.com/en/knowledge/2019/12/new-imc-code-of-ethical-practices)
- Per IMC's own description, member funding generally cannot go to individual stakeholders, and members "should consider the location and venue of a third-party conference and assess its medical or scientific value by reviewing its detailed agenda before deciding whether to sponsor the conference" — [IMC ethics page](https://innovativemedicines.ca/about/ethics/); [Smart & Biggar](https://www.smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/updates-to-the-imc-code-of-ethical-practices)
- The "Guidelines for Transparency in Stakeholder Funding" are annexed to the Code as Annex A and apply to patient and advocacy groups; a 2024 IMC complaint summary involving a funded patient-organization webinar cited Annex A — [IMC 2020 Code PDF](https://innovativemedicines.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/IMC-EthicalPractices-2020-web-lowres-EN.pdf); [IMC ethics page](https://innovativemedicines.ca/about/ethics/)
- Companies (AbbVie, J&J) point patient groups to "section 3.3 – Guidelines for transparency in funding of patient and advocacy groups and patient associations (including Annex A)" of the 2022 Code — [AbbVie Canada](https://www.abbvie.ca/en/community/patient-associations.html); [J&J Canada grant portal](https://www.jnj.com/innovativemedicine/canada/grants-giving/submit-grant/patient-organization)
- IMC's own sponsorship programme lists "unrestricted grants and sponsorships, typically capped at $10,000," excludes individuals, individual conference attendance and fundraising events, and requires adherence to the Code and transparency guidelines — [IMC sponsorship applications](http://innovativemedicines.ca/contact/sponsorship-applications/)
- A 2022 IJHPM study examined whether IMC member companies list on their websites the patient groups they donate to (i.e., disclosure is at company-website level, not central) — [IJHPM](https://www.ijhpm.com/article_4180_084e07fda250303f989474c15de51f6b.pdf)
- Voluntary disclosure framework: IMC set up a voluntary framework in June 2017 (retroactive to 2016) for member companies to publish aggregate annual totals in three categories: fees paid to HCPs for services, funding to healthcare organizations, and travel support for HCPs attending international congresses — [Longwoods observational study](https://www.longwoods.com/content/26729/healthcare-policy/pharmaceutical-company-payments-to-healthcare-professionals-and-healthcare-organizations-in-canada-); [U of T](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/what-big-pharma-pays-your-doctor-u-t-expert)
- Ten companies disclosed at launch (AbbVie, Amgen, BMS, Eli Lilly, Gilead, GSK, Roche, Merck, Novartis, Purdue); 10 companies paid >$75 million in 2017; ~$345 million over five years; "only eight companies out of the 48 that belong to IMC make even these minimum disclosures"; disclosures are gross figures only, not centrally collected, no penalties — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5769443/big-pharma-paying-doctors-hospitals/); [The Conversation (2024)](https://theconversation.com/canadians-need-to-know-how-much-money-big-pharma-gives-health-care-providers-but-this-information-is-far-too-difficult-to-find-225066)
- Example current disclosure: AbbVie Canada published "Voluntary Disclosure of Payments to HCPs and HCOs for January through December 2024" reporting $10,541,316 in HCP fees (excl. taxes); Roche Canada updates annually under the "Voluntary Disclosure Framework"; GSK Canada has a transparency reporting page — [AbbVie](https://www.abbvie.ca/en/who-we-are/operating-with-integrity/voluntary-disclosure-of-payments.html); [Roche Canada](https://www.rochecanada.com/about/ethics-and-integrity); [GSK Canada](https://ca.gsk.com/en-ca/responsibility/ethical-standards/transparency-reporting/)
- Ontario Health Sector Payment Transparency Act, 2017 (S.O. 2017, c. 25, Sched. 4) received Royal Assent 12 Dec 2017 but is marked "not yet in force," commencement "on a day to be named by proclamation" — [Ontario e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/17h25/v1)
- Draft HSPTA regulations consulted on in early 2018; government "will not be proceeding with the final approval of regulations before the 2018 election"; incoming Ford government reviewed all legislation not yet in force — [Pharma in Brief](https://www.pharmainbrief.com/2018/04/ontario-delays-implementation-of-regulations-under-the-health-sector-payment-transparency-act/); [CMAJ 2019](https://cmaj.ca/content/191/8/E241)
- Medtech Canada (formerly MEDEC) "revised and restated" its 2005 code of conduct on interactions with HCPs and urged all medtech companies in Canada to adopt it — [In Vivo/Informa](https://invivo.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/SC096013/Canadas-MEDEC-recommends-industrywide-adoption-of-revised-code-of-conduct)
- IBA 2024 Canada healthcare survey responses exist as a secondary source on Canadian rules — [IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/document?id=Healthcare-Survey-Responses-2024-Canada)

### Inferences
- Platform data model for Canada: funding-type enum {grant, donation, sponsorship, collaboration/partnership} with per-type standards; recipient-type block on individuals; a conference-review checklist (detailed agenda + venue assessment) stored as a prior-approval artefact; Annex A patient-group transparency fields (funding purpose, amount, acknowledgement); annual aggregate roll-up into the three voluntary-disclosure categories (HCP service fees; HCO funding; international-congress travel) for calendar-year publication on the company website.
- Because the Ontario Act is dormant and disclosure is voluntary, the platform should treat Canadian individual-level ToV disclosure as a configurable (off-by-default) feature rather than a mandatory report, while keeping data granular enough to switch on if HSPTA is ever proclaimed.

### Gaps
- Current edition/date of the IMC Code (there are references to 2020 and 2022 versions and an "updated" code; I could not confirm whether a 2024/2025/2026 edition exists or its exact section numbering). Verify at innovativemedicines.ca.
- Full text of Medtech Canada's current Code of Conduct (grant/donation/conference rules) — not retrievable; only the MEDEC lineage is sourced.
- Quebec-specific rules (Collège des médecins du Québec code, Quebec provincial requirements) — no sources returned.
- CFPOA (Corruption of Foreign Public Officials Act) — not addressed by any returned source; (unverified background) it is Canada's foreign-bribery statute, relevant to Canadian-headquartered tenants funding foreign public hospitals.
- Whether the HSPTA was formally repealed after 2019 — no post-2019 source found.

## 2. Japan — JPMA Transparency Guideline (categories A–E), scholarship donations, JPMA Code of Practice (May 2025), Clinical Trials Act COI, JFTC Fair Competition Code

### Takeaway
Japan's regime is industry self-regulation with granular public disclosure: JPMA members disclose annually by category A (R&D expenses), B (academic research support incl. scholarship donations, renamed "academic research grant expenses, etc." in the April 2026 revision), C (manuscript/lecture fees), D (information provision), E (other); the April 2026 revision adds advertising fees and supporting-member fees to disclosure scope (applying to business years beginning Jan 2027 or later, per the Japanese footnote). The JPMA Code of Practice was revised May 2025 (effective 1 Oct 2025), and the Clinical Trials Act (2018, amended effective 31 May 2025) governs COI for industry-funded "specified clinical trials."

### Cited Findings
- JPMA published the "Transparency Guideline for the Relation between Corporate Activities and Medical Institutions" in 2011; member companies have had to disclose scholarship-donation details annually on their websites since 2013 — [IJHPM 2023](https://www.ijhpm.com/MKmNZ/article_4485.html); [PMC case study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8761953/)
- Disclosure categories: "A. Research and development expenses, etc.", "B. Academic research support expenses", "C. Manuscript/writing fees, etc.", "D. Information provision-related expenses", "E. Other expenses" — [Medius (company disclosure page)](https://www.medius.co.jp/company/governance/transparency/?lang=en); [ScienceDirect 2016-data analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168851020300749); [MediSpend Japan](https://www.medispend.com/japan-transparency-reporting/)
- Category B covers payments subsidising recipients' academic activities unrelated to the company's R&D, allowing recipients to specify purpose; Category A includes clinical trial costs, research other than clinical trials, and "other R&D expenses" (meetings related to R&D) — [Medius](https://www.medius.co.jp/company/governance/transparency/?lang=en)
- JPMA notice (1 Apr 2026): the Transparency Guideline was revised; "Advertising Fees" and "Supporting Member Fees" newly added to the scope of disclosure; item B renamed from "academic research grant expenses" to "academic research grant expenses, etc."; a Japanese footnote indicates the new items apply to payments for business years beginning January 2027 or later (inference from footnote; verify) — [JPMA 2026 notice](https://en.jpma.or.jp/information/code_compliance/news/2026/260401.html); [JPMA TG page](https://en.jpma.or.jp/basis/patient_tomeisei/aboutguide/tomeiseigl.html)
- JPMA listing shows an August 20, 2026 posting of FY2025 payment disclosures under the Guideline (i.e., annual publication cadence continues) — [JPMA 2026 code compliance news index](https://en.jpma.or.jp/information/code_compliance/news/2026/index.html)
- JPMA Code of Practice: May 2025 revision approved 22 May 2025, effective 1 Oct 2025; first revision in ~six years (since Sept 2019); four parts: revised definition of "promotion," changes to the Code of Ethical Drug Promotion, removal of duplicate entries, and re-arrangement of JPMA Notice/IFPMA Guidance listings — [JPMA book notice](https://en.jpma.or.jp/information/code_compliance/news/2025/251001.html); [JPMA Code PDF (Oct 2025 English)](https://www.jpma.or.jp/basis/code/lofurc0000001dqt-att/251001E.pdf); [commentary](https://note.com/pharma_manage/n/n645d2b2a0ef2?hl=en)
- Per the commentary on the 2025 Code: funding to institutions including research grants must be tied to patient benefit/medical development and not be a sales objective; for funding academic conferences/training the purpose must advance medicine/pharmacy/public health; companies should set internal rules, cap honoraria and sponsorship amounts, and document the basis of each payment; R&D expenditures and public research subsidies fall under the Transparency Guideline; cash/cash-equivalents/gifts that could unduly influence decisions are barred — [commentary](https://note.com/pharma_manage/n/n645d2b2a0ef2?hl=en)
- Scholarship donations (奨学寄付金) are "a donation scheme unique to Japan, provided to healthcare organizations and their subunits to encourage educational and academic activities"; the Mie University anesthesiology / Ono Pharmaceutical case showed a scholarship donation "used by a pharmaceutical company to increase the prescription of one of its key drugs at a hospital department" — [PMC (Frontiers in Public Health 2021/2022)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8761953/)
- Clinical Trials Act 2018: trials of a company's drug funded by that same company are "specified clinical trials," requiring COI management, review by a certified committee, and public disclosure of funding; amendments came into force 31 May 2025 (subject-burden and off-label scope changes per the Osaka University seminar excerpt) — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8761953/); [Osaka Univ. GCRSO](https://www.gcrso.med.osaka-u.ac.jp/en/news/fy2025-seminar-report-1-2/)
- Medical device side: JFMDA Transparency Guidelines (revised Oct 2022) list scholarship donations under academic research support, disclosed by department/university with number of cases and amount; also general/academic-conference donations and co-sponsoring expenses — [JFMDA 2022 PDF](https://www.jfmda.gr.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Transparency-Guidelines-revised-2022-October.pdf)
- Japanese medical societies maintain COI policies requiring disclosure of industry funding (e.g., JSGE COI policy 2025; JDDW COI bylaws) — [JSGE 2025](https://jsge.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/policycoi2025.pdf); [JDDW](https://www.jddw.jp/english/meeting/download/jddw_coi_guidelinebylaw.pdf)
- Comparative analysis of UK vs Japan payment disclosure (self-regulation, granularity) — [PMC 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9985252/)

### Inferences
- Platform must tag every Japanese payment with the JPMA category (A/B/C/D/E) plus the two new sub-items (advertising fees; supporting-member fees) from FY2027, capture recipient at institution/department level with case counts and yen totals, and support company-website publication annually (FY2025 data published Aug 2026 pattern).
- Scholarship donations should carry heightened controls: separation from sales, documented academic purpose, recipient-specified use, and linkage to the department (not individual) given the Mie/Ono precedent and 2023 academic scrutiny.
- For "specified clinical trials" funded by the company, store COI-management records and certified-committee review evidence for public disclosure.

### Gaps
- Exact JPMA Transparency Guideline text (publication deadlines, aggregation rules, whether 2025/2026 revisions changed the level of HCP-name disclosure) — primary page blocked.
- JFTC Fair Competition Code for ethical drugs (公正競争規約) rules on donations to medical institutions — no search run (budget exhausted). (Unverified background: the Code, administered by the Fair Trade Council of the Ethical Pharmaceutical Drugs Marketing Industry, restricts premiums/donations to medical institutions that could induce purchasing; verify specifics.)
- University rules on accepting donations (e.g., national university corporation donation-acceptance rules) — not found.
- Confirmation of the "business years beginning Jan 2027" effective date for the new disclosure items.

## 3. China — 2023–2026 anti-corruption campaign, NHC/NHFPC 2015 donation measures, SAMR 2025 compliance guidelines, AUCL 2025, RDPAC Code, PIPL data localisation

### Takeaway
China layers (a) the NHFPC 2015 "Measures on Administration of Acceptance of Public Welfare Donations by Healthcare Entities (Trial)" (written agreement, institutional review, permitted purposes, bans on donations tied to procurement/commercial bribery), (b) the July 2023 nationwide healthcare anti-corruption campaign, (c) SAMR's "Compliance Guidelines for Preventing Commercial Bribery Risks in the Pharmaceutical Industry" (final 14 Jan 2025) which flag donations/sponsorships/grants as high-risk, and (d) the revised Anti-Unfair Competition Law (published 27 Jun 2025, in force 15 Oct 2025) with higher fines and individual liability. PIPL cross-border rules (CAC Provisions of 22 Mar 2024) set the thresholds under which hosting Chinese HCP/HCO personal data offshore triggers standard-contract filing or security assessment.

### Cited Findings
- NHFPC issued the "Administrative Measures on Accepting Donations for Public Welfare by Healthcare Entities (for Trial Implementation)" in 2015, replacing the 2007 Interim Measures; sources differ on effective date (effective 26 Aug 2015 and published 20 Oct 2015 per Ropes & Gray; 20 Oct 2015 per Lexology); it added "a new institutional review mechanism prior to accepting a donation" and prohibits donations involving "suspected unfair competition and commercial bribery" — [Covington 2015](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2015/10/china_adopts_new_rules_on_healthcare_donations.pdf); [Ropes & Gray 2016](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2016/09/donations-and-grants-compliance-controls-in-china-beyond-t-e); [Lexology/Liao](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e27ffadb-8703-4516-ba54-50084eab54f3)
- Scope: not-for-profit public institutions (hospitals, universities, research institutes) and "social organizations for public good" (medical associations, public-welfare foundations); donations are voluntary, non-returnable funds or property; permitted purposes: training and research, reducing medical costs, public health services, infrastructure and equipment, other non-profit activities; sponsors must sign written donation agreements — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/china/Food-Drugs-Healthcare-Life-Sciences/455896/China39s-New-Rules-On-Donations-To-Healthcare-Entities--Implications-For-Multinational-Companies); [Global Compliance News](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2015/11/09/chinas-new-donation-rules-provide-guidance-to-healthcare-enterprises/); [Law.asia](https://law.asia/public-welfare-sponsorship-or-commercial-bribery/)
- Baker McKenzie (2016) describes charitable donations to hospitals as governed by the "Provisional Measures for the Administration of the Acceptance by Medical and Health Institutions of Private Donations" — [Baker McKenzie China handbook](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/-/media/files/insight/publications/2016/01/promoting-medical-devices-globally/pmpg_china.pdf?la=en)
- 2013 Circular 49 "nine prohibitions" apply to all hospitals and employees, including a bar on individuals in medical institutions accepting donations from companies; July 2023 nationwide inter-agency healthcare anti-corruption campaign targets hospital/pharma executives and insurance funds — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/chinas-new-anti-corruption-policies-in-the-health-care-industry)
- SAMR published final compliance guidelines on 14 Jan 2025, effective on publication; not legally binding but reflecting enforcement priorities under the AUCL; sponsorships, donations and grants to HCOs are a high-risk area — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/01/chinese-regulators-publish-final-anti-corruption); [Covington Jan 2025](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2025/01/chinas-samr-issues-guidance-to-healthcare-companies-on-preventing-commercial-bribery-risks.pdf); [MoFo](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/241028-china-s-compliance-guidelines-for-healthcare)
- Draft guidance (Oct 2024): companies may not use donations/sponsorships to gain competitive advantage, e.g., tying equipment donations to future consumables purchases or influencing procurement — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/10/china-state-administration-for-market-regulation-releases-draft-compliance-guide-for-healthcare-companies); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102jvc6/china-tightens-the-reins-anti-corruption-compliance-expectations-for-companies-i)
- China also released a compliance case handbook for life-sciences companies — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/global-anti-corruption-perspective/china-releases-first-of-its-kind-compliance-case-handbook-for-life-sciences-companies)
- Revised Anti-Unfair Competition Law published 27 Jun 2025, in force 15 Oct 2025; draft proposed penalising bribe recipients, higher corporate fines, and fines on legal representatives/individuals (final-text retention not confirmed) — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/crt/2025-07/china-releases-the-2025-anti-unfair-competition-law); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102jvc6/china-tightens-the-reins-anti-corruption-compliance-expectations-for-companies-i)
- RDPAC Code: Covington (Jan 2025) cites the "2022 RDPAC Code" as current, requiring member caps on HCP service fees; Covington (2021) notes "the RDPAC Code of Practice does not provide detailed guidelines on donations" and that neither RDPAC nor IFPMA codes prohibit companies from retaining influence over which HCPs benefit from grants to HCOs — [Covington 2025](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2025/01/chinas-samr-issues-guidance-to-healthcare-companies-on-preventing-commercial-bribery-risks.pdf); [Covington 2021](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2021/04/chinese-pharmaceutical-biotech-and-medical-device-industry-associations-issue-first-comprehensive-compliance-guidelines.pdf)
- 2025 enforcement context — [Control Risks](https://www.controlrisks.com/our-thinking/insights/china-s-healthcare-sector-in-2025-anti-corruption-and-beyond)
- PIPL cross-border: CAC "Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows" effective 22 Mar 2024; security assessment required where, from 1 Jan of the current year, cumulative exports exceed PI of 1 million individuals or sensitive PI of 10,000 individuals; standard contract applies to non-CIIO exporters below those thresholds; exemptions for contractual necessity etc. — [Gide](https://www.gide.com/sites/default/files/gide_china_client_alert_china_eases_cross-border_data_transfer_controls_apr2024.pdf); [Han Kun](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/china-finalizes-its-provisions-to-promote-and-regulate-cross-border-data-transfers); [IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/China-cross-border-transfer-data-legislation); [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/cross-border-data-transfer-new-provisions-clarify-contract-procedure-for-personal-information-export/)

### Inferences
- Platform controls for China: recipient must be an eligible public-welfare entity (public hospital, university/research institute, medical association, or registered public-welfare foundation); mandatory written donation agreement; capture evidence of the recipient's internal institutional review/acceptance; purpose code limited to the 2015 Measures' permitted purposes; hard block on any link to procurement, tenders, consumables or prescribing; no donations to individuals; retention of SAMR-guideline-aligned risk assessment and approvals.
- Data residency: a cloud platform holding Chinese HCP/HCO personal data offshore should either keep a China-resident instance or maintain standard-contract filings/security-assessment evidence and track cumulative annual export counts against the 1M / 10k thresholds.

### Gaps
- Whether the 2015 Measures have been amended (2023–2026) and the exact review, publicity and procurement-firewall articles (needs Chinese primary text).
- Charity Law (2016, amended 2023/2024) requirements on routing donations via registered charitable organisations and foundations — search not run (budget exhausted); (unverified background) foundations such as the China Primary Health Care Foundation are commonly used intermediaries.
- RDPAC Code current edition (2022 vs any 2025 update) — conflicting secondary sources; no primary text.
- Specific FCPA/China enforcement examples involving donations — search not run.

## 4. Korea — Expenditure (K-Sunshine) reports, KPBMA/KRPIA Fair Competition Code, academic-conference support rules

### Takeaway
Since 2018 (Pharmaceutical Affairs Act / Medical Device Act), companies must prepare annual "expenditure reports" on economic benefits to HCPs/HCOs (samples, academic-conference sponsorship, PMS honoraria, clinical trial funding, etc.), retain them five years, and — since the 2021 amendment and FY2023 implementation guidelines — submit them to MOHW (via HIRA) for de-identified public disclosure. Conference support runs through the Fair Trade Commission-approved Fair Competition Code (KPBMA 5th revision approved Jan 2026; tightened rules effective July 2026 with 3-hour/3-credit/50-participant minimums and booth-fee caps).

### Cited Findings
- Companies must prepare an expense report on economic interests within three months after the end of each fiscal year and keep it with supporting records for five years; MOHW may request submission when necessary — [Policy & Medicine 2017](https://policymed.com/2017/08/sunshine-act-takes-effect-in-south-korea.html); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.co.uk/healthcare/574222/implementation-details-of-koreas-sunshine-act-under-discussion)
- Reportable categories include academic conference sponsorships, samples, PMS honoraria and clinical trial funding — [Policy & Medicine](https://policymed.com/2017/08/sunshine-act-takes-effect-in-south-korea.html); [Korea Biomed](https://www.koreabiomed.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=1316)
- 2021 amendments (20 Jul 2021) to the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act and Medical Device Act require expenditure reports and extended obligations to contract sales organisations (CSOs), which now face rebate liability — [Shin & Kim via Law.asia](https://law.asia/?p=559417)
- March 2024 MOHW guidelines set the schedule, content and method for public disclosure of expenditure reports from fiscal year 2023; recipient names and confidential clinical-trial details are de-identified under Article 44(2)(3) of the Enforcement Rule; data flows through HIRA with publication planned for December — [Dailypharm 2024](https://www.dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/5928); [Dailypharm 2024 (disclosure debate)](https://dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/6170)
- MOHW surveys the reports, publishes survey results and submitted reports, and may request supporting data — [Daeryun Law](https://foreign.daeryunlaw.com/en/broadcast/4893)
- Enforcement example: Abbott sponsored the Korean Society of Interventional Cardiology for 12 overseas conferences; lawmaker criticised MOHW for sampling only four companies' 2018 reports — [Dailypharm 2020](https://dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/2055)
- KPBMA received Fair Trade Commission approval for the 5th revision of its Fair Competition Code for Pharmaceutical Transactions and Detailed Operational Standards (announced Jan 2026); a conference sponsor cannot also provide overlapping donations, F&B, booth rentals or advertising for the same conference; "international" conferences must be screened by medical/pharma associations, be held in Korea for ≥2 days with on-site attendance from ≥5 countries and ≥50 foreign HCPs; KPBMA can demand explanation/correction and suspend support — [Dailypharm](https://www.dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/6633); [Dailypharm](https://www.dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/7555); [Dailypharm](https://dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/7569); [Dailypharm](https://dailypharm.com/eng/user/news/6807)
- Revised code effective July 2026: sponsorship/participation limited to conferences with an educational programme of ≥3 hours awarding ≥3 continuing-education credits; ≥50 participants (≥25 for rare-disease fields); booth fee cap KRW 3 million (society/research-institution hosts) and KRW 1 million (healthcare-facility hosts) — [eDaily July 2026](https://en.edaily.co.kr/news/eda202607205734)
- Korean-language academic overview of the K-Sunshine Act expenditure report — [JKD](https://e-jkd.org/journal/view.php?number=76)

### Inferences
- Platform must generate the Korean expenditure report by category with HCP/HCO identifiers, retain supporting documents ≥5 years, produce a de-identified submission file for MOHW/HIRA, and apply conference eligibility checks (hours, credits, participant counts, international criteria, booth caps, no-overlap rule between sponsorship and donation/booth/ad for the same event).

### Gaps
- KRPIA's own current code and whether multinational members follow KRPIA or KPBMA screening — search not run.
- The KMA/MFDS mechanism for approving conferences eligible for support (the question's "KMA-approved conferences") — only the association-screening criteria for "international" status were sourced.
- 2025 public-disclosure outcomes (first publication) — no source.

## 5. Australia and New Zealand — Medicines Australia Code Edition 20, MTAA Code Edition 14, Medicines NZ/NZMA transparency guidelines

### Takeaway
Medicines Australia Code Edition 20 (adopted 30 Oct 2024, effective 30 Mar 2025) extended transparency reporting to grants and donations to HCOs and third-party meeting sponsorships (Section 14.2), with transition reporting windows (1 May–31 Dec 2025 due 30 Apr 2026, published 10 Jun 2026; 1 Jan–30 Jun 2026 due 31 Oct 2026, published 10 Dec 2026). MTAA's medtech Code is at Edition 14 (2026). New Zealand uses joint Medicines NZ/NZMA transparency guidelines (effective 1 Jan 2021; annual company-website publication by 30 June, kept ≥3 years) that cover transfers of value to HCOs.

### Cited Findings

## 6. India — UCPMP 2024, NMC 2023 regulations (in abeyance), CBDT disallowance, FCRA

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- A global platform should implement the IFPMA 2019 baseline as the default rule-set (no gifts; no entertainment; grants/donations only to organisations; written agreements; transparency), with national-code overlays, and should include patient-organisation fields derived from the IFPMA/IAPO Note for Guidance (independence safeguards, non-exclusive funding, public acknowledgement).

## Consolidated platform requirement list (synthesis of the sourced findings above)

- **Funding-type taxonomy:** grant / donation / sponsorship / collaboration-partnership (IMC); charitable donation / educational grant / research grant (Mecomed, APACMed); JPMA categories A–E plus advertising fees and supporting-member fees (FY2027+); MA "grants and donations" vs "third-party meeting sponsorship"; Korea expenditure-report categories.
- **Recipient eligibility rules:** organisations only, never individuals (IMC, HKAPI, AFIDRO, Mecomed, APACMed, IPASA/MCA, CETIFARMA, UCPMP monetary-grant ban); China: eligible public-welfare entities with institutional review and written agreement; India: FCRA registration/prior permission + SBI NDMB account for foreign-source funds; Korea: conference eligibility (≥3h/≥3 credits/≥50 participants; international criteria; booth caps).
- **Prior approvals/declarations:** conference agenda/venue review (IMC, APACMed, IFPMA Note on events); Mecomed CVS reference; Korea association screening for "international" conferences; Brazil physician CRM-Virtual declaration within 60 days; Japan specified-clinical-trial COI review.
- **Required documents:** written agreement (China 2015 Measures, Mecomed, AFIDRO, APACMed); recipient legal-entity proof; purpose statement within permitted purposes (China); FCRA certificate; de-identified Korean submission file; acknowledgement-of-support evidence (HKAPI, CETIFARMA).
- **Disclosure outputs and deadlines:** Canada voluntary aggregate (3 categories, annual); Japan annual company-website disclosure by category (FY2025 data published Aug 2026); Korea expenditure report within 3 months of FY end, 5-year retention, MOHW/HIRA public disclosure (FY2023 onward); Australia MA Section 14.2 (periods 1 May–31 Dec 2025 due 30 Apr 2026 / pub. 10 Jun 2026; 1 Jan–30 Jun 2026 due 31 Oct 2026 / pub. 10 Dec 2026); New Zealand annual by 30 June, 3-year retention; Brazil Minas Gerais company disclosure + CFM physician platform; Mecomed educational-grant disclosure; Interfarma public list of supported organisations with nature/value.
- **Anti-bribery red flags to encode:** any link to procurement/consumables/tenders (China SAMR), donations tied to rep access (South Africa), overlapping sponsorship + donation + booth for the same event (Korea), administrative-expense funding limits for patient groups (Interfarma), exclusive sponsorship of a patient organisation (CETIFARMA).
