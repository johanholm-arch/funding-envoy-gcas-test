## Key Question 1: Federal Anti-Kickback Statute, safe harbors, OIG compliance guidance (2003 CPG, Nov 2023 GCPG, ICPGs) and Special Fraud Alerts (speaker programs 2020; charitable patient assistance 2005/2014; 2016–2020 settlements)

### Takeaway
The 2003 OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Pharmaceutical Manufacturers (May 5, 2003) is still the operative industry-specific guidance for grants in 2026 — no pharma or device Industry-Specific CPG has been found despite OIG's 2023 announcement that ICPGs would begin in 2024 — and it is supplemented by the General Compliance Program Guidance of Nov 6, 2023. The 2020 Speaker Program Special Fraud Alert, the 2005/2014 patient-assistance bulletins, the 2017–2020 copay-charity settlements (~$1 billion+ combined), and favourable OIG advisory opinions 24-02 and 26-17 define the concrete control features (separation from sales, objective criteria, no product/volume conditions, no conduit to patients, uniform need-based rules) a grants platform must enforce.

### Cited Findings

**2003 CPG for Pharmaceutical Manufacturers**
- OIG issued the Compliance Program Guidance for Pharmaceutical Manufacturers on May 5, 2003 (68 FR 23731). It identifies educational grants as a risk area: manufacturer grants to purchasers, GPOs, PBMs and similar entities raise concerns under the anti-kickback statute, and funding conditioned, in whole or in part, on the purchase of product implicates the statute even if the educational or research purpose is legitimate — [OIG CPG PDF (68 FR 23731)](https://www.oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/799/050503FRCPGPharmac.pdf); [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/05/05/03-10949/oig-compliance-program-guidance-for-pharmaceutical-manufacturers); [GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2003-05-05/pdf/03-10949.pdf)
- The 2003 CPG advises manufacturers to keep grant-making separate from sales and marketing, to use objective grant criteria that ignore the volume or value of a recipient's purchases, and to structure fee-for-service research contracts with product purchasers to fit the personal services safe harbor where possible — [OIG CPG PDF](https://www.oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/799/050503FRCPGPharmac.pdf); [HHS guidance portal entry](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/oig-compliance-program-guidance-pharmaceutical-manufacturers)
- A 2007 Senate Finance Committee print (S. Prt. 110-21) reported that companies had taken steps to separate the grant-making process for educational programs from their marketing efforts, and noted that OIG compliance program guidance is not mandatory — [Senate Finance Committee print](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/Prt110-21.pdf)

**Nov 2023 General Compliance Program Guidance (GCPG) and Industry-Specific CPGs**
- OIG issued the GCPG on Nov 6, 2023 as a reference guide for the whole healthcare compliance community; it does not replace the 2003 pharma guidance and creates no new legal obligations — [OIG GCPG PDF](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/1135/HHS-OIG-GCPG-2023.pdf); [Holland & Knight, Nov 2023](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2023/11/oig-releases-new-compliance-program-guidance-for-all-healthcare)
- OIG stated that starting in 2024 it would publish industry segment-specific CPGs ("ICPGs"); a 2024 law-firm guide states there is no published date for a pharmaceutical ICPG to replace the 2003 guidance — [Akin Gump 2024 Guide to DOJ and HHS-OIG Guidance](https://www.akingump.com/a/web/5vpTEWSojTA926xwieF4be/8TuVLc/akin-2024-guide-to-doj-and-hhs-oig.pdf); [Legal500 overview for life sciences](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/corporate-commercial-law/oig's-new-general-compliance-program-guidance-gcpg-overview-and-takeaways-for-life-sciences-companies)
- The GCPG raises OIG's expectations of compliance officers (independence, board access, resources) at pharmaceutical and medical device companies — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/compliance-program-guidance-what-compliance-officers-at-pharmaceutical-and-medical-device-companies-need-to-consider)
- No search result from 2024–2026 showed a pharmaceutical or device ICPG; the OIG compliance guidance index page is the authoritative place to confirm — [OIG Compliance Guidance page](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/compliance-guidance/)

**Special Fraud Alert: Speaker Programs (Nov 2020)**
- OIG's Special Fraud Alert on speaker programs (issued November 2020) treats speaker-program remuneration as a potential kickback and flags: minimal educational content, lavish venues or alcohol, repeat programs without new clinical information, and compensation tied to prescription volume — [OIG Special Fraud Alert PDF](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/special-fraud-alerts/865/SpecialFraudAlertSpeakerPrograms.pdf); [Steptoe analysis](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/hhs-oigs-recent-special-fraud-alert-and-the-future-of-speaker-programs.html); [Gardner Law](https://gardner.law/news/speaker-programs-under-scrutiny)
- 2025 settlements with Gilead and Pfizer involving speaker programs show that physicians, as well as sponsors, face AKS exposure — [Health Law Alliance](https://www.healthlawalliance.com/blog/speaker-programs-and-kickback-enforcement)

**Patient Assistance Program bulletins (2005, 2014) and advisory opinions (2024, 2026)**
- OIG's Special Advisory Bulletin on Patient Assistance Programs for Medicare Part D Enrollees was published Nov 22, 2005 (70 FR 70623); the Supplemental Special Advisory Bulletin: Independent Charity Patient Assistance Programs was announced May 21, 2014 and published May 30, 2014 (79 FR 31120) — [Federal Register 2014 bulletin](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2014/05/30/2014-11769/supplemental-special-advisory-bulletin-independent-charity-patient-assistance-programs); [OIG bulletin PDF (79 FR 31120)](https://www.oig.hhs.gov/documents/special-advisory-bulletins/879/independent-charity-bulletin.pdf); [Cooley, 2014](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2014/hhs-oig-issues-supplemental-special-advisory-bulletin-on-patient-assistance-programs)
- The 2014 bulletin focuses on (1) disease funds, (2) eligible recipients, and (3) the conduct of donors; an independent charity PAP must not function as a conduit for payments from the manufacturer to patients and must not impermissibly influence beneficiaries' drug choices; the principles apply regardless of which Federal health care program covers the drugs — [Federal Register 2014 bulletin](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2014/05/30/2014-11769/supplemental-special-advisory-bulletin-independent-charity-patient-assistance-programs); [Reed Smith, June 2014](https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2014/06/oig-issues-advisory-bulletin-impacting-independent)
- The 2014 bulletin supplements rather than replaces the 2005 SAB; alongside publication OIG sent letters to prior favourable-advisory-opinion recipients asking charities to certify compliance with the added factors — [K&L Gates, Mar 2018](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/Increased-Scrutiny-of-Patient-Assistance-Programs-Enforcement-Overview-and-Considerations-03-20-2018); [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/health-care-counsel-blog/oig-releases-supplemental-guidance-bona-fide-charitable)
- OIG Advisory Opinion 24-02 addressed PAPs independently managed by a 501(c)(3) but funded primarily by pharmaceutical manufacturers; it is time-limited and in effect only until Jan 1, 2027 — [Morgan Lewis, Feb 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/asprescribed/2025/02/patient-assistance-programs-the-dos-and-donts-from-hhs-oig); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/hhs-oig-highlights-anti-fraud-safeguards-patient-assistance-programs-backed-mainly)
- OIG Advisory Opinion 26-17 (2026) is a favourable opinion for an independent charity PAP covering premium and copayment assistance for federal program beneficiaries; OIG found low risk because assistance is awarded first-come, first-served under a reasonable, verifiable, uniformly applied financial-need policy, and reserved the right to revisit the opinion in two years — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/hhs-oig-issues-favorable-advisory-opinion-regarding-independent-charity-patient); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=516450d1-dd80-455d-904a-e58a551ddafe)
- Ropes & Gray published a July 2025 analysis of PAP enforcement trends and regulatory challenges — [Ropes & Gray podcast, July 2025](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/podcasts/2025/07/patient-assistance-programs-enforcement-trends-and-regulatory-challenges)

**Copay-charity (AKS) settlements, 2017–2020**
- United Therapeutics: $210 million (Dec 2017) over donations to Caring Voice Coalition used to cover Medicare copays for its PAH drugs — [FiercePharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/legal/united-therapeutics-inks-210m-settlement-over-charity-donations)

- OIG's index of Special Fraud Alerts and Special Advisory Bulletins — [OIG alerts page](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/alerts/#special-advisory-bulletins)

### Inferences
- Platform workflow rules directly derivable from the 2003 CPG + 2014 bulletin + settlements: (a) grant/donation review must be owned by a function outside Sales/Marketing with system-enforced role separation (sales users cannot create, score, or approve requests; at most "awareness" visibility); (b) scoring criteria must be objective and stored, and must exclude any field capturing the applicant's purchase volume/value or prescribing; (c) require an attestation that funding is not conditioned on purchase, formulary status, or referrals; (d) for charity PAP donations: capture the disease fund name, confirm the charity's independence, block earmarking to a specific product, and bar the funder from receiving patient-level or product-specific data from the charity (the "conduit" and "donor conduct" factors).
- The time-limited nature of AO 24-02 (expires Jan 1, 2027) and the two-year revisit clause in AO 26-17 mean a platform should store the advisory opinion relied upon for each PAP donation and surface expiry dates.
- **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** The AKS is codified at 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b); regulatory safe harbors are at 42 C.F.R. § 1001.952. There is no safe harbor for educational grants, charitable donations or sponsorships as such; the personal services and management contracts safe harbor (§ 1001.952(d)) is the one the 2003 CPG points to for research/consulting arrangements. Civil monetary penalties and False Claims Act liability (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(g), as amended by ACA 2010) attach to AKS-tainted claims. Confirm against eCFR.
- **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** OIG has historically issued separate guidance on educational grants in its 2003 CPG section "Relationships with Physicians and Other Persons" (speaker/consultant payments, research funding, educational grants) and its Nov 2024 ICPG was for nursing facilities (not pharma). Confirm on the OIG compliance guidance page.

## Key Question 2: Physician Payments Sunshine Act / Open Payments — what grant and donation payments are reportable, indirect-payment and CME rules, data elements, thresholds, timelines, recipient identity capture

### Takeaway
Open Payments (42 C.F.R. Part 403 Subpart I) requires applicable manufacturers/GPOs to report payments or transfers of value to physicians, teaching hospitals and (since Jan 1, 2021) non-physician practitioners, with 2026 thresholds of $13.82 per transfer / $138.13 annual aggregate; grants and charitable contributions to covered recipients are reportable, while funding to an accredited CME provider is not an "indirect payment" unless the manufacturer requires, instructs, directs or otherwise causes the provider to pay a particular covered recipient. Reports are due in the March window and published by June 30; records must carry NPPES name, NPI and state licence (or the CMS teaching hospital list identity) and research payments flow through the delayed-publication rules of § 403.910.

### Cited Findings

**Covered recipients and thresholds**
- Covered recipients are physicians, non-physician practitioners (NPPs: physician assistants, nurse practitioners, clinical nurse specialists, certified registered nurse anesthetists and anesthesiologist assistants, certified nurse midwives) and teaching hospitals — [CMS Open Payments Program Participants](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/program-participants)
- The SUPPORT Act expanded covered recipients effective Jan 1, 2021 to include the NPP categories; Program Year 2021 was the inaugural year NPPs appeared in the data — [CMS FY2021 Report to Congress](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/open-payments-fy-2021-annual-report-congress.pdf); [CMS glossary](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/glossary-acronyms)
- For the Jan 1 – Dec 31, 2026 collection period CMS lists a small-payment threshold of $13.82 and an annual aggregate threshold of $138.13; thresholds are adjusted annually by CPI; payments below the per-transfer minimum are excluded unless the annual aggregate to a covered recipient is exceeded, in which case all payments must be reported — [CMS Data Collection for Reporting Entities](https://www.cms.gov/openpayments/program-participants/reporting-entities/data-collection); a third-party site lists $13.07/$130.66 for 2026, which conflicts with CMS and should be treated as wrong — [Qordata](https://www.qordata.com/financial-thresholds-in-cms-open-payments-data/)

**Indirect payments and CME**
- CMS defines an indirect payment as one where the applicable manufacturer or GPO "requires, instructs, directs or otherwise causes" a third party to provide the payment or transfer of value, in whole or in part, to a covered recipient — [CMS Open Payments General FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/OpenPayments/Downloads/open-payments-general-faq.pdf)
- In the CY2015 PFS final rule (Nov 2014) CMS eliminated the separate reporting exclusion for payments to physicians for speaking at accredited/certified CME programs; CMS reasoned that funding a third-party CME provider is not a reportable indirect payment if the manufacturer does not direct the provider to pay a specific physician, and later learning the physician's identity does not make it reportable — [CMS Nov 2014 revision document](https://www.cms.gov/openpayments/downloads/open-payments-revision-nov-2014.pdf); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/continuing-medical-education-cme-payments-largely-remain-excluded-sunshine-act-repor); [Powers Law](https://www.powerslaw.com/cms-removes-continuing-medical-education-exemption-from-physician-payment-sunshine-act-rules/)
- CMS stated that tuition fees for physician attendees generally subsidized by manufacturers at continuing education events are not expected to be reported, and that it would issue sub-regulatory guidance — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/continuing-medical-education-cme-payments-largely-remain-excluded-sunshine-act-repor)
- Industry groups lobbied to keep the CME exemption during that rulemaking — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/industry-groups-urge-medicare-to-keep-cme-exemption-for-open-payments-program)

**Data elements, research payments, delayed publication**
- 42 C.F.R. § 403.904(c) general reporting requirements cover recipient name, address, specialty, identifiers and the payment amount; for physician/NPP recipients the report must include the name as listed in NPPES, the NPI, and at least one state professional licence number with state; where no NPI exists the field may be left blank — [42 CFR 403.904 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/403.904); [eCFR 42 CFR Part 403 Subpart I](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-A/part-403/subpart-I)
- § 403.904(f) sets special rules for research payments made under a written agreement, a research protocol, or both — [eCFR Part 403 Subpart I](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-A/part-403/subpart-I)
- Manufacturers must flag whether a payment is eligible for delayed publication under § 403.910; such payments are still reported on the first reporting date after the year they occur but are not posted publicly at that time; flagged records remain eligible for covered-recipient review and dispute — [eCFR Part 403 Subpart I](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-A/part-403/subpart-I); [CMS Data Dictionary Methodology](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/methodology-data-dictionary-document.pdf)
- CMS defines teaching hospitals as hospitals that received Medicare direct GME, IPPS IME, or psychiatric hospital IME payments in the last calendar year with available data (CMS publishes the list annually) — [CMS Data Dictionary Methodology](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/methodology-data-dictionary-document.pdf)
- A research payment to a university or institution is reportable if the entity has at least one physician principal investigator; payments to a non-teaching military medical centre are reportable when they ultimately reach a covered recipient — [CMS Open Payments General FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/OpenPayments/Downloads/open-payments-general-faq.pdf)
- The current Open Payments Data Dictionary is approved under OMB Control No. 0938-1237 with expiration 07/31/2026 (implying a Paperwork Reduction Act renewal in 2026) — [CMS Open Payments Data Dictionary](https://www.cms.gov/openpayments/downloads/openpaymentsdatadictionary.pdf)

**Timelines and publication**
- CMS publishes the full dataset for the previous program year annually on or before June 30 and refreshes data each January; records submitted after the submission closing date are generally excluded from initial publication and the next refresh — [CMS Open Payments Data overview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/data); [CMS Data Dictionary Methodology](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/methodology-data-dictionary-document.pdf)
- CMS issued a covered-recipient registration and review-and-dispute tutorial dated January 2026 — [CMS tutorial, Jan 2026](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/tutorial-op-covered-recipient-review-dispute-january-2026.pdf)
- CMS's data overview refers to a Program Year 2025 update with general payments ≈ $3.92 billion, research payments ≈ $9.50 billion and ownership/investment ≈ $1.25 billion (page labelling ambiguous — verify at openpaymentsdata.cms.gov) — [CMS Open Payments Data overview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/data); [Open Payments summary](https://openpaymentsdata.cms.gov/summary)
- CMS's FY2025 Annual Report to Congress on Open Payments is published — [CMS FY2025 Report to Congress](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/open-payments-fy-2025-report-congress.pdf)
- CMS's "Open Payments Overview and Enhancements" deck summarises recent program changes — [CMS overview & enhancements](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/open-payments-overview-enhancements.pdf)
- The CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule final rule was released Oct 31, 2025 and published in the Federal Register Nov 5, 2025; search results on that rule did not identify any Open Payments provisions — [Federal Register, CY2026 PFS final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/05/2025-19787/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-cy-2026-payment-policies-under-the-physician-fee-schedule-and-other); [Holland & Knight summary](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/cms-releases-cy-2026-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule)

### Inferences
- Grants-platform data model for Open Payments: every payee record needs (i) recipient type (physician / NPP / teaching hospital / non-covered entity); (ii) for individuals: NPPES legal name, NPI, primary specialty, at least one state licence + state, practice address; (iii) for teaching hospitals: match to the CMS teaching hospital list for the program year, with the list's hospital name/TIN/address captured as published; (iv) program year, payment date, amount, form of payment, nature of payment, product association (drug/device/biological, NDC where applicable), third-party-recipient and "payment made to a third party at the request of / designated on behalf of" flags; (v) delayed-publication eligibility flag and research fields (study name, PI(s), ClinicalTrials.gov ID, context).
- The indirect-payment test implies the platform must capture, for every educational grant to a CME provider, (a) whether the funder designated, instructed or required any speaker/attendee, and (b) a dated attestation of non-direction; if the funder later learns identities (e.g., in a reconciliation report) the rule says that does not retroactively create an indirect payment, but the platform should record when and how identities were learned to evidence this.
- Any grant or charitable contribution paid *to* a teaching hospital (or to a physician-owned entity on behalf of a physician) is directly reportable; the platform should therefore screen all applicant organisations against the teaching hospital list at intake and again at payment.
- **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** Nature-of-payment categories at § 403.904(e) include "Grant", "Charitable contribution", "Education", "Research", "Compensation for services other than consulting", "Consulting fee", "Honoraria", "Gift", "Entertainment", "Food and beverage", "Travel and lodging", "Royalty or license", "Current or prospective ownership or investment interest", "Space rental or facility fees", and — added by the CY2021 PFS final rule effective for PY2021 — "Debt forgiveness", "Long-term medical supply or device loan" and "Acquisitions"; the two former CME-speaker categories (accredited vs unaccredited) were consolidated into a single "Medical education programs"-type category in the same rule. Confirm against the CMS data dictionary and the CY2021 PFS final rule.
- **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** Submission/attestation window runs Feb 1 – Mar 31 following the program year; covered-recipient review and dispute runs 45 days from publication-preview opening in April, with a 15-day correction period; statutory CMPs are up to $10,000 per unreported payment (cap $150,000/yr) and up to $100,000 per knowing failure (cap $1,000,000/yr), inflation-adjusted. Confirm against § 403.908 and § 403.912.

## Key Question 3: State laws — Vermont, Massachusetts, Minnesota, California, Connecticut, Nevada, New Jersey and others; gift bans, disclosure, marketing-code and compliance-program requirements; reporting formats

### Takeaway
Seven states impose obligations a grants platform must encode: Vermont (gift ban + April 1 annual disclosure of allowable expenditures including grants and donations, with clinical-trial deferral; AG RY2025 guide issued Oct 6, 2025), Massachusetts (code of conduct + July 1 disclosure of ≥$50 per-occurrence transfers to covered recipients that include hospitals), Minnesota (statutory gift ban with carve-outs for conference-sponsor payments used solely for bona fide education), California (public Comprehensive Compliance Program with a self-set annual per-HCP dollar cap and annual declaration), Connecticut (code of conduct + OIG-aligned compliance program + annual July 1 certification; new 2023 registration/reporting regime), Nevada (annual May 1–June 1 marketing-code filing to the Board of Pharmacy with audit and certification), and New Jersey (prescriber $10,000 annual cap on promotional/consulting compensation with education, research and royalty carve-outs).

### Cited Findings

**Vermont (18 V.S.A. §§ 4631a, 4632)**
- 18 V.S.A. § 4632 requires manufacturers to file annual disclosures with the Attorney General by April 1 covering the prior calendar year, stating the value, nature, purpose and recipient information of allowable expenditures or gifts to health care providers or Green Mountain Care Board members — [18 V.S.A. § 4632 (Justia 2024)](https://law.justia.com/codes/vermont/title-18/chapter-91/section-4632/); [Vermont Legislature § 4632](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/091/04632); [Chapter 91](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/chapter/18/091)
- Excluded from § 4632 disclosure: royalties and licensing fees; rebates and discounts given in the normal course of business; clinical trial payments, which are disclosed later — after FDA approval or four calendar years after payment — [18 V.S.A. § 4632 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/vermont/title-18/chapter-91/section-4632/)
- A separate subsection requires disclosure of samples given to providers (product, recipient, number of units, dosage) by April 1 each year; this was added by amendment — [18 V.S.A. § 4632 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/vermont/title-18/chapter-91/section-4632/); [Lexology on samples amendment](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3e10079a-ce94-419d-b53e-930a8420be83)
- The Vermont AG, which enforces the law, published an RY2025 gift-ban guide on Oct 6, 2025 — [Vermont AG disclosures page](https://ago.vermont.gov/disclosures-manufacturers-prescription-drugs-biological-products-medical-devices)
- A 2025 journal table lists Vermont's disclosure threshold as payments of $25 or more and fines up to $10,000 plus attorneys' fees — [Frontiers in Health Services, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/health-services/articles/10.3389/frhs.2025.1666949/full)
- Historical AG reports (RY2015, RY2018) show the reported categories (value, nature, purpose, recipient) and aggregate statistics — [RY2015 report](https://ago.vermont.gov/sites/ago/files/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/2015-Prescribed-Product-Disclosures-Report.pdf); [RY2018 report](https://ago.vermont.gov/sites/ago/files/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2018-Prescribed-Products-Disclosures-Report.pdf)
- The original 2009 Act 59 created the modern gift ban/disclosure scheme — [Act 59 (2009)](http://www.leg.state.vt.us/docs/2010/Acts/ACT059.pdf); a 2021 House Ways & Means working document proposed changing the manufacturer fee date (enactment not confirmed) — [Vermont Legislature document](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2022/WorkGroups/House%20Ways%20and%20Means/Bills/H.436%20-%20Misc.%20Tax%20Bill/W~Jennifer%20Carbee~Language%20-%20Pharmacy%20Manufacturer%20Fee%20Date%20Change~3-11-2021.pdf)

## Key Question 4: FCPA as applied to grants, donations and sponsorships abroad; DOJ/SEC enforcement examples; expected due diligence; 2025–2026 enforcement posture

### Takeaway
DOJ/SEC have repeatedly charged pharma/medtech companies where grants, donations, honoraria or sponsorships to foreign public hospitals, universities, or foundations tied to government health officials functioned as bribes or were mis-recorded (Eli Lilly 2012, Stryker 2013, Teva 2016, Fresenius 2019, Novartis 2020, Alexion 2020). After a 2025 enforcement pause, DOJ's June 2025 FCPA Guidelines and a March 2026 department-wide Corporate Enforcement Policy (three-tier declination/NPA/DPA structure) restarted enforcement — the first declination under the new policy (Balt SAS) involved payments to a physician at a state-owned French hospital — so platforms must treat foreign public-hospital/university grants as high-risk third-party payments with documented due diligence.

### Cited Findings

**Enforcement examples involving grants/donations/sponsorships**
- Eli Lilly (SEC complaint filed Dec 2012, No. 12-cv-02045, D.D.C.; $29 million settlement): Lilly's Polish subsidiary paid $39,000 between 2000 and 2003 to a foundation started by the head of a regional government health authority, allegedly in exchange for pharmaceutical business from the health fund — [SEC speech, Mar 3, 2015 (Ceresney)](https://www.sec.gov/news/speech/2015-spch030315ajc.html); [Deloitte DART summary](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/ov-resource/6a302eb0-3f1e-11e6-95db-07c5da222a76.html)
- Schering-Plough (2004): Polish subsidiary's $76,000 donation to a charitable foundation headed by the director of a governmental purchasing body — [SEC speech 2015](https://www.sec.gov/news/speech/2015-spch030315ajc.html)
- Stryker (2013): a subsidiary's purported ~$200,000 "donation" in 2007 to a public university in Greece funded a lab in exchange for a doctor's commitment to provide business — [SEC speech 2015](https://www.sec.gov/news/speech/2015-spch030315ajc.html); [Policy & Medicine, Mar 2015](https://policymed.com/2015/03/sec-walks-through-violate-the-foreign-corrupt-practices-act.html)
- Alstom (2014): DOJ/SEC treated >$2 million paid to a charity associated with a foreign official as a bribe even though the charity was bona fide — [Willkie Compliance Concourse, Sept 2016](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/insights-2016-20160923-recent-fcpa-enforcement-action/)
- Teva (Dec 2016): $519 million to resolve parallel DOJ/SEC charges of bribing officials in Russia, Ukraine and Mexico (~$283M DOJ / $236M SEC) — [Phillips & Cohen](https://www.phillipsandcohen.com/whistleblower-resources/largest-fcpa-settlements/); [Baron & Budd](https://www.becomeawhistleblower.com/foreign-corrupt-practices-act/pharmaceutical/)
- Fresenius Medical Care (2019): $231 million global settlement with SEC and DOJ — [SEC FCPA cases list](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-enforcement/enforcement-topics-initiatives/sec-enforcement-actions-fcpa-cases); [Phillips & Cohen](https://www.phillipsandcohen.com/whistleblower-resources/largest-fcpa-settlements/)
- Novartis (June 2020): ~$345.9 million total (reported as $234M DOJ + $113M SEC; SEC portion $112.8M = $92.3M disgorgement + $20.5M interest) over an alleged bribery scheme at hospitals in Greece and improper recordkeeping in Vietnam and other countries — [FiercePharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/novartis-inks-settlements-worth-347m-doj-sec-over-foreign-doctor-payments-records-keeping); [Miller & Chevalier FCPA Summer Review 2020](https://www.millerchevalier.com/publication/fcpa-summer-review-2020); [Drug Discovery & Development](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/novartis-alexion-charged-with-fpca-violations/)
- Alexion (SEC, July 2, 2020): >$21 million for books-and-records and internal-controls violations involving Turkey and Russia subsidiaries; Brazil and Colombia subsidiaries also failed to maintain accurate records; neither admitted nor denied — [Miller & Chevalier Summer Review 2020](https://www.millerchevalier.com/publication/fcpa-summer-review-2020); [JD Supra, Alexion Part 2](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/alexion-pharmaceuticals-fcpa-48911/); a practitioner analysis titled "Lessons on Honoraria and Research Grants" (Anti-Corruption Report, July 22, 2020) treats Alexion as a grants/honoraria case — [RSHC analysis PDF](https://www.rshc-law.com/docs/default-source/articles/alexion-fcpa-sec-settlement-ac-report.pdf)
- Stanford FCPA Clearinghouse enforcement-action dataset entry for a pharma matter — [Stanford FCPA Clearinghouse](https://fcpa.stanford.edu/enforcement-action.html?id=801)

**2025–2026 DOJ enforcement posture**
- DOJ's June 2025 FCPA Guidelines ended the 2025 enforcement pause and identified four focus areas: eliminating cartels and transnational criminal organisations; conduct detrimental to U.S. companies; U.S. national-security interests; and "serious misconduct" — [Dorsey, "New DOJ Guidelines Mark the End of the FCPA Enforcement Pause"](https://www.dorsey.com/insights/new-doj-guidelines-mark-the-end-of-the-fcpa-enforcement-pause); [Alston & Bird, Dec 2025](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/doj-aci-fcpa-enforcement-2026)
- Under the 2025 guidelines, matters lacking a substantial U.S. nexus, or involving de minimis courtesies or low-value "customary business practices", may be declined for U.S. investigation — [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/doj-aci-fcpa-enforcement-2026); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/fcpa-enforcement-priorities-continue-to-drive-outcomes-an-update-on-developments-102o452)
- DOJ issued its first FCPA deferred prosecution agreement under the new guidelines in late 2025/early 2026 — [NYU Compliance & Enforcement, Jan 7, 2026](https://wp.nyu.edu/compliance_enforcement/2026/01/07/doj-issues-first-fcpa-deferred-prosecution-agreement-under-new-guidelines/)
- In March 2026 DOJ adopted a department-wide corporate enforcement policy with three tiers of outcomes, starting with declinations for companies that voluntarily self-disclose, fully cooperate, timely remediate and have no aggravating circumstances; the first declination under it was for medical-device maker Balt SAS, which paid bribes to a physician in a senior role at a state-owned French public hospital to cause purchases of Balt devices, with ~$1.2 million disgorgement — [Arnold & Porter, Global Anti-Corruption Insights Summer 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2026/07/global-anti-corruption-insights-summer-2026)
- Commentary notes DOJ Criminal Division leadership stating at a Sept 30, 2026 event that FCPA enforcement is "really ramping up" (attribution among the search-result sources uncertain; most likely the Arnold & Porter or Alston pieces or a later update) — [Arnold & Porter Summer 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2026/07/global-anti-corruption-insights-summer-2026); [Xcelus, "Is the FCPA still being enforced in 2026?"](https://www.xcelus.com/is-the-fcpa-still-being-enforced/)
- Healthcare-sector overview of shifting federal enforcement priorities in 2025 — [Corporate Compliance Insights](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/what-you-need-know-about-healthcare-compliance-shifting-federal-enforcement-priorities/)

### Inferences
- The consistent fact pattern (foundation/university/hospital linked to a government health official; donation or grant timed with tenders or formulary decisions; mis-booked as "marketing" or "education") maps to platform controls: (a) flag any foreign recipient that is a government-owned/controlled hospital, university or health fund, or a foundation whose officers include public officials (capture beneficial-ownership/officer list and PEP screening); (b) require business-purpose memo, absence of pending tenders/purchasing decisions, and payee-bank-account ownership match; (c) book the transaction under an accurate GL category and retain evidence (the Alexion and Novartis charges were books-and-records/internal-controls, which turn on documentation quality).

## Key Question 6: Tax and corporate considerations — charitable deduction vs business expense, documentation, fair market value

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** Key rules a platform should support: (1) IRC § 170(c) charitable contributions are deductible only if made to qualifying organisations (domestic 501(c)(3), governmental units) without expectation of substantial return benefit; corporate deductions are capped at 10% of taxable income (§ 170(b)(2)) and, under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, subject to a new 1% floor for tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2025 — so only contributions exceeding 1% of taxable income are deductible, with carryforward rules; (2) payments with a business purpose (educational grants, sponsorships with advertising, research grants with deliverables) are generally § 162 ordinary and necessary business expenses rather than § 170 gifts; (3) § 170(f)(8) requires a contemporaneous written acknowledgment from the charity for any contribution of $250 or more, stating whether goods or services were provided and their good-faith value; § 6115 requires the charity to disclose quid pro quo for payments over $75; (4) § 513(i) "qualified sponsorship payments" are not unrelated business income to the charity if the sponsor receives only acknowledgment (name/logo/product lines) and no "substantial return benefit" (advertising, exclusive provider arrangements, >2% of payment in goods/services); (5) product donations by C corporations of inventory to 501(c)(3)s for the care of the ill, needy or infants get enhanced deduction under § 170(e)(3) (basis + 50% of gain, capped at 2× basis), requiring a written statement from the donee; (6) grants to foreign charities are not deductible under § 170 (domestic-organisation requirement) and are typically business expenses or made via a US "friends of" intermediary; (7) FMV documentation is required for in-kind donations (Form 8283 for non-cash contributions over $500; qualified appraisal over $5,000).
- Platform implication: each award needs a "tax classification" field (charitable contribution / business expense / in-kind product donation / research services) decided by Tax or Finance, with the supporting documents (donee acknowledgment, sponsorship benefits schedule, FMV basis) stored against it.

## Key Question 7: 2024–2026 DOJ/OIG posture — Corporate Enforcement Policy, 2024 ECCP updates on data analytics and AI, implications for audit trails and AI use in compliance workflows

### Takeaway
DOJ's Sept 23, 2024 revision of the Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs added explicit expectations that companies assess and control AI/emerging-technology risk (including "false approvals and documentation generated by AI"), continuously test that technology works as intended, use data analytics to measure compliance effectiveness, manage data quality (accuracy/precision/recall of models), give compliance timely data access, and protect whistleblowers; combined with the Nov 2023 OIG GCPG and DOJ's March 2026 department-wide corporate enforcement policy, this means a grants platform must produce an immutable, queryable audit trail and must document any AI-assisted screening or drafting with human review and accuracy testing.

### Cited Findings
- The Criminal Division revised the ECCP on Sept 23, 2024; prosecutors use it to judge program effectiveness at the time of the offense and at charging, which affects resolution terms and monitorship decisions — [Jones Day, Oct 2024](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2024/10/doj-updates-corporate-compliance-program-guidance-with-a-focus-on-ai); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/doj-updates-evaluation-of-corporate-compliance-programs-guidance-focused-on-ai-and-emerging-technologies/)
- The update asks how companies identify and manage AI risks; a DOJ official flagged "false approvals and documentation generated by AI" and said DOJ will look at whether companies have tools to check the accuracy of data they rely on, and at continuous monitoring and testing of technology to evaluate if it is functioning "as intended" — [Cooley, Oct 9, 2024](https://investigations.cooley.com/2024/10/09/doj-focuses-on-ai-emerging-tech-in-newly-issued-guidance-updates-for-evaluating-corporate-compliance-programs/); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/doj-s-updated-guidance-for-evaluating-corporate-compliance-programs-emphasizes-double-edged-sword-of-new-technologies)
- Prosecutors ask whether companies use data analytics to make compliance more efficient and to measure effectiveness, whether compliance staff can get relevant data in a timely way, and "how does the company manage the quality of its data sources and measure the accuracy, precision, or recall of its analytics models?" — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-whistleblowers-and-data-analytics-updated-doj-compliance-guidance); [Carlton Fields, 2025](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/2025/doj-updates-evaluation-of-corporate-compliance-programs-guidance-what-companies-need-to-know)
- The 2024 update also addresses whistleblower incentives and anti-retaliation protections — [Global Investigations Blog](https://www.globalinvestigations.blog/uncategorized/doj-updates-guidance-on-corporate-compliance-programs/); [GMS summary](https://www.groupmgmt.com/blog/the-doj-s-updated-evaluation-of-corporate-compliance-programs/)
- OIG's Nov 2023 GCPG raises expectations for compliance-officer independence, authority and resourcing at pharma and device companies — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/compliance-program-guidance-what-compliance-officers-at-pharmaceutical-and-medical-device-companies-need-to-consider); [OIG GCPG](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/1135/HHS-OIG-GCPG-2023.pdf)
- DOJ's March 2026 department-wide corporate enforcement policy: three outcome tiers; declination for voluntary self-disclosure + full cooperation + timely remediation + no aggravating circumstances — [Arnold & Porter Summer 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2026/07/global-anti-corruption-insights-summer-2026)
- Akin Gump's 2024 consolidated guide covers both DOJ ECCP and OIG GCPG expectations — [Akin 2024 Guide](https://www.akingump.com/a/web/5vpTEWSojTA926xwieF4be/8TuVLc/akin-2024-guide-to-doj-and-hhs-oig.pdf)

### Inferences
- Audit-trail requirements derivable from the ECCP: immutable event log (who/what/when/prior value/new value) for every request, score, approval, payment, screening hit, and policy exception; role-based access with segregation of duties enforced in software (sales vs. grants committee vs. finance); retention aligned with federal look-back (platform should default to ≥ 10 years to cover FCA 6–10 year limitations); exportable analytics (e.g., grant concentration by HCP/institution, correlation of grants with sales territories, repeat-funding patterns, aggregate spend per covered recipient against state caps) that compliance can run without IT.
- AI-use requirements: any model used for screening, triage, scoring or drafting must be versioned, have documented accuracy/precision/recall testing, human-in-the-loop approval for consequential outcomes, and the ability to show the human reviewer saw and overrode/accepted AI output — directly answering the "false approvals generated by AI" concern.
- **[UNVERIFIED – background knowledge]** DOJ Criminal Division also revised its Corporate Enforcement and Voluntary Self-Disclosure Policy on May 12, 2025 (Galeotti memo) and DOJ/HHS announced a joint False Claims Act Working Group on July 2, 2025 prioritising, among other things, kickbacks and Medicare Advantage; neither was confirmed in this session.

### Gaps

## Consolidated platform requirements implied by the sourced findings (cross-reference list for the report writer)

Derived from the Cited Findings above; items marked † rest partly on unverified background knowledge.

**Required data fields per request/award**
- Funding type (educational grant / research grant / charitable donation / sponsorship / corporate membership / patient advocacy / PAP donation / product donation); business purpose statement; therapeutic area; product association (Y/N, product name/NDC)†.
- Applicant organisation: legal name, EIN/TIN, W-9/W-8†, 501(c)(3) status evidence†, teaching-hospital-list match (program year), state(s) of operation, government ownership/control flag (FCPA), officers/PEP flag (FCPA), bank account ownership match (FCPA).
- Individual beneficiaries (where any): NPPES name, NPI, state licence + state, specialty, address, covered-recipient type (physician/NPP/TH) — 42 CFR 403.904(c).
- Open Payments fields: program year, date, amount, form, nature of payment, third-party/indirect flags, delayed-publication eligibility (§ 403.910), research fields (§ 403.904(f)).
- State fields: VT value/nature/purpose/recipient and clinical-trial deferral; MA ≥$50 per-occurrence and sales-and-marketing-connection flag; CA cap category and exclusion flag; NJ cap-relevant compensation; MN conference-sponsor-only payee check.
- PAP-specific: charity name, disease fund, advisory-opinion relied upon and expiry, earmarking prohibition attestation, no data-feedback attestation (2014 bulletin factors).
- Tax classification (charitable / business expense / in-kind) and substantiation documents†.

**Workflow rules**
- Sales/marketing excluded from create/score/approve (2003 CPG); objective, stored scoring criteria with no purchase-volume inputs; no conditioning on purchases (2003 CPG).
- CME grants: non-direction attestation; record of when identities became known (2014 CMS indirect-payment reasoning).
- FCPA: enhanced due diligence tier for foreign public institutions; documented purpose; no pending tender; post-award use-of-funds monitoring.
- Screening at intake, pre-payment and monthly†: LEIE, SAM, OFAC, state Medicaid exclusions, FDA debarment, IRS TEOS; store list version, match, adjudication.
- AI assistance: versioned models, accuracy testing, human approval evidenced (ECCP 2024).

**Reporting outputs**
- Open Payments submission file (March window; publication by June 30; January refresh); VT April 1 file; MA July 1 file; CA annual declaration cap-utilisation report; CT July 1 and NV May 1–June 1 certification evidence packs; 1099-MISC/NEC extract at $2,000 threshold for 2026 payments; FCPA due-diligence register.

**Audit trail**
- Immutable, time-stamped, user-attributed log of every state change, approval, screening result, document upload and payment; exportable analytics for compliance (ECCP 2024; GCPG 2023); retention ≥ 10 years†.
