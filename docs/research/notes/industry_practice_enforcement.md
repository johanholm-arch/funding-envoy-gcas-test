## Key question 1 — Operating model and SOP structure of a typical pharma/medtech grants and donations function (request families, triage, firewall, lead times, LOA, reconciliation, outcomes, KPIs, publication)

### Takeaway
The de facto industry SOP visible from public portals is remarkably standardized: a request is filed through a vendor-hosted portal (Versaic/Bonterra, NGCS, Giving Connection, etc.) at least 60 calendar days before the activity (120 days ex-US at Lilly; 90 days for some Merck/MSD patient-advocacy types from 1 Oct 2025; 8 weeks at Stryker), reviewed by a grants office that is explicitly separated from sales/marketing, decided within roughly 45–90 days, paid only after a signed letter of agreement (LOA), and closed with a reconciliation of actual spend plus return of unused funds (45–90 days after the activity) and an outcomes report graded on Moore-style levels. Formal KPI/KRI benchmarks for the funder side were not found in public sources.

### Cited Findings

Request families and portal scope
- Novartis US organizes "External Funding" into professional medical education grants, patient education grants, and sponsorships and memberships, each with its own page and rules; sponsorship/membership requests "related to Novartis products, in particular marketing and sales activities (i.e. promotional exhibit or booth space ... promotional product theater)", "contractual service agreements or fee-for-service arrangements" and "initiatives focused solely on raising awareness of Novartis-specific clinical trials" are explicitly not accepted — [Novartis US Sponsorships and Memberships](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/sponsorships-and-memberships); [Novartis US Educational Grants](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/educational-grants)
- Medtronic's "Giving Connection" (MGC) system reviews requests for medical conferences, charitable events, medical education and public education; general philanthropic requests are not supported through MGC; "some grant types are only awarded at certain times of year"; every application is checked against Medtronic's Business Conduct Standards and the AdvaMed Code of Ethics — [Medtronic Grants & Donations FAQ](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/our-company/governance/grants-donations/donation-faqs.html); [Medtronic Charitable Donations](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/our-company/governance/grants-donations/medtronic-charitable-donations.html)
- Abbott (cardiovascular) runs separate tracks for educational grants, fellowship grants, and product/charitable donations: fellowship grants go only to U.S.-based teaching hospitals and universities, fellows may not apply on their own behalf, funding covers one academic year with annual reapplication; educational-grant budgets are limited to non-transfer-of-value costs (accreditation, audiovisual, marketing, meeting room, administrative) and charitable applications may not contain educational content for clinicians, patients or the public — [Abbott CV Product and Charitable Donations](https://www.cardiovascular.abbott/us/en/hcp/grants-donations/product-charitable-donations.html); [Abbott CV Grants & Donations FAQ](https://www.cardiovascular.abbott/us/en/hcp/grants-donations/product-charitable-donations/frequently-asked-questions.html); Abbott's older (1 Jan 2013) Operating Procedures for Program Funding exclude capital projects and lobbying — [Abbott OPPF (2013)](https://dam.abbott.com/en-us/documents/pdfs/partners/OPPF_Complete.pdf)
- Boston Scientific runs "grants, donations, exhibits" as one giving function; its IME page requires a completed W-9 and announces a migration to a "new Grantee and Applicant Experience starting on July 20, 2026", with legacy applications to be submitted before that date — [Boston Scientific IME grants](https://www.bostonscientific.com/en-US/corporate-responsibility/giving/grants-donations-exhibits/educational-conference.html)
- Pfizer's "Global Medical Grants & Partnerships" runs a competitive grant program via public RFPs: a "general grant program RFP" is reviewed and approved internally, while a "specific grant program RFP" gives more detail and uses an external review panel; individuals and medical practice groups are not eligible; applications go through an online portal; a sample RFP sets a 15-page limit excluding references; overhead of up to 28% of the total proposed budget may be supported — [Pfizer Competitive Grant Program FAQ](https://pfe-pfizercom-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/CompetitveGrantProgram_FrequentlyAskedQuestions.pdf); [Pfizer 2025 Migraine IME RFP](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/2025-02/GMG-2025-IM-US-Migraine-IME.pdf?VersionId=0vSeQ6EIaXYOQxiyMshHPSI4QqNkWygO); [Pfizer tucatinib ISR RFP, Nov 2024](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/2024-11/tucatinib_therapy_management_research_ISR_RFP2024.pdf?VersionId=6eDQRTtO5SY0F.aPoYMoU431vuOy6hHd)
- Jazz accepts unsolicited medical-education requests only twice a year; Axogen reviews research grants quarterly and charitable donations on a rolling basis and cites both AdvaMed and PhRMA codes; Vertex takes sponsorships on a rolling basis — [Jazz Corporate Giving](https://www.jazzpharma.com/our-purpose/corporate-giving); [Axogen grants, donations and sponsorships](https://www.axogeninc.com/grants-donations-and-sponsorships/); [Vertex funding FAQ](https://www.vrtx.com/sites/default/files/Common-Questions-and-Funding-Information.pdf)
- Merck/MSD requires an IME applicant's conflict-of-interest policy and by-laws or charter, and applications must be complete and submitted through the grant management system — [Merck IME FAQ (Versaic)](https://cdn.versaic.com/merck/b031d12e-17cf-4b71-90d9-7fe0af4e254e); Merck's portal login is hosted at msd.versaic.com — [MSD Proposal Management System](https://msd.versaic.com/login)

Firewall from sales/marketing and non-linkage to business
- The 2007 Senate Finance Committee staff report (Committee Print 110-21, Baucus/Grassley, April 2007) found that "drug manufacturers have implemented compliance policies that separate the grant-making process for educational programs from their marketing efforts, and in most cases limited sales representatives' involvement," while also finding ACCME records showing "numerous cases over the past 3 years in which companies exercised too much influence over the content" — [FDA Law Blog summary, May 2007](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2007/05/senate_finance_/); [Phillips & Cohen summary](https://www.phillipsandcohen.com/senate-report-says-drug-companies-use-continuing-medical-education-grants-to-influence-doctors/); report PDF — [Senate Finance Prt110-21](https://finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/Prt110-21.pdf)
- Novartis US: "grants must never be linked to prescribing, purchasing, formulary status or reimbursement" and "cannot include any tangible benefits to Novartis beyond an acknowledgement (e.g. 'Supported by an educational grant from Novartis')" — [Novartis US external funding FAQs](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/faqs)
- A compliance guide states grant decisions should be based on educational merit and patient benefit with "no sales targets or formulary status considered," and that companies should separate CME grant-making from sales and marketing — [IntuitionLabs, Pharma Funding for Physician Education](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/pharma-funding-physician-education-compliance)

Lead-time rules (submission before activity start)
- 60 days before activity start: Phathom (also "does not provide retroactive funding"; requests "should not exceed $15,000"), Apellis, AbbVie (portal "will not allow a program start date to be entered after the 60-day deadline"), Acadia, Lundbeck, Boehringer Ingelheim, Mirum, PTC, Abbott, Novartis ("at least 60 calendar days prior to the event date ... the grant request may be denied"), Medtronic ("at least 60 days prior to the requested funding decision date or event start date") — [Phathom IME](https://www.phathompharma.com/our-science/ime-grants/); [Apellis IME](https://apellis.com/about-us/corporate-giving/independent-medical-education/); [AbbVie How to Apply](https://www.abbvie.com/science/independent-educational-grants/how-to-apply.html); [Acadia IME](https://acadia.com/en-us/healthcare-professionals/resources/independent-medical-education-grants); [Lundbeck US IME](https://www.lundbeck.com/us/community-engagement/grants-and-contributions/independent-medical-education); [BIPI grants tutorial](https://docs.boehringer-ingelheim.com/misc/BIPI-Medical-Education-Grants-Tutorial.pdf); [Mirum](https://mirumpharma.com/medical-affairs/grants-sponsorships/); [PTC](https://www.ptcbio.com/grants-and-donations/); [Novartis PME grants](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/educational-grants/professional-medical-education-grants); [Medtronic FAQ](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/our-company/governance/grants-donations/donation-faqs.html)
- Lilly Grant Office: at least 60 days before the program start date (or the request is not reviewed); organizations outside the U.S. or events abroad are advised to file 120 days ahead — [Lilly Grant Office Requirements](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/requirements)
- Merck IME: at least 60 calendar days before the activity start date; an older MSD CME/CE FAQ (rev. 09/2018) said 45 days; an MSD portal notice says that from 1 Oct 2025 the submission timeline for "Patient Advocacy Educational and Fundraiser Event" grant types changes from 60 to 90 days — [Merck IME FAQ](https://cdn.versaic.com/merck/b031d12e-17cf-4b71-90d9-7fe0af4e254e); [MSD CME-CE FAQ 2018](https://msd.versaic.com/Simple/Merck/CME-CE_FAQs.pdf); [MSD portal login notice](https://msd.versaic.com/login)
- Stryker: at least 8 weeks before program start; review considers whether content comes from an independent medical education provider, audience, location and budget; "funding is limited, and not all requests will receive support" — [Stryker educational grants and charitable donations](https://www.stryker.com/us/en/about/contact/donations.html)
- Novartis post-congress grants must be received 60 days before the congress starts; Novartis "will not consider funding for events which have already begun or taken place" — [Novartis PME grants](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/educational-grants/professional-medical-education-grants)
- argenx: scope changes must be requested at least 30 days before the activity start and approved before the activity starts — [argenx grants](https://argenx.com/grants)

Decision timelines
- Lilly: review goal within 60 days (USA) and 90–120 days (outside USA) after complete documentation is received — [Lilly Grant Office FAQ](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/faq)
- Johnson & Johnson (Innovative Medicine US): decisions within 90 days of a completed submission after internal review — [J&J Education Grants and Giving](https://www.jnj.com/innovativemedicine/us/grants-and-giving/educational)
- Merck nonprofit grant guidelines (Feb 2025): "the entire review and decision-making process may take up to eight weeks"; a Merck medical-education RFP says there is no submission deadline and review times depend on volume — [Merck Grant Application Guidelines](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2024/01/Merck_GrantApplicationGuidelines.pdf); [Merck Collaborative for Equity in Cardiac Care CFP, Nov 2024](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2024/11/Collaborative-for-Equity-in-Cardiac-Care_Call-for-Proposals_11.05.24.pdf)
- Phathom notifies applicants 15–45 days after submission; UCB (2018 FAQ) needs at least 60 days to review and respond; PENTAX Medical review "generally 45 days"; Medtronic review "may take less than 60 days for certain types" — [Phathom](https://www.phathompharma.com/our-science/ime-grants/); [UCB FAQ April 2018](https://www.ucb.com/_up/ucb_com_our_company/documents/USA-Grant-FAQ-April2018.pdf); [PENTAX](https://www.pentaxmedical.com/en/about-us/grant-and-donation-requests); [Medtronic FAQ](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/our-company/governance/grants-donations/donation-faqs.html)
- Novartis: applicants receive e-mail correspondence from the Grants Office "commencing as soon as your complete grant application has been submitted and throughout the review process"; "previous support by Novartis does NOT guarantee future support" — [Novartis PME grants](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/educational-grants/professional-medical-education-grants)

Letter of agreement, payment, milestones
- Pfizer: payment is released only after the signed LOA is accepted, and it may take up to 45 days from LOA acceptance for funds to arrive — [Pfizer Process Overview](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/responsibility/education/process_overview.pdf)
- Apellis: all grant payments are paid to the CME/CE provider (not to third parties) — [Apellis IME](https://apellis.com/about-us/corporate-giving/independent-medical-education/)

Reconciliation and return of unused funds
- Pfizer: recipients must provide a post-activity reconciliation (PAR) report within 60 days of the educational activity covering how funds were spent and any available outcomes data — [Pfizer Process Overview](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/responsibility/education/process_overview.pdf)
- AbbVie and Boehringer Ingelheim: reconciliation within 90 days after program completion — [AbbVie How to Apply](https://www.abbvie.com/science/independent-educational-grants/how-to-apply.html); [BIPI tutorial](https://docs.boehringer-ingelheim.com/misc/BIPI-Medical-Education-Grants-Tutorial.pdf)
- Lilly: grantees must complete a post-program budget reconciliation including return of unused funds where applicable — [Lilly Grant Office FAQ](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/faq)
- Merck US Patient Advocacy: separate outcomes form and reconciliation form, each due within 45 calendar days; "you must return any unused funds by sending a check to the address on the reconciliation form"; the outcomes form covers attendance and knowledge change and verifies funding was used as described — [Merck US Patient Advocacy, After you apply](https://merckuspatientadvocacy.com/after-you-apply)
- Merck medical-education RFP requires providers to measure learning effectiveness and submit a final report and financial reconciliation — [Merck CFP Nov 2024](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2024/11/Collaborative-for-Equity-in-Cardiac-Care_Call-for-Proposals_11.05.24.pdf)

Outcomes reporting (Moore-style levels)
- Lilly requires, for IME aimed at measurable gains in competence/performance: status reports, periodic and final outcomes reports, monthly Level 1 participation data, and quarterly Level 2–4+ data entered in the portal; a separate "IME Outcomes Reporting Guidance Document" is on the portal — [Lilly Grant Office Requirements](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/requirements)
- A Lilly Call for Grant Applications (Sept 2024) requires an outcomes plan using the Outcomes Standardization Project glossary, naming expected learner and completer counts, and says a generic outcomes-model description is insufficient — [Lilly CGA, global brain health (ACEHP-hosted)](https://www.acehp.org/Portals/0/RFPs/9.20.24.globalbrainhealth.CGA.final.pdf?ver=h7Ja03NPg8kPu9ZGP43xUA%3D%3D&timestamp=1726851373380)
- argenx requires an outcomes report within 5 business days after completion of a live activity — [argenx grants](https://argenx.com/grants)

Exclusions / "not funded" lists
- Novartis will not fund overhead/administrative costs, recreational components (golf, spa, resort activities), gifts to speakers or attendees, or activities where speakers/participants may bring a spouse or guest to a meal; patient-education grants only to 501(c)(3), (c)(4) or (c)(6) nonprofits; therapeutic areas "subject to change and funding availability may vary" — [Novartis FAQs](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/faqs); [Novartis Patient Education Grants](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/educational-grants/patient-education-grants)
- AbbVie only considers grant requests from providers in the "AbbVie IME Provider Network" (a pre-qualified network) — [AbbVie How to Apply](https://www.abbvie.com/science/independent-educational-grants/how-to-apply.html)
- Genentech: accredited activities must be requested by the accrediting organization (the accredited provider) — [Genentech IME](https://www.gene.com/good/giving/corporate-giving/imed)

Publication of policies and award lists
- Merck discloses grants above $500 from its Global Human Health division to U.S. organizations (organization, amount, payment date, project), updated quarterly in the U.S. and annually ex-U.S.; its gPRIME transparency files list payee name, city, state and grant/payment type — [Merck transparency disclosures](https://www.merck.com/company-overview/sustainability/transparency-disclosures/); [gPRIME 2023 data](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2024/01/gPRIME-2023_Transparency_Data.pdf); [gPRIME 2022 data](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2023/07/gPRIME_2022_Transparency_Data.pdf)
- Lilly publishes a "Grant Registry by Organizational Name" (2025 edition) — [Lilly grant registry 2025](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/assets/pdf/grant-registry-2025.pdf); Lilly announced it would publicly post all educational grant funding at the time of the 2007 Senate report — [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2007/05/senate_finance_/)
- Novartis publishes RFPs with stated budgets (e.g., hyperlipidemia RFP: "Up to USD 750 000 in total support is available for 2023") — [Novartis hyperlipidemia RFP](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/about/external-funding/educational-grants/professional-medical-education-grants/request-proposal-rfp-hyperlipidemia)
- The Allergan 2010 CIA (SEC exhibit) required the company, within 120 days of the effective date and then bi-annually, to post on its website medical-education grants and charitable contributions to U.S. health care institutions (recipient, program description, amount) — [Allergan CIA exhibit 10.2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/850693/000119312510202820/dex102.htm)

- "Many teaching hospitals these days are unable to accept independent funding directly from industry grantors due to institutional policies against accepting commercial support" — [Springer, J Cancer Educ (2014)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13187-014-0664-4)

### Inferences
- The modal SOP is: portal intake → completeness check → medical/scientific merit review (plus legal/compliance) separated from commercial → decision in 45–90 days → LOA → payment (up to 45 days) → activity → reconciliation + refund of unused funds (45–90 days) → outcomes report (Levels 1–4+) → closure. No company page found describes committee composition or voting rules publicly; that layer stays internal.
- The repeated "60 days" rule is clearly an industry convention, not a regulatory requirement; deviations are longer (90/120 days ex-US, 90 days for patient-advocacy event types at MSD from Oct 2025), never shorter, suggesting lead-time rules are driven by internal review capacity and cross-border legal checks.
- Vendor portals dominate: Merck (Versaic/Bonterra), Novartis (NGCS, being replaced 16 Nov 2026), Medtronic (Giving Connection), Boston Scientific (new grantee experience 20 Jul 2026), Pfizer (online portal). Multiple concurrent platform migrations in 2026 indicate active re-platforming in the market.
- Outcomes requirements are increasingly standardized on the Alliance's Outcomes Standardization Project vocabulary (Lilly explicitly requires it), which a platform should support natively.

### Gaps
- No public source found describing funder-side KPIs/KRIs (time to decision, % approved, information-request rounds, agreement-before-start compliance, overdue reconciliations) or annual monitoring of grants programs. The search for "grants KPIs" returned only generic grant-dashboard material — [InetSoft grant KPIs](https://www.inetsoft.com/business/bi/kpis-in-grant-management-dashboards/) — not pharma-specific.
- No company page found that states a revenue-concentration cap (e.g., "not more than X% of recipient revenue"); see Key question 6 for the closest benchmarks.
- Fair-market-value/reasonableness methodology for budget review, milestone-payment schedules, and record-retention periods were not found on public pages.
- UNVERIFIED (prior knowledge, verify): Moore, Green & Gallis (J Contin Educ Health Prof, 2009) define the 7-level outcomes framework (1 participation; 2 satisfaction; 3A/3B declarative/procedural knowledge; 4 competence; 5 performance; 6 patient health; 7 community health) that the "Level 1–4+" language on Lilly's page refers to.
- Pages for AstraZeneca, Amgen, Bristol Myers Squibb, Takeda, Gilead, Roche (ex-Genentech) grants programs were not reached before the search budget ran out; their lead times, documents and cycles remain unconfirmed.

## Key question 2 — What the named companies publicly disclose about their grants processes (Pfizer, Lilly, Novartis, AbbVie, Gilead, Merck/MSD, J&J, AstraZeneca, Amgen, BMS, Takeda, Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Abbott, Roche/Genentech)

### Takeaway
Of the 16 named companies, concrete public process details were confirmed for Pfizer, Lilly, Novartis, AbbVie, Merck/MSD, J&J, Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Abbott and Genentech; the remaining five (Gilead, AstraZeneca, Amgen, BMS, Takeda) surfaced only as portal names (Takeda's grants portal is listed by ACEHP) before the search budget was exhausted.

## Key question 3 — Enforcement actions and settlements since 2005 involving grants, donations, charitable patient assistance, sponsorships or memberships, and the control failures revealed

### Takeaway
Three enforcement waves map onto three control failures: (1) 2004–2010 educational-grant/off-label cases (Serono 2005 $704M, Cephalon 2008 $425M, Lilly 2009 $1.415B, AstraZeneca 2010 $520M, Allergan 2010 $600M, Novartis 2010 $423M) produced CIAs that forced public posting of grants and the firewall; (2) 2016–2024 device and copay-charity cases (Olympus 2016 ≈$623–646M; the 2017–2025 copay-foundation series including Teva's $425M+$25M in Oct 2024 and Dompé's $32M self-disclosure) revealed grants/donations tied to purchases, earmarked disease funds and data flowing back from charities; (3) FCPA/UK Code cases (Schering-Plough 2004, Lilly 2012, BMS 2015, Novartis 2016/2020, Alexion 2020, Fresenius 2019; PMCPA cases through 8 Oct 2026) show donations/sponsorships to foundations, universities and HCOs used as bribes or made without written agreements, certification or disclosure.

### Inferences (control that should have caught each mechanism)
- Copay-foundation cases: the failing controls were (a) donation sizing driven by the company's own patient counts/forecasts for its drug, (b) disease funds defined narrowly enough to cover only the donor's product, (c) receipt of patient-level or product-share data from the foundation, and (d) commercial/market-access ownership of the donation budget. A platform control set: donations to independent charities routed through a non-commercial function; prohibition on receiving utilization data; disease-fund breadth attestation; documented budget rationale independent of product forecasts; annual legal review. (Mechanism descriptions drawn from the Teva, Gilead and Biogen reporting above; OIG 2005/2014 bulletin text not verified this session — see Gaps.)
- Olympus/Merit/Medtronic: grants and "educational" spend were initiated or conditioned by sales (grant held until a purchase; grants only to selected purchasers; sales staff expensing social events as education). Controls: sales cannot initiate, approve, or be informed of grant status; grant decisions cannot reference purchasing; expense categories reconciled to Open Payments reporting.
- FCPA cases: donations to a foundation controlled by a public purchaser, congress sponsorships tied to prescription quotas, mis-recorded payments. Controls: beneficial-owner/affiliation screening of recipient organizations against HCP/official lists; sponsorship decisions decoupled from sales metrics; accurate books-and-records categorization.
- PMCPA 2024–2026 cases cluster on documentation hygiene rather than intent: no written agreement, agreement missing the mandatory acknowledgement wording, no final-signatory certification, and disclosure errors over multi-year grants — all of which a workflow system can hard-stop (agreement template with mandatory clauses, certification gate before payment, automatic disclosure feed).

## Key question 4 — Requestor-side pain points and metrics (medical societies, hospitals, universities, patient advocacy groups, charities)

### Takeaway
Direct survey data on requestor burden (time to prepare, % returned for information, reasons for declines) was not found in public sources this session; the pain points are instead evidenced structurally — a dozen-plus distinct portals and rules, duplicate applications for joint calls, portal migrations, shifting lead times (60→90 days), and separate outcomes/reconciliation forms with 45–90 day deadlines — plus patient-organisation research showing rising financial pressure and unreliable disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- ACEHP's funding page lists separate sponsor systems (Merck grant portal, Takeda support portal, Novartis NGCS) and hosts individual RFPs/CGAs; a joint Bayer–Lilly call required two separate applications — [ACEHP Funding Opportunities](https://www.acehp.org/Resource/Funding-Opportunities); [Bayer–Lilly joint call](https://www.acehp.org/Portals/0/Joint%20Call%20for%20Grants%20Proposal_Bayer%20and%20Lilly_Tumor-agnostic%20testing_FINAL_1.pdf)

- Supply-demand: Novartis says many more requests are received than can be funded; Stryker says not all requests receive support — [Novartis FAQs](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/faqs); [Stryker](https://www.stryker.com/us/en/about/contact/donations.html)
- Institutional barriers: many teaching hospitals cannot accept industry funding directly due to institutional policies (oncology CME analysis) — [J Cancer Educ 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13187-014-0664-4)
- Funding environment: ACCME 2024 data show commercial support down 5% YoY against record $3.7B total income — [ACCME](https://accme.org/news/accme-releases-2024-data-report-evolving-impact-in-a-shifting-landscape/); commercial support fell from 50.3% of CME income (2006) to 39.2% (2009) — [EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/cme-who-will-pay-the-tab/); between 2014 and 2017 the value and number of disbursements for accredited CME to med-ed firms rose 821% and 209% respectively — [MM+M](https://www.mmm-online.com/home/channel/increase-in-med-ed-firms-cme-support-shows-how-funding-is-changing-in-a-post-sunshine-world/)

- Patient organisations: EURORDIS's 2024 membership survey (July–Sept 2024; 140 valid responses from 1,085 members across 31 countries) reports growing demands and "increasing financial pressures" on patient groups with limited resources; EURORDIS keeps core operations independent of corporate funding but expects to expand company support for activities and projects — [EURORDIS Navigating Change survey](https://www.eurordis.org/publications/navigating-change-membership-survey-report/); [EURORDIS policy on commercial support](https://www.eurordis.org/publications/eurordis-policy-on-financial-support-by-commercial-companies-2/)

- Researcher call (Oct 2024) for mandatory transparency of pharma funding to patient advocacy groups — [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2024-10-pharma-company-funding-patient-advocacy.html)

### Inferences
- The requestor burden is a product of fragmentation (N portals × distinct schemas × distinct clocks) rather than any single portal's complexity; the measurable consequences (duplicate data entry, document re-uploads, missed 60/90-day windows during migrations) are inferred from the structural facts above, not from survey data.
- Declined-without-reason is common practice by inference: no company page found commits to giving reasons, while several stress discretionary, merit-based, non-guaranteed decisions.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying time-to-prepare a request, % of requests returned for more information, or decision timelines from the requestor perspective was found (searches of CMSS, ACEHP/Almanac, SACME returned RFP postings and clinician-attitude studies only).
- National Health Council (NHC) principles/standards on industry funding and its surveys were not retrieved (search returned nothing for NHC).
- University research-office perspectives on industry IIT/grant portals were not reached.
- UNVERIFIED (prior knowledge): the Alliance's Outcomes Standardization Project (OSP) glossary and the Alliance Industry Summit as the main grantor–provider forum.

## Key question 5 — Funder-side pain points and metrics (coordinators, medical reviewers, compliance, legal, finance)

### Takeaway
Public evidence of funder-side operations is thin: companies disclose volumes only qualitatively ("many more requests than can be funded"), none publish requests-per-coordinator or review-time metrics, and the enforcement record (Key question 3) is the best proxy for where internal controls break — undocumented grants, missing agreements/certification, sales involvement, data flows from charities, and transparency-reporting mismatches.

### Cited Findings
- Volume pressure acknowledged without numbers: Novartis — "many more requests are received than can be funded"; Merck — review times depend on how many proposals arrive — [Novartis FAQs](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/faqs); [Merck CFP Nov 2024](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2024/11/Collaborative-for-Equity-in-Cardiac-Care_Call-for-Proposals_11.05.24.pdf)

- Bonterra (formerly CyberGrants) markets a "medical affairs platform" for expanded access, medical education and research programs and says grant volume is rising across IME, research and expanded access — [Bonterra life sciences](https://www.bonterratech.com/solutions/life-sciences); [Bonterra guide](https://www.bonterratech.com/resources/faster-smarter-grantmaking-for-life-sciences-teams)
- Transparency-reporting exposure: Medtronic's 2020 $9.2M settlement included allegations of inaccurate Open Payments reporting — the first DOJ settlement with a Sunshine Act component — [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2020/10/10_30-novel-doj-settlement); UK: CSL Seqirus (8 Oct 2026) and Novo Nordisk (£7.8M undisclosed, 2020–2022) were sanctioned for disclosure failures on grants/payments — [PMCPA 8 Oct 2026](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news/pmcpa-press-release-8-october-2026-case-ruled-in-breach-of-clause-2/); [Bath](https://www.bath.ac.uk/announcements/novo-nordisk-found-in-breach-of-transparency-standards-by-pmcpa/)

## Key question 6 — Patient-organisation and HCO expectations on transparency and independence (EFPIA PO rules, NHC, society policies incl. caps and firewalls)

### Takeaway
EFPIA requires a written agreement stating amount and purpose for any financial or significant indirect/non-financial support to patient organisations, annual publication of funded organisations, and affirms PO independence and multi-source funding, but sets no numerical cap; the only numerical benchmarks found are the 2009 JAMA panel's "reassess if >25% of budget from industry" for medical societies, SVS's ~20% and AHA's ~20% self-reported levels, and the UK finding that only 4.5% of patient organisations exceed 50% dependency.

### Cited Findings
- EFPIA Code: companies must have a written agreement when providing financial support, significant indirect support and/or significant non-financial support to patient organisations; it must state the amount and purpose (e.g., unrestricted grant, specific meeting or publication), describe significant indirect support, name applicable codes, and report fees separately from expenses; each company must publish a list of POs it funds or contracts with, with transfers of value under donations, grants, sponsorship and contracted services disclosed annually; founding principles assure PO independence of "political judgement, policies and activities" and state that industry "welcomes funding from multiple sources" — [EFPIA Code of Practice 2026](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf); [EFPIA patient organisations page](https://www.efpia.eu/relationships-code/patient-organisations/); [EFPIA PO code (older)](https://www.efpia.eu/media/24310/3c_efpia-code-of-practice-on-relationships-pharmapluspt-orgs.pdf); [EFPIA "Working Together" donations/grants/sponsorship guidance](https://efpia.eu/media/2tmneiil/workingtogetherwithpatientsorganisations-donations-grants.pdf)
- EFPIA PO disclosure has been in force via self-regulation since 2012, typically as annual reports on company websites — [Industry funding of UK patient organisations (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10410975/)
- Cross-national comparison (US, UK, Germany, France, Australia, Canada) of disclosure codes for industry funding of PAOs — [Karas et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4441787); systematic review/meta-analysis of industry funding of patient and consumer organisations (2020) — [PubMed 31969320](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31969320/); Sweden five-year study — [PMC7313941](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7313941/)
- US patient advocacy organisations: 67% of a national sample received for-profit funding and 12% received more than half their funding from industry (JAMA Internal Medicine survey, ~2017) — [JAMA IM](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2598094); NEJM special report on PAO conflicts — [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsr1610625); a recent Health Affairs Scholar study of FDA advisory-committee patient representatives finds industry-tie estimates "considerably higher" than the earlier 68% figure — [Health Affairs Scholar](https://academic.oup.com/healthaffairsscholar/article/4/10/qxag269/8876111); ASPE report on PAOs' role in medical product development — [ASPE](https://aspe.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/documents/9bbc8dceb2d8fdb3af2b498b5b0d2fcc/Role-of-PAOs-in-med-prod-dev.pdf)
- Medical societies: a 2009 JAMA expert panel said physician organisations should strive for zero industry funding and that any society depending on industry for more than 25% of its budget (excluding exhibit-hall and journal-advertising income) should immediately reassess; Society for Vascular Surgery reports ~20% of revenue from industry, no aggregate cap, but must disclose the percentage of its budget industry supports; a critique cites AHA's 2016–17 budget of $912M with 20% corporate support and the European Society of Cardiology deriving 77% of annual income from industry — [amednews 2009](https://amednews.com/article/20090413/profession/304139976/2); [SVS policy](https://vascular.org/about/policies/industry-relations-and-conflict-interest-coi/management-relationships-industry); [Surgical Neurology International critique](https://surgicalneurologyint.com/article/professional-medical-associations-exert-undue-influence-despite-conflicts-of-interest); AHA journal position that education should not be supported by companies whose profitability depends on sales volume of discussed products — [Circulation 2009](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.109.869636); AAMC task force on industry funding of medical education — [AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/media/24271/download); ACP position paper 2021 — [Annals](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M21-1178)
- Novartis' public commitment that grants are never linked to prescribing/purchasing/formulary and carry no benefit beyond acknowledgement operates as the funder-side mirror of these expectations — [Novartis FAQs](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/faqs)
- PMCPA Clause 27.2 (patient organisations) requires a signed agreement; Clause 23.2 requires written agreements stating that support must be acknowledged and apparent from the start — [Novartis Case/0816/12/25](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/case08161225-complainant-v-novartis/); [Roche AUTH/3676/7/22](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/auth3676722-complainant-v-roche/)

### Inferences
- A "revenue-concentration" control in the new platform should be configurable (25% society benchmark; 50% PO dependency threshold used in UK research) with recipient-reported total income as a required field, since no regulator or code imposes a fixed number.
- Agreement templates should embed EFPIA/ABPI mandatory wording (amount, purpose, acknowledgement apparent from the start, applicable codes) because the UK cases show those exact omissions produce breaches.

### Gaps
- NHC "Standards of Excellence" and principles on industry funding not retrieved; CMSS Code for Interactions with Companies (2010, revised) not retrieved; ASCO/ACC written industry-funding policies not retrieved — all remain UNVERIFIED and should be pulled from cmss.org, asco.org, acc.org.
- EFPIA 2026 Code excerpt was fragmentary; exact clause numbers should be confirmed from the PDF.

## Key question 7 — Emerging practice 2024–2026: AI in grants review, centralized external-funding functions, Salesforce Experience Cloud portals, success metrics
