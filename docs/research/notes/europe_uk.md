## 1. EFPIA Code of Practice — donations/grants to HCOs, patient-organisation support, events, no funding of individual HCPs, and annual disclosure (incl. 2024–2026 updates and central-platform direction)

### Takeaway
The EFPIA Code of Practice (consolidated 2019, with subsequent amendments; a "2026" PDF edition is now published on efpia.eu) allows donations/grants only to HCOs and POs, only for healthcare/research/education purposes, only if documented and never as an inducement; donations/grants to individual HCPs are prohibited. Transfers of value (ToV) to HCOs and POs must be disclosed annually, within 6 months of the end of the calendar-year reporting period (in practice 20–30 June), remain public for at least 3 years, with records kept for at least 5 years. EFPIA's 2024–2026 updates centre on a mandatory PO disclosure template (first mandatory for the 2026 disclosure of 2025 ToVs), standardised methodological-note terminology/layout (same deadline), and a unified downloadable/searchable-by-recipient-name publication format mandatory for the 2027 disclosure of 2026 data; no EFPIA-wide central disclosure platform was found, but several national associations (UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden) already run central databases.

### Cited Findings

**Code versions / dates**
- EFPIA publishes a current Code of Practice PDF labelled "2026" at efpia.eu (the consolidated Code that merged the former HCP Code, PO Code and Disclosure Code in 2019) — [EFPIA Code of Practice (2026 PDF)](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf); earlier consolidated text (2019/2023 numbering) — [EFPIA Code of Practice (IFPMA mirror, 2023)](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf); the EFPIA Code Report on ethics & compliance activities, last updated 9 July 2026, is the source for the 2025–2026 amendments — [EFPIA Code Report 2025 (updated 9 July 2026)](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf).
- Section numbering changed between versions (2014 HCP Code placed donations in Article 11; the 2019+ consolidated Code places Donations & Grants in Section 12) — [EFPIA HCP Code 2014](https://efpia.eu/media/24302/3a_efpia-hcp-code-2014.pdf); [EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf).

**Donations and grants to HCOs/POs (Section 12)**
- Section 12.01: "Donations and Grants (in cash or in kind or otherwise) to HCOs and/or POs are only allowed if: (i) they are made for the purpose of supporting healthcare, research or education; (ii) they are documented and kept on record by the donor/grantor; and (iii) they do not constitute an inducement to recommend and/or prescribe, purchase, supply, sell or administer specific Medicinal Products." — [EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf)
- Section 12.02: "Donations and Grants to individuals are not permitted." — [EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf); EFPIA training module confirms "donations and grants to single individuals and/or unrelated to healthcare or scientific research or education are not permitted" — [EFPIA Module 3 Donations & Grants](https://www.efpia.eu/media/ioniysjn/module-3-donations-grants.pdf)

**Events / sponsorship / indirect ToVs**
- The 2026 Code text recommends that Member Companies confirm support/sponsorship of Events through professional congress organisers (PCOs) in written agreements, and states that event costs paid via third parties for named HCPs must be reported on an individually named basis as Indirect ToVs to HCPs — [EFPIA Code of Practice (2026 PDF)](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf)
- EFPIA disclosure Q&A (June 2025 edition) is the current EFPIA guidance on HCP disclosure questions — [EFPIA Disclosure Q&A June 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/i0yiz30d/disclosure-qa-june-2025.pdf)

**Patient organisations (written agreement + public listing)**
- Companies providing financial support, significant indirect support or significant non-financial support to a PO must have a written agreement stating the amount of funding and the purpose (e.g., unrestricted grant, specific meeting or publication), and describing significant indirect/non-financial support; a model template is provided in the Code's Annex — [EFPIA PO Code (original)](https://www.efpia.eu/media/24310/3c_efpia-code-of-practice-on-relationships-pharmapluspt-orgs.pdf); [EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf)
- Each company must publish a list of POs it supports with: PO name; monetary value of financial support and of invoiced costs; for non-financial support without a meaningful monetary value, a description of the non-monetary benefit; for contracted services, the total amount paid per PO per Reporting Period. Disclosure must be on the company website at national or European level, annually, each Reporting Period being a full calendar year — [EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf); company practice example (Astellas discloses at European level, by country, by PO registration country) — [Astellas PO methodological note](https://www.astellas.com/content/dam/astellas-com/global/en/documents/transparency-center-en/2023-patient-organisations-disclosure-methodology-note-v1.pdf)
- The EFPIA Ethics & Compliance Committee (E&CC) endorsed a mandatory PO disclosure template (based on an improved version of the optional 2018 template) with corresponding Code amendments; the Code states the template must be transposed and implemented at the latest for the 2026 disclosure of 2025 ToVs — [EFPIA Code Report 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf); [EFPIA Code 2026](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf)
- EFPIA's patient remuneration principles ("Working together with patients") are non-binding guidance; EFPIA/IPHA give no fair-market-value guidance for PO collaborations — [EFPIA patient remuneration principles](https://www.efpia.eu/media/413114/workingtogetherwithpatients_patient-remuneration-principles.pdf); [IPHA Code V8.6 page](https://www.iphacode.ie/courses/code-of-practice-for-the-pharmaceutical-industry-v8-6-effective-01-06-2025/)

**Disclosure of ToVs to HCOs/HCPs — timing, availability, retention, format**
- Disclosures must be made within 6 months after the end of the relevant Reporting Period; each Reporting Period covers a full calendar year; the common publication window used in company methodological notes is 20–30 June — [EFPIA Disclosure Code 2014](https://www.efpia.eu/media/25837/efpia-disclosure-code.pdf); [Biogen methodology note](https://transparency.biogen.com/content/dam/corporate-transparency/pdfs/Estonia/2023/Methodology-Note_2023-ToV-Estonia.pdf)
- Disclosed information must remain in the public domain for a minimum of 3 years unless a shorter period is required by national law or the data-protection legal basis lapses; each company must document all ToV disclosures for a minimum of 5 years after the end of the Reporting Period — [EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf); [EFPIA Module 6 Disclosure](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uoxnhpww/module-6_disclosure-of-transfers-of-value.pdf)
- HCO disclosure categories in the EFPIA template as applied by companies: Donations & Grants to HCOs; Contribution to costs of Events (sponsorship agreements with HCOs/third parties, registration fees, travel & accommodation); Fees for service and consultancy (fees, related expenses); R&D ToVs disclosed in aggregate — [Pierre Fabre EFPIA methodological note 2025](https://www.pierre-fabre.com/sites/pierre-fabre.com/files/2025-06/6-efpia-disclosure-methodological%20note-2025.pdf); [Ipsen Belux methodological note 2025](https://www.ipsen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Belux_2025_Methodological_Note_ENG.pdf)
- The Code requires reports to be available online, downloadable and searchable by Recipient name; the E&CC planned a unified format (downloadable, searchable) and a publication standard mandatory by the 2027 disclosure of 2026 data; standardised terminology and layout for methodological notes was due at the latest for the 2026 disclosure of 2025 data — [EFPIA Code Report 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf); [EFPIA Code 2026](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf)
- EFPIA guidance limits the R&D aggregate-reporting exemption for non-interventional studies to NIS that are prospective in nature — [EFPIA Code Report 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf)
- AI is not yet a Code amendment but is included in the E&CC priorities for 2027 — [EFPIA Code Report 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf)
- Sweden's LIF confirms the EFPIA methodological-note template becomes mandatory from the 2026 publication of 2025 ToVs, and searchability by recipient name/downloadability from the 2027 publication of 2026 ToVs — [LIF: Nytt i LER från 29 september 2025](https://www.lif.se/globalassets/etik/dokument/ler/nytt-i-lakemedelsbranschens-etiska-regelverk.pdf)

**Data protection basis for disclosure (EFPIA guidance)**
- GDPR offers consent, legitimate interest and legal obligation as bases; legitimate interest requires a balancing test weighing public transparency interest against HCP privacy — [EFPIA Disclosure Q&A 2019](https://www.efpia.eu/media/580613/disclosure-qa.pdf)
- Spain's data-protection authority accepted individual disclosure on legitimate-interest grounds — [EFPIA blog: 7th publication](https://www.efpia.eu/news-events/the-efpia-view/blog-articles/the-efpia-disclosure-where-are-we-for-the-7th-publication/)
- Where consent is the basis, withdrawal obliges removal of the individual's data from public view and inclusion in the aggregate — [EFPIA Q&A 2016](https://www.efpia.eu/media/15481/efpia-disclosure-code-your-questions-answered-march-2016.pdf)
- Ipsen's 2026 guidance groups countries into "legitimate interest: no consent needed" vs "consent-based: consent checkbox is a condition of signing the agreement", with Poland in the "no consent = aggregate disclosure" group — [Ipsen Expert Consent for Disclosure (May 2026)](https://www.ipsen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Expert-Consent-for-Disclosure-of-Transfer-of-Value-ENG.pdf)
- Bayer UK moved from written consent to legitimate interest from 1 January 2023 (named disclosure by default, no opt-out) — [Bayer UK EFPIA disclosure](https://www.bayer.co.uk/en/what-the-efpia-disclosure-code-is-about); in some countries consent from HCOs is also obtained — [Bayer facts on disclosure](https://www.bayer.com/en/sustainability/facts-on-disclosure)

### Inferences (platform requirements)
- Grant/donation records must capture the three Section 12.01 conditions as structured fields: purpose category (healthcare / research / education), documentation attached, and a non-inducement attestation; recipient type must be validated as HCO or PO and the system must hard-block any individual (natural person) as recipient of a donation/grant.
- PO funding records must generate/hold a written agreement with amount, purpose, description of indirect and non-financial support, and feed an annual PO public list (name, monetary value, description of non-monetary benefit, total contracted-services fees) in EFPIA's mandatory PO template from the 2026 publication cycle.
- Disclosure module must produce the EFPIA HCO template categories (Donations & Grants; Contribution to Costs of Events split into sponsorship agreements / registration fees / travel & accommodation; Fees for service; R&D aggregate), by calendar year, publishable by 30 June, downloadable and searchable by recipient name (mandatory from the 2027 cycle), with a standardised methodological note; data must stay public ≥3 years and records retained ≥5 years, with per-country overrides.
- Indirect ToVs (e.g., event support routed via a PCO or HCO that benefits named HCPs) must be traceable to the ultimate beneficiary for individual reporting.
- A per-country configurable lawful-basis setting (legitimate interest vs consent) with consent capture/withdrawal workflow and automatic aggregation on withdrawal is needed.

### Gaps
- The exact wording/section numbers of the 2026 edition of the EFPIA Code (e.g., whether Donations & Grants remains Section 12, and the precise new PO-template fields) could not be read directly because efpia.eu was blocked; the report writer should treat section numbers as those of the 2019/2023 consolidated text.
- No source found describing an EFPIA-wide central disclosure platform; the 2026 Code and Code Report describe a unified *format* and publication standard, not a single European database. If "central disclosure platform" refers to the national central databases (Disclosure UK, transferofvalue.ie, Transparantieregister Zorg, betransparent.be, LIF samarbetsdatabas), see country sections.

## 2. UK — ABPI Code of Practice (2024 edition), Disclosure UK, PMCPA cases, NHS sponsorship rules, Bribery Act/ECCTA, Charity Commission

### Takeaway
The 2024 ABPI Code (in effect 1 October 2024; fully in force 1 January 2025) is the current UK code; no 2026 Code amendment was found, though the PMCPA opened a consultation on its Constitution & Procedure on 3 August 2026 (new C&P planned early 2027) and expects industry engagement on a Code update "later in 2026". Clause 23 requires donations/grants to organisations only (never individuals), for healthcare/research/education, under a certified written agreement (Clause 8.3), not bearing a medicine's name, and publicly disclosed annually (Clause 28 for HCOs, Clause 29 for POs). PMCPA cases in 2025 (Moderna voluntary admission; Roche; Jazz; Organon) all turned on Clause 23.2 written-agreement/certification failures. NHS England's 2017 conflicts-of-interest guidance governs acceptance of sponsorship on the recipient side; the Charity Commission's March 2024 guidance governs charities' acceptance of donations.

### Cited Findings

**Code status and timeline**
- The 2024 ABPI Code came into effect on 1 October 2024, replacing the 2021 Code, and came into full force on 1 January 2025 — [ABPI news, Sept 2024](https://www.abpi.org.uk/media/news/2024/september/2024-abpi-code-of-practice-launched-with-a-new-constitution-and-procedure-for-the-pmcpa/); [Inside EU Life Sciences](https://www.insideeulifesciences.com/2024/10/01/new-abpi-code-and-pmcpa-constitution-and-procedure-published/); full text — [2024 ABPI Code PDF](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/media/r0anf5ya/2024-abpi-code.pdf)
- On 3 August 2026 the PMCPA launched a public consultation on targeted amendments to its Constitution and Procedure, intending to implement a new C&P in early 2027 subject to ABPI General Meeting approval; this is distinct from any Code update, on which the PMCPA expects to begin industry engagement later in 2026 — [PMCPA consultation news](https://pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news/pmcpa-consults-on-changes-to-its-constitution-and-procedure)
- No source found announcing 2026 amendments to Clauses 23, 27, 28 or 29 — (search of abpi.org.uk / pmcpa.org.uk, see [PMCPA Clause 23 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-23-donations-and-grants/))

**Clause 23 Donations and Grants (2024 Code)**
- Donations and grants must support healthcare, scientific research or education; must not induce prescribing; must be prospective; must not bear a medicine's name (exception: independently produced textbook/journal that mentions medicines); goods/services may bear a corporate name; they must not be provided to individuals — [PMCPA Clause 23 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-23-donations-and-grants/); [Lexology summary of the Code](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8641b373-fb11-4fdf-b4b6-8c5f0336e26d)
- The written agreement must be certified as set out in Clause 8.3; Clause 8 lists "the written agreement for donations and grants, including where relevant internal company and service provider instructions as described in Clause 23 and its supplementary information" among items requiring certification — [PMCPA Clause 8 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-8-certification-and-examination/); [PMCPA Clause 23 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-23-donations-and-grants/)
- 2021 Code wording of 23.2 (carried into 2024): the written agreement should include a description of the donation/grant and its objective, how it will support healthcare, scientific research or education, and the names of the organisations or parties involved — [PMCPA Clause 23 (2021)](https://pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/previous-abpi-codes-of-practice/2021-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-23-donations-and-grants/)
- "Donations, grants and sponsorships (including in relation to events/meetings) must be publicly disclosed annually as set out in Clause 29" (POs), and donations and grants to HCOs/institutions/other organisations are ToVs under Clause 28.2 — [PMCPA Clause 23 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-23-donations-and-grants/); [PMCPA Clause 28 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-28-annual-disclosure-of-transfers-of-value-to-health-professionals-other-relevant-decision-makers-and-healthcare-organisations/)
- The 2021 Code replaced the former "MEGS" (medical and educational goods and services) with the donations-and-grants clause and extended it to POs — [Lexology: New Code of Practice](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8641b373-fb11-4fdf-b4b6-8c5f0336e26d)

**Patient organisations (Clause 27) and PO disclosure**
- Clause 27.2 governs the written agreement for PO donations/grants/sponsorship; Clause 27.3 requires companies to make their involvement clear and to ensure arrangements comply with the ban on advertising prescription-only medicines to the public — [ABPI/PIF handbook 2025](https://www.abpi.org.uk/media/zafndfa3/pif_abpi_handbook_2025_final_16-may.pdf); [PMCPA Clause 27 (2024)](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-27-relationships-with-patient-organisations/)
- Under the ABPI Code, companies must publish annually a list of POs with the monetary value and description of each interaction; reports cover a full calendar year and are published by end of June of the following year — [ScienceDirect: Evaluating transparency of pharma payments to POs in the UK](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168851019301915)
- Example of 2026 cycle: Novo Nordisk UK's 2025 PO disclosure report lists first publication 30 June 2026 — [Novo Nordisk UK PO report 2025](https://www.novonordisk.co.uk/content/dam/nncorp/gb/en/pdfs/efpia/Patient-Organisations-Disclosure-Report-V2-2025.pdf)
- May 2025: ABPI issued best-practice guidance encouraging POs to disclose industry funding, noting there is no Code requirement on POs themselves — [ABPI best practice guidance](https://www.abpi.org.uk/publications/best-practice-guidance-supporting-patient-organisations-to-report-industry-funding/); [Freshfields commentary](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/uks-abpi-publishes-updated-industry-guidance-on-working-with-patients-and-patien-102hzpu)
- A UK study found only 47.1% (200 of 425) of listed recipient POs reported the payments themselves; ten companies provided 67% of funding — [ScienceDirect UK PO study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168851019301915)

**PMCPA cases 2025 on grants/donations**
- CASE/0646/06/25 Voluntary admission by Moderna: breaches of Clause 8.3 (certification of donations/grants), Clause 23.2 (grant requirements) and Clause 5.1 (high standards) of the 2024 Code regarding approval, oversight and failure to certify eight payments (grants aligned with UK government-led research); outcome: breach of 5.1, and of 8.3 and 23.2 on five occasions, no breach on three; completed 7 October 2025, undertaking given — [PMCPA CASE/0646/06/25](https://pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/case06460625-voluntary-admission-by-moderna/); [case report PDF](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/media/gbme5xxr/0646-case-report-14-november-25.pdf)
- AUTH/3851/11/23 Complainant v Roche (2021 Code), completed 10 February 2025: breach of Clause 23.2 for failing to include in the grant written agreement a statement that all parties understand the grant must be clearly acknowledged — [PMCPA AUTH/3851/11/23](https://pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/auth38511123-complainant-v-roche/)
- Jazz Pharmaceuticals (AUTH/3907/05/24) and Organon (CASE/0219/06/24), completed mid-2025, are indexed with breaches of Clauses 23.2 and 25.3 (snippet fragmentary) — [PMCPA news index](https://pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news?page=14)
- PMCPA press releases on Clause 2 breaches (17 Feb 2025; 27 May 2025) — [PMCPA press release 27 May 2025](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news/pmcpa-press-release-27-may-2025-case-ruled-in-breach-of-clause-2/); [17 Feb 2025](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news/pmcpa-press-release-17-february-2025-cases-ruled-in-breach-of-clause-2-published-by-pmcpa-between-4-december-2024-and-3-february-2025/)

**NHS recipient-side rules**
- NHS England "Managing conflicts of interest in the NHS: guidance for staff and organisations" came into force 1 June 2017 — [NHS England Q&A for medical staff](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/coi-qa-medical-staff.pdf); main page — [NHS England guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/managing-conflicts-of-interest-in-the-nhs/)
- Event sponsorship should only be approved where it delivers clear benefit to the organisation and NHS; no breach of patient confidentiality/data protection; sponsors must not have dominant influence over content; sponsor involvement must be clearly identified; sponsorship does not equal endorsement; local policy may require senior approval before accepting — [NHS England guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/managing-conflicts-of-interest-in-the-nhs/); [Q&A for medical staff](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/coi-qa-medical-staff.pdf)
- Sponsored posts require written confirmation that the arrangement will not affect purchasing/prescribing decisions, audited for the duration; written agreements should set out exit terms — [NHS England Standards of Business Conduct v2.3](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/standard-of-business-conduct-v2.3.pdf); [UHB Trust policy (reproducing NHSE text), issued 05/11/2025](https://www.uhb.nhs.uk/media/aksihcvn/hospitalitygiftspolicy.pdf)
- Industry-funded research must not act as an inducement to prescribe or buy a medicine or device — [NHS England guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/managing-conflicts-of-interest-in-the-nhs/)

**Bribery Act 2010 / ECCTA 2023**
- The ECCTA "failure to prevent fraud" offence took effect 1 September 2025 (s.199 ECCTA 2023), modelled on s.7 Bribery Act 2010, with a "reasonable procedures" defence equivalent to Bribery Act "adequate procedures"; applies to large organisations meeting two of: >250 employees, >£36m turnover, >£18m assets; unlimited fine — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/the-economic-crime-and-corporate-transparency-act-and-the-failure-to-prevent-fraud/); [Transparency International UK](https://www.transparency.org.uk/news/failure-prevent-fraud-here-strengthen-anti-corruption-and-anti-fraud-controls-effective); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/criminal-law/the-uk's-new-failure-to-prevent-fraud-offence-global-organisations-take-note)

**Charity Commission (recipient charities)**
- Charity Commission guidance on accepting, refusing or returning donations was published 4 March 2024: starting point is to accept donations; trustees may refuse where refusal is in the charity's interests; some donations must be refused (illegal source/conditions); trustees must weigh risks of accepting vs refusing and must not let personal views drive decisions; renegotiating restrictions with donors is encouraged — [UK Fundraising](https://fundraising.co.uk/2024/03/04/commission-issues-new-guidance-on-accepting-refusing-or-returning-donations/); [Civil Society](https://www.civilsociety.co.uk/news/your-starting-point-is-to-accept-donations-commission-tells-charities-in-new-guidance.html); [Mills & Reeve](https://www.mills-reeve.com/blogs/charity/november-2024/new-ethical-guidance-on-accepting-and-refusing-donations/)

### Inferences (platform requirements, UK)
- UK workflow must enforce: recipient = organisation (block individuals); purpose category; prospective-only (block retrospective funding); product-name-free (name check on grant title/materials); a written agreement containing description/objective, how it supports healthcare/research/education, parties, and an acknowledgment statement (the Roche ruling); a Clause 8.3 certification step by a nominated signatory with audit trail (the Moderna ruling shows that missing certification is itself a breach).
- Disclosure: feed Disclosure UK (HCO ToVs, Clause 28) and the company's annual PO list (Clause 29) by 30 June for the prior calendar year.
- Recipient-side evidence: capture NHS trust approval (senior sign-off), sponsorship acknowledgement wording, and written confirmation of no influence on purchasing/prescribing for sponsored posts; capture charity-trustee acceptance decision for charity recipients.
- Anti-bribery controls (risk assessment, due diligence, approval thresholds, monitoring) map to Bribery Act adequate-procedures principles and ECCTA reasonable-procedures.

## 3. Germany — FSA Codes (Fachkreise, Transparenz, Patientenorganisationen), AKG, criminal law

### Takeaway
German industry rules are the FSA-Kodex Fachkreise (2024 edition), FSA-Transparenzkodex and FSA-Kodex Patientenorganisationen (December 2024 edition), binding on FSA members; PO donations/sponsorship/memberships are published annually between 20 and 30 June (≤6 months after the reporting year), public ≥3 years, documentation kept ≥5 years. The 2025 publication (2024 data) showed ~EUR 768.7m to HCPs/HCOs and ~EUR 9.9m to POs. AKG (the second German code body) and the §299a/b StGB anti-corruption provisions could not be researched in this session (search budget exhausted).

### Cited Findings

## 4. France — Loi anti-cadeaux (DMOS reformed by Ordonnance 2017-84, Décret 2020-730, arrêtés of 7 August 2020), EPS/IDAHE declaration platforms, Transparence Santé

### Takeaway
Since 1 October 2020, any benefit (including donations for research, hospitality, remuneration) from a company to a health professional, student, association of HCPs or other covered person/entity must be governed by a written convention transmitted to the competent Ordre (or ARS where no Ordre exists), and is subject either to prior declaration (below the thresholds of the arrêté of 7 August 2020) or prior authorisation (at or above). Thresholds reported: research donations EUR 5,000 per convention (declaration if below), association donations EUR 1,000 (EUR 10,000 for associations reconnues d'utilité publique); a second arrêté sets "negligible value" amounts (e.g., impromptu meals ≤EUR 30, twice a year). Declarations go through IDAHE 2 (Ordre des médecins) or EPS (other Ordres/ARS). ## 15. EU-level law — Directive 2001/83/EC art. 94, EU pharma package (2023–2026), GDPR for a grants system, EU AI Act timing

### Takeaway
Article 94 of Directive 2001/83/EC prohibits gifts/pecuniary advantages/benefits in kind to persons qualified to prescribe or supply unless inexpensive and relevant to practice (threshold set nationally). ## 17. Enforcement and rulings 2025–2026 (UK PMCPA, Germany FSA, France)

### Takeaway
UK PMCPA rulings in 2025 consistently penalised process failures in grants (missing certification, incomplete written agreements) rather than the grants' substance: Moderna (voluntary admission, Clause 8.3/23.2/5.1), Roche (23.2 acknowledgement statement), Jazz and Organon (23.2/25.3). No FSA (Germany) or French sanction cases on grants/donations were found in this session.
