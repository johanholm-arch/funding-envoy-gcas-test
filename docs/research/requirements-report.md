# Encode Forty Jurisdictions Into One Funding Platform

**Bottom line.** A multi-tenant, AI-assisted external-funding platform for pharma and medtech can be built on one global core rule-set that is strikingly consistent across the United States, Europe and the rest of the world — fund organisations, never individuals; keep decisions structurally separate from sales; apply objective, stored criteria; execute a written agreement before the activity; never link support to purchasing, prescribing or formulary status; acknowledge support publicly; and disclose annually — and then parameterise roughly forty jurisdiction-specific overlays (thresholds, prior-approval steps, deadlines, retention periods, lawful bases). In the US the operative baseline is still the 2003 OIG Compliance Program Guidance plus the 2014 patient-assistance bulletin, Open Payments with 2026 thresholds of **$13.82 per transfer and $138.13 aggregate**, seven state regimes with distinct clocks (Vermont April 1, Massachusetts July 1 at **$50 per occurrence**, Nevada May 1–June 1, Connecticut July 1) and an FCPA regime that restarted enforcement through DOJ's June 2025 Guidelines and a March 2026 department-wide Corporate Enforcement Policy. Industry codes (PhRMA 2022, AdvaMed June 2023, ACCME Standards in force since 1 January 2022 and now adopted by nursing, pharmacy and, from June 2026, dentistry) converge on one "accredited CE" rule set. Europe adds EFPIA disclosure within six months of year-end (public for three years, records for five, mandatory patient-organisation template from the 2026 cycle, searchable-by-recipient publication from 2027) and national prior-approval regimes — French anti-cadeaux declarations and authorisations, Belgian Mdeon visas, MedTech Europe's 50-day Conference Vetting System — while 2025–2026 UK PMCPA rulings show that missing certification or a missing acknowledgement clause, not the grant itself, is what gets companies sanctioned. Enforcement history (more than **$1 billion in copay-charity settlements**, Teva's **$425 million** in October 2024, Olympus's **$646 million** device case) maps to four control failures the platform must hard-stop: sales-initiated or purchase-conditioned grants, product-earmarked charity funds, utilisation data flowing back from charities, and undocumented or mis-booked payments. The vendor market is small and consolidating (MediSpend–RLDatix, IQVIA, SteepRock, Envision, a zero-customer "Early" Veeva Grants), no pharma grants module advertises AI, and large sponsors are re-platforming in 2026, so a new entrant's differentiators are a shared requestor experience, auditable AI triage and managed operations. AI must be designed to the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties (live since 2 August 2026, with Annex III high-risk duties deferred to 2 December 2027) and DOJ's September 2024 ECCP question about "false approvals generated by AI": every AI action is a logged, human-reviewed, reversible suggestion. One caveat governs the whole report: all six research notes were built from search-result extracts because primary sites were blocked, so the final section lists every item that still needs primary-source verification.

## US federal law makes sales separation and objective criteria the core design constraint

The federal Anti-Kickback Statute, as interpreted in OIG's **May 5, 2003 Compliance Program Guidance for Pharmaceutical Manufacturers**, remains the operative industry-specific guidance for grants in 2026: it identifies educational grants as a risk area, states that funding conditioned in whole or in part on product purchases implicates the statute even where the educational or research purpose is legitimate, and advises manufacturers to separate grant-making from sales and marketing and to use objective criteria that ignore the volume or value of a recipient's purchases ([OIG CPG PDF](https://www.oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/799/050503FRCPGPharmac.pdf); [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/05/05/03-10949/oig-compliance-program-guidance-for-pharmaceutical-manufacturers)). OIG's **General Compliance Program Guidance of 6 November 2023** supplements rather than replaces the 2003 document and creates no new legal obligations, while raising expectations of compliance-officer independence and resourcing at pharma and device companies ([OIG GCPG PDF](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/compliance-guidance/1135/HHS-OIG-GCPG-2023.pdf); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/compliance-program-guidance-what-compliance-officers-at-pharmaceutical-and-medical-device-companies-need-to-consider)). OIG said industry-segment-specific guidance would begin in 2024, but no pharmaceutical or device ICPG surfaced in any 2024–2026 result ([Akin Gump 2024 Guide](https://www.akingump.com/a/web/5vpTEWSojTA926xwieF4be/8TuVLc/akin-2024-guide-to-doj-and-hhs-oig.pdf); [OIG Compliance Guidance page](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/compliance-guidance/)). The 2009 OIG testimony to the Senate makes the rationale explicit: separating grant-making from sales and marketing "helps ensure grant funding is not influenced by sales or marketing motivations" ([OIG testimony, 29 July 2009](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/testimony/157/20090729_-_Morris.pdf)).

Charitable patient-assistance donations carry their own body of guidance. OIG's 2005 Special Advisory Bulletin and the **May 2014 Supplemental Special Advisory Bulletin on Independent Charity Patient Assistance Programs** (79 FR 31120) focus on disease-fund design, eligible recipients and donor conduct: an independent charity must not function as a conduit for manufacturer payments to patients and must not impermissibly influence beneficiaries' drug choices, regardless of which federal programme covers the drug ([Federal Register 2014 bulletin](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2014/05/30/2014-11769/supplemental-special-advisory-bulletin-independent-charity-patient-assistance-programs); [OIG bulletin PDF](https://www.oig.hhs.gov/documents/special-advisory-bulletins/879/independent-charity-bulletin.pdf)). Two live advisory opinions set the current boundary: **AO 24-02**, addressing PAPs independently managed by a 501(c)(3) but funded primarily by manufacturers, is in effect only until **1 January 2027** ([Morgan Lewis, Feb 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/asprescribed/2025/02/patient-assistance-programs-the-dos-and-donts-from-hhs-oig)), and **AO 26-17** (2026) found an independent-charity PAP low-risk because assistance is awarded first-come, first-served under a reasonable, verifiable and uniformly applied financial-need policy, with OIG reserving the right to revisit in two years ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/hhs-oig-issues-favorable-advisory-opinion-regarding-independent-charity-patient); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=516450d1-dd80-455d-904a-e58a551ddafe)). The 2017–2021 settlement series shows what happens when those factors are ignored: United Therapeutics **$210 million** (December 2017), Actelion **$360 million** (December 2018), Astellas **$100 million** and Amgen **$24.75 million** (2019), Gilead **$97 million** (2020), Biogen **$22 million**, Incyte **$12.6 million** and Pfizer about **$24 million** ([FiercePharma, United Therapeutics](https://www.fiercepharma.com/legal/united-therapeutics-inks-210m-settlement-over-charity-donations); [CHEManager, Actelion](https://chemanager-online.com/en/node/408942); [FiercePharma, Astellas/Amgen](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/astellas-amgen-fork-over-125m-doj-s-latest-charity-kickback-settlements); [FiercePharma, Gilead](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/gilead-inks-97m-charity-kickbacks-settlement-feds-but-argues-it-did-no-wrong); [FiercePharma, Biogen](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/biogen-inks-22m-charity-kickbacks-deal-feds-joining-long-list-drugmakers-involved); [KFF Health News, Pfizer](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/pfizer-settles-kickback-case-related-to-copay-assistance-for-24m/)). The wave has not ended: Teva paid **$425 million** in October 2024 over Copaxone copays routed through two foundations that received more than $300 million between 2006 and 2017, and Dompé self-disclosed and paid **$32 million** without a corporate integrity agreement ([STAT, 10 Oct 2024](https://www.statnews.com/pharmalot/2024/10/10/teva-antitrust-kickback-charities-medicare/); [DOJ, Dompé](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/dompe-us-agrees-pay-32m-resolve-false-claims-act-liability-relating-self-disclosure-patient)). Speaker programs are a parallel risk channel: OIG's November 2020 Special Fraud Alert flags minimal educational content, lavish venues, alcohol, repeat programmes and compensation tied to prescription volume ([OIG Special Fraud Alert PDF](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/special-fraud-alerts/865/SpecialFraudAlertSpeakerPrograms.pdf)), and Gilead's **$202 million** April 2025 settlement alleged monitors failed to report exceeded meal limits and inappropriate venues ([Mintz, 14 May 2025](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2146/2025-05-14-speaker-programs-two-recent-enforcement-actions-serve)).

**What this means for the platform.** Role separation must be enforced by the permission model, not policy: sales and marketing roles cannot create, score, approve or see the status of grant, donation or PAP requests (at most an "awareness" view after decision). Scoring criteria must be stored as structured, versioned fields with no input capturing the applicant's purchase volume, formulary position or prescribing. Every request carries a requestor attestation and a reviewer confirmation that support is not conditioned on purchase, formulary status or referrals. For charity PAP donations the data model needs disease-fund name and breadth, a charity-independence check, a hard block on product earmarking, a prohibition on receiving patient-level or product-share data from the charity, a documented budget rationale independent of the company's own product forecasts, the advisory opinion relied upon and its expiry date (AO 24-02 lapses 1 January 2027; AO 26-17 is revisited in 2028), and routing to a non-commercial owner. Speaker programmes are a separate promotional channel: a request that looks like one (company-selected faculty, meals, venue) must be rejected from the grants channel, and any education-adjacent spend should retain meal-cap and venue data for audit.

### FCPA treats foreign public hospitals, universities and officials' foundations as high-risk payees

Enforcement examples where grants, donations or sponsorships were the vehicle follow one fact pattern. Schering-Plough's Polish subsidiary paid about **$76,000** to a foundation headed by the director of a regional health fund, with none of the payments accurately recorded ([SEC admin order 34-49838](https://sec.gov/litigation/admin/34-49838)); Eli Lilly's Polish subsidiary paid **$39,000** to a foundation started by the head of a regional health authority in exchange for business, part of a **$29 million** 2012 settlement ([SEC speech, 3 March 2015](https://www.sec.gov/news/speech/2015-spch030315ajc.html)); a Stryker subsidiary's purported **~$200,000 "donation"** to a Greek public university funded a lab in exchange for a doctor's commitment to provide business ([SEC speech 2015](https://www.sec.gov/news/speech/2015-spch030315ajc.html)); Novartis paid **~$345.9 million** in June 2020 over Greek hospital bribery, including paying for physicians to attend congresses with a warning that support would be withdrawn if prescription quotas were not met, plus Vietnamese record-keeping failures ([FiercePharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/novartis-inks-settlements-worth-347m-doj-sec-over-foreign-doctor-payments-records-keeping); [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/news/foreign-bribery-by-the-pharmaceutical-industry/)); Alexion paid **>$21 million** in July 2020 for books-and-records and internal-controls failures in Turkey, Russia, Brazil and Colombia ([Miller & Chevalier Summer Review 2020](https://www.millerchevalier.com/publication/fcpa-summer-review-2020)); Teva paid **$519 million** in 2016 and Fresenius **$231 million** in 2019 ([Phillips & Cohen](https://www.phillipsandcohen.com/whistleblower-resources/largest-fcpa-settlements/)). Alstom's 2014 case established that more than **$2 million** paid to a bona fide charity associated with a foreign official was still a bribe ([Willkie, Sept 2016](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/insights-2016-20160923-recent-fcpa-enforcement-action/)). After the 2025 enforcement pause, DOJ's **June 2025 FCPA Guidelines** restarted enforcement with four focus areas (cartels, conduct harming US companies, national security, serious misconduct) and signalled declination for matters lacking a substantial US nexus or involving de minimis courtesies ([Dorsey](https://www.dorsey.com/insights/new-doj-guidelines-mark-the-end-of-the-fcpa-enforcement-pause); [Alston & Bird, Dec 2025](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/doj-aci-fcpa-enforcement-2026)); the **March 2026 department-wide Corporate Enforcement Policy** created three outcome tiers starting with declination for self-disclosure, cooperation and timely remediation, and its first declination went to device maker **Balt SAS**, which bribed a physician at a state-owned French hospital and disgorged about $1.2 million ([Arnold & Porter, Summer 2026](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2026/07/global-anti-corruption-insights-summer-2026)). HCPs at state hospitals are treated as foreign officials and "anything of value" expressly includes donations, scholarships and travel ([FCPA Professor](https://fcpaprofessor.com/healthcare-professionals-foreign-officials-2/); [Baron & Budd](https://www.becomeawhistleblower.com/foreign-corrupt-practices-act/pharmaceutical/)).

**What this means for the platform.** Every non-US recipient record needs a government-ownership/control flag, an officer and beneficial-owner list screened for public officials (PEP screening), and a payee-bank-account ownership match. Requests flagged as public-institution or official-linked route to an enhanced-due-diligence tier requiring a business-purpose memo, a "no pending tender or purchasing decision" declaration, a check that the request did not originate from an official, and post-award use-of-funds monitoring. The general-ledger category must be accurate (Alexion and Novartis were books-and-records cases), and the audit trail must be able to reconstruct who approved what, when and against which criteria, because that reconstruction is the evidence base for "timely remediation" credit under the 2026 policy.

## Open Payments and seven state regimes dictate the US payee data model and reporting calendar

Open Payments (42 CFR Part 403 Subpart I) requires applicable manufacturers and GPOs to report payments or transfers of value to physicians, teaching hospitals and, since **1 January 2021**, non-physician practitioners (PAs, NPs, clinical nurse specialists, CRNAs, anesthesiologist assistants, certified nurse midwives) ([CMS Program Participants](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/program-participants); [CMS FY2021 Report to Congress](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/open-payments-fy-2021-annual-report-congress.pdf)). For the 2026 collection year CMS lists a small-payment threshold of **$13.82** and an annual aggregate of **$138.13**, adjusted annually by CPI; payments under the per-transfer minimum are excluded unless the aggregate is exceeded, in which case everything must be reported ([CMS Data Collection](https://www.cms.gov/openpayments/program-participants/reporting-entities/data-collection)). Grants and charitable contributions to covered recipients are reportable natures of payment ([CMS natures of payment](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/natures-payment.pdf)), and the scale is large: PY2025 general payments were about **$3.92 billion** across **1,327 teaching hospitals**, with research payments about $9.50 billion ([PALTmed on PY2025](https://paltmed.org/news-media/cms-publishes-program-year-2025-open-payments-data); [CMS Open Payments Data overview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/data)). The decisive rule for educational grants is the indirect-payment test: a payment is indirect only where the manufacturer "requires, instructs, directs or otherwise causes" a third party to pay a covered recipient; CMS reasoned in the CY2015 PFS rule that funding an accredited CME provider is not reportable if the manufacturer does not direct the provider to pay a specific physician, and later learning the physician's identity does not make it reportable ([CMS Open Payments General FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/OpenPayments/Downloads/open-payments-general-faq.pdf); [CMS Nov 2014 revision document](https://www.cms.gov/openpayments/downloads/open-payments-revision-nov-2014.pdf); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/continuing-medical-education-cme-payments-largely-remain-excluded-sunshine-act-repor)). Records for physicians and NPPs must carry the NPPES name, NPI and at least one state licence with state; teaching hospitals are those receiving Medicare GME or IME payments per CMS's annual list; research payments made under a written agreement or protocol follow § 403.904(f) and can be flagged for delayed publication under § 403.910 ([42 CFR 403.904](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/403.904); [eCFR Part 403 Subpart I](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-A/part-403/subpart-I); [CMS Data Dictionary Methodology](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/methodology-data-dictionary-document.pdf)). A research payment to a university is reportable if it has at least one physician principal investigator ([CMS FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/OpenPayments/Downloads/open-payments-general-faq.pdf)). CMS publishes the prior year's data by **June 30** and refreshes each January; late records are excluded from initial publication ([CMS Open Payments Data](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments/data)). The data dictionary's OMB approval expires **31 July 2026**, implying a Paperwork Reduction Act renewal that could alter data elements for PY2026/2027 ([CMS Data Dictionary](https://www.cms.gov/openpayments/downloads/openpaymentsdatadictionary.pdf)); the CY2026 PFS final rule surfaced no Open Payments changes ([Federal Register, CY2026 PFS](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/05/2025-19787/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-cy-2026-payment-policies-under-the-physician-fee-schedule-and-other)). Inaccurate reporting is itself actionable: Medtronic's 2020 **$9.2 million** settlement over payments to a South Dakota neurosurgeon was the first DOJ settlement with a Sunshine Act component ([DOJ release](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/medtronic-pay-over-92-million-settle-allegations-improper-payments-south-dakota-neurosurgeon); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2020/10/10_30-novel-doj-settlement)).

State law adds seven distinct clocks. **Vermont** (18 V.S.A. § 4632) requires manufacturers to file by **April 1** the value, nature, purpose and recipient of allowable expenditures and gifts for the prior calendar year, excluding royalties, ordinary-course rebates and clinical-trial payments (disclosed after FDA approval or four years after payment), with a separate sample-disclosure subsection; the AG published its RY2025 guide on 6 October 2025, and a 2025 journal table lists a $25 threshold and fines up to $10,000 ([18 V.S.A. § 4632](https://law.justia.com/codes/vermont/title-18/chapter-91/section-4632/); [Vermont AG disclosures page](https://ago.vermont.gov/disclosures-manufacturers-prescription-drugs-biological-products-medical-devices); [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/health-services/articles/10.3389/frhs.2025.1666949/full)). **Massachusetts** (105 CMR 970.009) requires disclosure by **July 1** of payments of **$50 or more per occurrence** to covered recipients — which include hospitals and nursing homes as entities that decide which products are purchased — in connection with sales and marketing, and prohibits structuring payments to circumvent reporting ([105 CMR 970.009](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/105-CMR-970-009); [Mass.gov code requirements](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/pharmaceutical-code-of-conduct-requirements)); a May 2026 vendor alert states that payments already reported federally are exempt from separate state disclosure, which is a secondary claim ([QPharma alert, 26 May 2026](https://qpharmacorp.com/regulatory-alerts/may-26-2026-state-of-massachusetts-regulatory-alert/)). **Minnesota** (§ 151.461) bans gifts to practitioners but permits payments to a conference or educational-programme sponsor provided they are not paid directly to a practitioner and are used solely for bona fide educational purposes ([Minn. Stat. § 151.461](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2020/cite/151.461)). **California** (H&S Code § 119402) requires a public Comprehensive Compliance Program following the OIG 2003 guidance and the PhRMA Code, a self-set annual dollar cap per California HCP (company declarations show **$2,000–$5,000**, with independent education funding, scholarships and FMV services typically excluded), an annual written declaration posted with a toll-free number, and programme updates within six months of any OIG revision ([Cal. H&S Code § 119402](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_health_and_safety_code_section_119402); [Krystal Bio declaration](https://www.krystalbio.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Annual-Declaration-of-Compliance-for-Purposes-of-California-Health-Safety-Codes-.pdf); [Lenz Therapeutics](https://lenz-tx.com/california-compliance/)). **Connecticut** (§ 21a-70e) requires a code of conduct meeting PhRMA or AdvaMed, an OIG-aligned compliance programme, annual certification to the Department of Consumer Protection by **July 1**, a named compliance officer and penalties up to $5,000 per violation; 2023 HB 6669 added registration for manufacturers employing sales reps, effective 1 October 2023 with reports from 1 July 2024 ([Conn. Gen. Stat. § 21a-70e](https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-21a/chapter-417/section-21a-70e); [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/new-connecticut-rules-for-drug-makers-employing-sales-reps-take-effect-oct-1)). **Nevada** (NRS 639.570; NAC 639.616) requires an annual marketing-code filing with the Board of Pharmacy received between **May 1 and June 1**, covering the twelve months to April 30, certifying an annual audit; late forms are non-compliant, and the Board published its 2025 compliance report on 15 January 2026 ([Nevada Board of Pharmacy](https://bop.nv.gov/resources/ALL/WholesalersEPP/); [NAC 639.616](https://regulations.justia.com/states/nevada/chapter-639/manufacturers/section-639-616); [2025 compliance report](https://bop.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/bopnvgov/content/Resources/2025%20Marketing%20Code%20of%20Conduct%20compliance%20report-%20final.pdf)). **New Jersey** (N.J.A.C. 13:45J, effective 16 January 2018) caps at **$10,000 per calendar year** the promotional-speaking, advisory-board and consulting compensation a prescriber may receive from all manufacturers, with education events, research, royalties and (after amendment) research-related advisory boards outside the cap and meals capped at $30 ([FDA Law Blog, Jan 2018](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2018/01/new-jersey-finalizes-new-limits-on-pharmaceutical-manufacturer-gifts-and-payments-to-prescribers/); [NJBIA](https://njbia.org/new-jersey-finalizes-prescriber-gift-ban-amendments/)). On the tax side, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised the **Form 1099-NEC/MISC threshold from $600 to $2,000** for payments made after 31 December 2025, indexed from 2027, with backup withholding moving to the same trigger ([CPA Practice Advisor, 30 July 2025](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/); [NATP](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/new-1099-rules-under-obbba-what-tax-pros-need-to-know-for-2026-and-beyond/)).

**What this means for the platform.** The US payee model needs a covered-recipient type (physician / NPP / teaching hospital / non-covered entity); for individuals the NPPES legal name, NPI, primary specialty, state licence and state, and practice address; for institutions an automated match against the CMS teaching-hospital list for the programme year at intake and again at payment, because a grant paid to a teaching hospital is directly reportable. Each payment carries programme year, date, amount, form, nature of payment, product association, third-party and "on behalf of" flags, the delayed-publication flag and research fields (study, PIs, ClinicalTrials.gov ID). For every CME grant the platform captures whether the funder designated or instructed any speaker or attendee, a dated non-direction attestation, and a log of when and how any HCP identities later became known (so the "later learned" defence is evidenced). State logic is a per-transaction rule set keyed on recipient state and beneficiary residency: a Vermont April 1 export with value/nature/purpose/recipient and clinical-trial deferral; a Massachusetts per-occurrence $50 test, hospital/nursing-home covered-recipient flag, a "sales-and-marketing connection" determination and a same-day/same-recipient split detector for anti-structuring review; a Minnesota organisation-only payee check with a bona-fide-education attestation; a California configurable per-HCP annual cap with exclusion categories and a cap-utilisation report for the annual declaration; Connecticut and Nevada certification evidence packs on July 1 and June 1 calendars; and a New Jersey running total of capped compensation per prescriber. Finance needs a W-9/W-8 collection step before first payment, a 1099 exposure computation against the $2,000 threshold for 2026 payments, and a tax-classification field (charitable contribution, business expense, in-kind product donation, research services) owned by Tax. All thresholds, caps and dates should live in versioned regulatory-parameter configuration, never in code, because Open Payments thresholds move with CPI every year and the OMB renewal in July 2026 may change the file layout.

## Industry codes and every US accreditor converge on one "accredited CE" rule set

The **PhRMA Code** (2002; revised 2009, 2019 and August 2021, effective 1 January 2022) requires that CME support go directly to the CME provider and never to individual clinicians, that the company give no advice or guidance to the provider even if asked, that support educate on the full range of treatment options rather than promote a product, and that companies separate CME grant-making from sales and marketing and apply objective criteria; the 2022 revision extended those principles to virtual events and tightened speaker programmes (no alcohol, no repeat attendance, defined selection criteria, FMV pay unrelated to prescribing) ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-effect-january-2022); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/phrma-code-revisions-to-go-into-effect-january-2022/); [UCSF copy of PhRMA Code](https://meded.ucsf.edu/sites/meded.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/PhRMACode.pdf); [McGuireWoods](https://mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/Alerts/2008/7/PhRMA-Code-on-Interactions-with-Healthcare-Professionals); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2021/09/phrma-code-2022-speaker-programs-enter-a-prohibition-era)). No revision after the 2022 edition was found through October 2026 ([PhRMA Code PDF](https://cdn.aglty.io/phrma/global/resources/PhRMA%20Code%20-%20Final.pdf)). The **AdvaMed Code** (Revised and Restated, effective **1 June 2023**, still the baseline referenced by member codes dated November 2025) consolidates educational grants, commercial sponsorships, research grants and charitable donations into Section IV with distinct sales rules per type: "in all cases, sales personnel may not be involved in grantmaking decisions" for educational grants; companies must establish controls so sales cannot control or influence research-grant recipient selection; and for charitable donations sales may give input but cannot control or unduly influence the decision ([AdvaMed Code PDF](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf); [AdvaMed flashcards](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/advamed-code-flashcards.pdf); [Exact Sciences, Nov 2025](https://s22.q4cdn.com/877809405/files/doc_governance/2025/11/exact-sciences-code-of-conduct-on-interactions-with-health-care-professionals-november-2025.pdf)). Grants may go to the third-party organiser or an organiser-designated training institution, never to an individual HCP; organiser-controlled funds may cover modest meals, refreshments and educational items but not gift baskets, entertainment or branded non-educational items; a commercial sponsorship is distinguished by a "commercially reasonable fee" for marketing benefits such as signage or exhibit space; and a satellite symposium is a company-organised, company-funded programme that the organiser does not control ([AdvaMed Code PDF](https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Englsih.pdf); [Cardinal copy of 2023 Code](https://www.cpo2.com/siteassets/us/documents/pdf/2023-advamed-code-of-ethics-202501162.pdf)). Section IV also embeds a reviewer checklist for educational-grant requests ([Cardinal copy](https://www.cpo2.com/siteassets/us/documents/pdf/2023-advamed-code-of-ethics-202501162.pdf)). Both codes are voluntary but incorporated by state law in California, Nevada, Massachusetts and Connecticut ([FDA Law Blog, Jan 2022](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2022/01/revised-phrma-code-took-effect-on-january-1-2022-and-certain-state-obligations-follow/); [Kendall PC](https://kendallpc.com/differences-between-phrma-code-advamed/)).

The **ACCME Standards for Integrity and Independence in Accredited Continuing Education** (released December 2020, mandatory from **1 January 2022**, replacing the Standards for Commercial Support) define the recipient-side rules that every grant agreement must honour. An "ineligible company" is one whose primary business is producing, marketing, selling or distributing healthcare products used by or on patients ([Weill Cornell copy of Standards](https://teach.weill.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/accme_standards_for_integrity_and_independence_in_accredited_continuing_education.pdf); [ACCME Eligibility](https://accme.org/rule/eligibility/)). Standard 3 requires a **24-month look-back** disclosure of all financial relationships with ineligible companies with **no minimum dollar threshold**, and excludes owners and employees of ineligible companies from controlling content ([ACCME Standard 3](https://accme.org/rule/identify-mitigate-and-disclose-relevant-financial-relationships/); [ACCME info package](https://accme.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/881_20220623_Standards-for-Integrity-and-Independence-in-Accredited-CE-Information-Package.pdf)). Standard 4 requires that the provider make all decisions on receipt and disbursement of support; that ineligible companies not pay directly for any education or learner expense; that support may fund planner and faculty honoraria or travel but never individual learners; that the terms and purposes be recorded in a written agreement between company and provider **executed before the education starts** (a joint provider or disbursing third party may be named but cannot sign in the provider's place); that the provider keep a record of the amount or kind of support and how it was used and produce that accounting on request; and that learners be told the supporter's name and the nature of any in-kind support before the activity, without corporate or product logos ([ACCME Standard 4](https://accme.org/rule/manage-commercial-support-appropriately/); [AAFP eligibility requirements](https://www.aafp.org/cme/credit-system/eligibility-requirements.html); [PAMED guidelines, rev. Mar 2025](https://www.pamedsoc.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/cme/accreditation-info-center/additional-resources/cme-planning-resources/guidelines-for-financial-support-and-commercial-promotion-in-conjunction-with-a-cme-activity.pdf?Status=Master&sfvrsn=9f918a8_1)). Standard 5 bars marketing, exhibits and non-accredited education in the educational space **within 30 minutes** before or after an accredited activity and requires non-accredited parts to be clearly labelled ([UCF CPD policy](https://med.ucf.edu/continuous-professional-development/policies/standards-for-integrity-and-independence/)). Providers report each activity's commercial support source and amount in PARS, flagging in-kind support by source without valuing it ([ACCME data reporting FAQ](https://accme.org/data-reporting/data-reporting-quick-answers/); [ACCME PARS tutorial](https://accme.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/936_20211116_CME%20Activity%20Tutorial.pdf)), and ACCME posted a Compliance Check on "Disclosing In-Kind Commercial Support" on **26 August 2026**, signalling an active audit theme ([ACCME Announcements](http://accme.org/news-publications/announcements/)). The same Standards now govern nursing (ANCC), pharmacy (ACPE, compliance by 1 January 2022), family medicine (AAFP), osteopathic medicine (AOA, which bars commercial funds for non-faculty attendees' travel, lodging, registration or honoraria), Joint Accreditation (which also bars sharing learner contact data with ineligible companies without explicit consent) and dentistry, where ADA CERP's Recognition Standards 2026 (adopted 26 September 2024, effective **1 June 2026**, first assessed January 2027) adopt them ([ANCC news release](https://www.nursingworld.org/news/news-releases/2020/ancc-nursing-continuing-professional-development-program-adopts--new-consensus-standards-for-integrity-and-independence/); [ACPE SII Checklist](https://www.acpe-accredit.org/wp-content/uploads/SIIChecklist.pdf); [AOA Guide for Commercial Supporters](https://www.une.edu/sites/default/files/AOA_cme-commercial-sponsors-guide.pdf); [Joint Accreditation FAQ](https://jointaccreditation.org/faq/); [ADA CERP Recognition Standards 2026](https://ccepr.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ccepr/files/2026_cerp_standards.pdf?rev=7d58a49a4ec34d8691a48d4df68019ee&hash=DFFB03C125600ED71AF5F67853E65F45)). CAPCE (EMS) keeps its own manual with a commercial-bias prohibition and medical-director content review rather than the full Standards ([CAPCE 2025 manual](https://capce.org/docs/ManualVersions/2025/CAPCE_Combined_Accreditation_Manual.pdf)). Behind all of this sits FDA's **December 1997 guidance on industry-supported scientific and educational activities**, whose twelve non-dispositive factors (control of content and selection of presenters, disclosure, focus of the programme, provider–supporter relationship, provider involvement in sales or marketing, audience selection and so on) remain the industry template for "independence" even though a 1998–99 court found the guidances unconstitutional as applied to off-label discussion ([Federal Register, 3 Dec 1997](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1997-12-03/pdf/97-31741.pdf); [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/industry-supported-scientific-and-educational-activities); [WLF v. Friedman](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/36/16/2413102/)).

Outcomes conventions are equally settled. Moore, Green and Gallis's 2009 seven-level framework (1 participation, 2 satisfaction, 3A/3B declarative and procedural knowledge, 4 competence, 5 performance, 6 patient health, 7 community health) is the standard, with levels 1–5 provider-level and 6–7 patient- and community-level, and a 2018 Medical Teacher update ([ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227627769_Achieving_Desired_Results_and_Improved_Outcomes_Integrating_Planning_and_Assessment_Throughout_Learning_Activities); [Annals of Family Medicine 2022](https://www.annfammed.org/content/20/Supplement_1/3106); [Moore et al. 2018](https://s9193.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Moore-Chappell-A-conceptual-framework-for-planning-and-assessing-learning-in-cE-activities-designed-for-clinicians-in-one-profession-and-or-clinical-teams.pdf)). Funder application packages converge on a letter of request, needs assessment, objectives, agenda, outcomes plan, budget and W-9 (Daiichi Sankyo), plus a conflict-of-interest policy and by-laws (Boehringer Ingelheim, Merck) ([Daiichi Sankyo](https://daiichisankyo.us/independent-grants); [BIPI tutorial](https://docs.boehringer-ingelheim.com/misc/BIPI-Medical-Education-Grants-Tutorial.pdf); [Merck IME FAQ](https://cdn.versaic.com/merck/b031d12e-17cf-4b71-90d9-7fe0af4e254e)), and Lilly requires an outcomes plan using the Outcomes Standardization Project glossary with expected learner and completer counts, rejecting generic outcomes-model descriptions ([Lilly CGA 2024](https://www.acehp.org/Portals/0/RFPs/9.20.24.globalbrainhealth.CGA.final.pdf?ver=h7Ja03NPg8kPu9ZGP43xUA%3D%3D&timestamp=1726851373380)). No code or accreditor mandates a particular Moore level.

**What this means for the platform.** Request types must be mutually exclusive — educational grant, research grant, charitable donation, commercial sponsorship, membership, company-conducted training, satellite symposium — because each carries a different sales-involvement rule (none for educational grants; input-but-no-control for donations; influence controls for research) and a different payee rule; a satellite symposium or exhibit is routed to a commercial channel with a "company-controlled" flag. The accredited-CE record needs: supporter legal name; activity identifier, title and dates; monetary amount or in-kind flag with a mandatory free-text "nature of in-kind support" (PARS no longer requires it but Standard 4.4 learner disclosure does and ACCME is auditing it); a written agreement with both signatures whose execution date the system checks against the activity start date (hard stop); payee validated as the accredited provider or an organiser-designated institution, never an individual or an ineligible company; line-item budget validation that blocks learner travel, lodging, registration, gift baskets, entertainment and branded non-educational items while allowing modest meals and educational items; a budget-versus-actual ledger that satisfies "produce that accounting on request"; the learner-disclosure text (name only, no logos); and a Standard 5 attestation on 30-minute and spatial separation. Independence attestations mirror the FDA factors and PhRMA/AdvaMed rules: organiser controls content, faculty and venue; no funder input will be sought or accepted; audience not selected by sales; programme not focused on a single product; learner data not shared; other industry support disclosed. Accreditor identity (ACCME, Joint Accreditation, ANCC, ACPE, ADA CERP, AAFP, AOA) is metadata on one shared rule set; CAPCE and non-accredited education (grand rounds without credit, patient education) trigger the full independence attestation set because no accreditor audits the provider. The outcomes module offers Moore levels 1–7 with 3A/3B, a method field (attendance records, evaluation survey, pre/post test, case vignettes, chart audit, registry), OSP-glossary vocabulary, and expected versus actual learner and completer counts. Reviewer forms reproduce the AdvaMed Section IV checklist as structured fields once its verbatim items are confirmed (see gaps).

## Europe layers EFPIA disclosure on top of national prior-approval regimes

The **EFPIA Code of Practice** (consolidated 2019; a 2026 edition is published; Code Report updated 9 July 2026) allows donations and grants to HCOs and patient organisations only if they support healthcare, research or education, are documented and kept on record, and do not constitute an inducement; donations and grants to individuals are not permitted (Sections 12.01–12.02 of the 2019/2023 numbering) ([EFPIA Code 2023](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/230220-EFPIA-Code.pdf); [EFPIA Code 2026 PDF](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf); [EFPIA Module 3](https://www.efpia.eu/media/ioniysjn/module-3-donations-grants.pdf)). Transfers of value to HCOs and HCPs must be disclosed **within six months of the calendar-year end** (in practice 20–30 June), remain public for **at least three years**, and be documented for **at least five years** ([EFPIA Disclosure Code 2014](https://www.efpia.eu/media/25837/efpia-disclosure-code.pdf); [EFPIA Module 6](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uoxnhpww/module-6_disclosure-of-transfers-of-value.pdf)). The HCO template categories as applied by companies are donations and grants; contribution to costs of events (sponsorship agreements, registration fees, travel and accommodation); fees for service and consultancy; and R&D in aggregate ([Pierre Fabre methodological note 2025](https://www.pierre-fabre.com/sites/pierre-fabre.com/files/2025-06/6-efpia-disclosure-methodological%20note-2025.pdf)). Event costs paid via third parties for named HCPs must be reported individually as indirect ToVs ([EFPIA Code 2026](https://www.efpia.eu/media/uzqpperi/efpia-code-of-practice-2026.pdf)). Patient-organisation support needs a written agreement stating amount and purpose and describing significant indirect or non-financial support, and companies must publish a list of supported POs with monetary values, descriptions of non-monetary benefit and total contracted-services fees per PO per year; the E&CC made a PO disclosure template **mandatory from the 2026 disclosure of 2025 ToVs**, standardised methodological notes on the same timetable, and set a unified downloadable, searchable-by-recipient publication standard **mandatory for the 2027 disclosure of 2026 data** ([EFPIA Code Report 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf); [LIF: Nytt i LER](https://www.lif.se/globalassets/etik/dokument/ler/nytt-i-lakemedelsbranschens-etiska-regelverk.pdf)). AI is not yet a Code amendment but sits in the E&CC's 2027 priorities ([EFPIA Code Report 2025](https://www.efpia.eu/media/vrbdngg0/efpia-code-report-2025.pdf)). No EFPIA-wide central database exists; the "central platforms" are national (Disclosure UK, transferofvalue.ie, Transparantieregister Zorg, betransparent.be, LIF's samarbetsdatabas). GDPR determines the publication basis per country: Spain's DPA accepted legitimate interest, Bayer UK moved to legitimate interest with no opt-out from 1 January 2023, while consent-based countries such as Poland require aggregation on refusal or withdrawal ([EFPIA blog, 7th publication](https://www.efpia.eu/news-events/the-efpia-view/blog-articles/the-efpia-disclosure-where-are-we-for-the-7th-publication/); [Bayer UK](https://www.bayer.co.uk/en/what-the-efpia-disclosure-code-is-about); [Ipsen Expert Consent, May 2026](https://www.ipsen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Expert-Consent-for-Disclosure-of-Transfer-of-Value-ENG.pdf); [EFPIA Q&A 2016](https://www.efpia.eu/media/15481/efpia-disclosure-code-your-questions-answered-march-2016.pdf)).

The **UK ABPI Code 2024** (in effect 1 October 2024, fully in force 1 January 2025) is the current code; the PMCPA opened a Constitution and Procedure consultation on 3 August 2026 with a new C&P planned for early 2027 and expects Code-update engagement later in 2026 ([ABPI news, Sept 2024](https://www.abpi.org.uk/media/news/2024/september/2024-abpi-code-of-practice-launched-with-a-new-constitution-and-procedure-for-the-pmcpa/); [PMCPA consultation](https://pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news/pmcpa-consults-on-changes-to-its-constitution-and-procedure)). Clause 23 requires donations and grants to support healthcare, research or education, not induce prescribing, be prospective, not bear a medicine's name and never go to individuals, under a written agreement certified per Clause 8.3 that describes the donation, its objective, the parties and how support will be acknowledged; HCO ToVs are disclosed under Clause 28 and PO support under Clause 29 by 30 June ([PMCPA Clause 23](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-23-donations-and-grants/); [PMCPA Clause 8](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-8-certification-and-examination/); [PMCPA Clause 28](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/the-code/2024-interactive-abpi-code-of-practice/clause-28-annual-disclosure-of-transfers-of-value-to-health-professionals-other-relevant-decision-makers-and-healthcare-organisations/)). The 2025–2026 case record is a catalogue of documentation failures rather than improper intent: Moderna's voluntary admission (Case/0646/06/25) found breaches of Clauses 5.1, 8.3 and 23.2 because grants committed since April 2024 were not certified by a UK Final Medical Signatory ([PMCPA Case/0646/06/25](https://pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/case06460625-voluntary-admission-by-moderna/)); Roche breached Clause 23.2 twice because written agreements did not state that the grant must be acknowledged "and apparent from the start" ([AUTH/3676/7/22](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/auth3676722-complainant-v-roche/); [AUTH/3851/11/23](https://pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/auth38511123-complainant-v-roche/)); Novartis breached Clause 27.2 for an unsigned PO donation agreement blamed on a team restructure ([Case/0816/12/25](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/cases/completed-cases/case08161225-complainant-v-novartis/)); CSL Seqirus was ruled in breach of Clause 2 on **8 October 2026** for failing to disclose substantial payments to an HCO over three years, with three grants to a France-based HCO undisclosed or incorrectly disclosed and no written agreement ([PMCPA press release, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.pmcpa.org.uk/about-us/media/news/pmcpa-press-release-8-october-2026-case-ruled-in-breach-of-clause-2/)); and Novo Nordisk was suspended from ABPI for two years in March 2023 and later admitted failing to disclose **£7.8 million** in 2020–2022 payments ([ABPI statement](https://www.abpi.org.uk/media/news/2023/march/novo-nordisk-is-suspended-from-abpi-membership/); [University of Bath](https://www.bath.ac.uk/announcements/novo-nordisk-found-in-breach-of-transparency-standards-by-pmcpa/)). On the recipient side, NHS England's 2017 conflicts-of-interest guidance requires sponsorship to deliver clear benefit, sponsors to lack dominant influence over content, involvement to be identified, and sponsored posts to carry written confirmation that purchasing and prescribing are unaffected, audited for the duration ([NHS England guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/managing-conflicts-of-interest-in-the-nhs/); [NHS Standards of Business Conduct v2.3](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/standard-of-business-conduct-v2.3.pdf)); the Charity Commission's 4 March 2024 guidance tells trustees their starting point is to accept donations but that some must be refused and risks weighed ([UK Fundraising](https://fundraising.co.uk/2024/03/04/commission-issues-new-guidance-on-accepting-refusing-or-returning-donations/)); and the ECCTA "failure to prevent fraud" offence took effect **1 September 2025** for large organisations with a reasonable-procedures defence modelled on the Bribery Act ([Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/the-economic-crime-and-corporate-transparency-act-and-the-failure-to-prevent-fraud/)).

**France** is the most procedurally demanding country in scope. Since **1 October 2020**, any benefit from a company to a health professional, student, HCP association or covered entity must be governed by a written convention transmitted to the competent Ordre (or ARS) and is subject to prior declaration below, or prior authorisation at or above, the thresholds of the arrêté of 7 August 2020; sources cite **EUR 5,000 per convention for research donations**, **EUR 1,000 for association donations** and **EUR 10,000 for associations reconnues d'utilité publique**, with an "8,000" figure whose category was truncated, so the arrêté must be read directly ([ONPP loi anti-cadeaux](https://www.onpp.fr/juridique/loi-anti-cadeaux.html); [Légifrance JORFTEXT000042234024](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000042234024); [Tours note](https://odontologie.univ-tours.fr/medias/fichier/loi-anti-cadeaux-comm-deontologie-09112020-export_1605266197475-pdf?ID_FICHE=288121&INLINE=FALSE); [Vigier Avocats](https://www.vigier-avocats.com/lac-publication-des-arretes-fixant-les-montants/)). Declarations go through IDAHE 2 (Ordre des médecins) or EPS (other Ordres and ARS); paper-filed conventions are not processed, and the Ordres published their first biennial reports in 2025 ([DGS FAQ](https://sante.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/eps_antikdo_faq_annexes.pdf); [Vigier: rapports bisannuels](https://www.vigier-avocats.com/encadrement-des-avantages-lea-premiers-rapports-bisannuels-ordres/)). Separately, Transparence Santé requires publication of every convention regardless of amount and every benefit of **EUR 10 or more** (rounded to the euro), with semester deadlines reported as 1 September and 1 March ([Transparence Santé FAQ](https://www.entreprises-transparence.sante.gouv.fr/content/pdf/DocumentFAQ.pdf); [Vigier: dispositif transparence 2024](https://www.vigier-avocats.com/dispositif-transparence/)). **Italy**'s Sunshine Act (Legge 62/2022) is enacted but not operational as of September 2026 because the activation notice for the Sanità Trasparente register has not been published and the implementing decree remains in draft, with rumoured start dates having passed ([Osborne Clarke, March 2026](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/italys-sunshine-act-system-ready-still-not-operational); [Guida Sunshine Act, 17 Sept 2026](https://guidasunshineact.it/)); in the meantime the Farmindustria Code (21 February 2025 edition; a July 2026 revision reported by a single source) requires non-nominative congress invitations sent to public institutions at least **60 days** ahead, caps meals at **EUR 60** in Italy, and bans economic incentives for attendance ([Farmindustria Code Feb 2025](https://www.farmindustria.it/app/uploads/2018/06/2025-FEBBRAIO-21New.pdf); [Farmindustria Code 2022](https://www.farmindustria.it/app/uploads/2018/06/2022-APRILE-5.pdf)). **Germany**'s FSA codes (Kodex Fachkreise 2024; Transparenzkodex; PO-Kodex December 2024) distinguish Spende (no consideration), Sponsoring (advertising value) and Fördermitgliedschaft (membership fee), require publication between **20 and 30 June**, public availability for three years and documentation for five, and FSA members reported about **EUR 768.7 million** to HCPs/HCOs and **EUR 9.9 million** to POs for 2024 ([FSA-Kodex Patientenorganisation 2024](https://www.fsa-pharma.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/FSA-Kodex-Patientenorganisation-2024.pdf); [FSA Transparenzveröffentlichungen 2025](https://www.fsa-pharma.de/transparenzveroeffentlichungen-2025/)). **Spain**'s Farmaindustria Code (June 2025) governs HCO donations in section 18.3 and publishes four categories on company websites under legitimate interest ([Farmaindustria Código 2025](https://www.codigofarmaindustria.org/sites/sarfi/docs/PRODF329156.pdf)). The **Netherlands** reports relations above **EUR 500 per recipient per year** to the Transparantieregister Zorg, with recipients checking entries before **1 July** and publication mid-July; from 1 January 2026 the register was renewed and approval must be given on the agreement itself ([CGR Nr. 9](https://cgr.nl/nr-9-voor-zorgprofessionals-zorgorganisaties-en-patientenorganisaties-controleer-voor-1-juli-uw-financiele-relaties-in-het-transparantieregister-zorg/); [CGR Nr. 8](https://cgr.nl/nr-8-wijzigingen-toelichting-gedragscode-per-1-januari-2026/)). **Belgium**'s Sunshine Act requires annual disclosure on betransparent.be (benefits since 1 January 2017, including in kind) operated by Mdeon, which must also issue a prior visa for sponsored events involving an overnight stay or multi-day online events with offered registration ([betransparent.be](https://betransparent.be/en/about-the-transparency-register/); [Chambers 2026 Belgium](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/pharmaceutical-advertising-2026/belgium)). **Portugal** is event-driven: any subsidy, sponsorship or grant must be communicated to Infarmed **within 30 working days** of materialising, by both funder and recipient, into a searchable public list ([Infarmed Circular](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1878988/Circular+Informativa_079+CD/0f8955d9-182b-41ff-93c1-86344a6463bc); [Infarmed FAQ](https://www.infarmed.pt/web/infarmed/perguntas-frequentes-area-transversal/placotrans)). **Sweden**'s LER (updated 29 September 2025) requires reporting collaborations to LIF's database **within three months** of the activity, with the EFPIA template mandatory from 2026 and searchability from 2027 ([LIF](https://www.lif.se/globalassets/etik/dokument/ler/nytt-i-lakemedelsbranschens-etiska-regelverk.pdf)). **Ireland**'s IPHA Code V8.6 (1 June 2025) publishes on transferofvalue.ie by the first working day of June, with PO disclosures moving to the central site for 2028 data; 2025 totals were **EUR 30.2 million** with **99% of HCPs** providing named transparency ([IPHA](https://www.ipha.ie/a-record-level-of-99-of-healthcare-professionals-have-provided-named-transparency-on-www-transfersofvalue-ie/)). **Switzerland** revised both Pharma Codes on 22 May 2025 (donations to HCOs and POs only, 15.5–15.7) ([Chambers 2025 Switzerland](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/pharmaceutical-advertising-2025/switzerland/trends-and-developments)); **Austria**'s Pharmig VHC bars donations to individual HCPs (Art 8.5d) and issued Verordnung 1/2025 standardising methodological notes ([Pharmig Transparenz](https://www.pharmig.at/pharmaindustrie/transparenz/)); Greece (Law 4316/2014 art. 66), Turkey (prior written approval above a minimum-wage-linked threshold) and Romania have statutory disclosure ([BioMarin](https://biomarin.eu/our-company/transfer-of-value-reporting/); [CBC Law Turkey](https://www.cbclaw.com.tr/en/healthcare-series-2-disclosure-transfers-value-pharmaceutical-companies-turkey)).

For devices, the **MedTech Europe Code** (September 2024 edition; Disclosure Guidelines dated February 2026) has barred direct sponsorship of individual HCPs to third-party conferences since 1 January 2018; support flows through educational grants to HCOs that select participants, every educational grant to a European HCO is disclosed **without value limit** on Transparent MedTech by end of August, the September 2024 text requires grant agreements to let the company verify use for the agreed purpose, single-practitioner organisations generally cannot receive grants, and events must pass the Conference Vetting System with submission **at least 50 days** before the event ([MedTech Europe Code Sept 2024](https://www.medtecheurope.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/code-september-2024.pdf); [MTE Disclosure Guidelines Feb 2026](https://www.ethicalmedtech.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/MTE-Code_disclosure_guidelines.pdf); [MTE Code Q&A 2020](https://www.medtecheurope.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/2020_MTE_MedTech-Europe-Code-of-Ethical-Business-Practice-QA-DG.pdf); [MedTech Europe CVS](https://www.medtecheurope.org/news-and-events/news/new-conference-vetting-system-features/)); Medtronic states its first Code-basis disclosure of 2026 data is due by 31 August 2027 ([Medtronic Transparent MedTech Europe](https://www.medtronic.com/en-ie/our-company/transparent-medtech-europe.html)). At EU level, Article 94 of Directive 2001/83/EC bars gifts and benefits to prescribers unless inexpensive and relevant to practice, and the pharma package (political agreement 11 December 2025; compromise texts 6 March 2026; Council adoption reported 28 September 2026; application expected around 2028) removes the "minimal intrinsic value" exception so any gift able to influence an HCP is caught ([Council press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/11/pharma-package-council-and-parliament-reach-a-deal-on-new-rules-for-a-fairer-and-more-competitive-eu-pharmaceutical-sector/); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/eu-pharma-package-advertising-compromise-proposal); [EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/about-us/what-we-do/reform-eu-pharmaceutical-legislation)).

**What this means for the platform.** The European rule set needs a per-country configuration object holding: recipient-eligibility rules (organisation-only everywhere; MedTech Europe adds a single-practitioner block); purpose categories (healthcare, research, education); the prior-approval mechanism (France: convention with beneficiary type, competent Ordre/ARS, whole-duration amount, category and automatic declaration-versus-authorisation routing with an execution block until the lead time has run and the IDAHE 2/EPS record number is stored; Belgium: Mdeon visa number and date for overnight/multi-day events; MedTech Europe and Mecomed: CVS decision reference with a 50-day lead rule); mandatory agreement clauses (ABPI: description, objective, parties, "acknowledged and apparent from the start"; EFPIA PO: amount, purpose, indirect and non-financial support, applicable codes; MedTech Europe: use-verification clause; UK: Clause 8.3 certification by a named signatory as a gate before payment); product-name screening of grant titles and materials; prospective-only enforcement (block retrospective funding); disclosure outputs (EFPIA HCO and PO templates by 30 June, searchable and downloadable from 2027; Disclosure UK; FSA 20–30 June; Transparantieregister with recipient pre-approval before 1 July and the >EUR 500 aggregation; betransparent.be including in-kind; Infarmed 30-working-day event-driven filings for both parties; LIF three-month rolling submissions; Transparence Santé semester files of all conventions and benefits ≥EUR 10; ## Rest-of-world regimes add FCRA, institutional review and data-residency constraints

Outside the US and Europe the pharma codes are IFPMA-derived and the medtech codes APACMed- or Mecomed-derived, so the organisation-only, no-inducement, written-agreement core holds; what varies is the prior-approval step, the disclosure category scheme and the money-movement rules. The **IFPMA Code** in force is the 2019 edition (effective 1 January 2019), which added a total ban on gifts and promotional aids for prescription medicines on top of the 2012 entertainment ban and moved to a values-based "Ethos"; IFPMA has signalled an update and issued a Note for Guidance with IAPO on protecting the independence of patients and patient organisations receiving support, but no 2025 revised Code surfaced ([IFPMA news](https://www.ifpma.org/news/expanded-ifpma-code-of-practice-for-pharmaceutical-industry-goes-into-effect-around-the-world/); [PharmaBoardroom](https://pharmaboardroom.com/articles/the-ifpmas-new-guidance-on-patient-patient-organisation-interactions/)). National codes republish it as baseline: SAPI 2025 (Singapore), IRPMA October 2024 effective 1 January 2025 (Taiwan), HKAPI 21st edition of 7 October 2022 (no grants to individual HCPs; recipients must acknowledge support), PhAMA Edition 23 (2025), FIFARMA in Latin America ([SAPI Code 2025](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/SAPI-Code-2025.pdf); [IRPMA Code](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/IRPMA-Code-of-Practice-October-2024-English.pdf); [HKAPI 21st ed.](https://www.ifpma.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/21st-edition_HKAPI-Code-of-Practice.pdf); [PhAMA publications](https://www.phama.org.my/index.cfm?menuid=6); [FIFARMA](https://fifarma.org/en/publications/ifpma-code-of-practice/)). The **APACMed Code** (2023 revision) lets members fund third-party events only through educational grants to organisers, HCOs, professional associations or bona fide grant administrators, forbids selecting or influencing which HCPs attend, requires written merit, venue and agenda assessment, and limits direct spend to booths, satellite symposia, registration and reasonable travel ([APACMed LEC Code 2023](https://apacmed.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/APACMed-LEC-Code-2023.pdf); [APACMed FAQ May 2023](https://apacmed.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/LEC-FAQ_13-MAY-2023.pdf)).

**Canada** has no in-force statutory sunshine law: Ontario's Health Sector Payment Transparency Act received Royal Assent on 12 December 2017 but is "not yet in force" and its regulations were shelved before the 2018 election ([Ontario e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/17h25/v1); [CMAJ 2019](https://cmaj.ca/content/191/8/E241)). The IMC Code (2020 revision) separates grants, donations, sponsorships and collaborations/partnerships, bars funding to individuals, requires agenda and venue assessment of third-party conferences, and annexes patient-group transparency guidelines (Annex A); only eight to ten of 48 IMC members publish aggregate voluntary disclosures in three categories (HCP service fees, HCO funding, international-congress travel) ([Smart & Biggar](https://www.smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/updates-to-the-imc-code-of-ethical-practices); [IMC ethics page](https://innovativemedicines.ca/about/ethics/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5769443/big-pharma-paying-doctors-hospitals/); [The Conversation 2024](https://theconversation.com/canadians-need-to-know-how-much-money-big-pharma-gives-health-care-providers-but-this-information-is-far-too-difficult-to-find-225066)). **Japan** discloses by JPMA category — A (R&D), B (academic research support including the uniquely Japanese scholarship donations), C (manuscript and lecture fees), D (information provision), E (other) — and the **1 April 2026** Transparency Guideline revision adds advertising fees and supporting-member fees, applying to business years beginning January 2027 per a Japanese footnote; the JPMA Code of Practice was revised in May 2025 effective 1 October 2025; the Clinical Trials Act (amended effective 31 May 2025) requires COI management, certified-committee review and public funding disclosure for company-funded "specified clinical trials"; and the Mie University/Ono case showed a scholarship donation used to increase prescribing in a hospital department ([JPMA 2026 notice](https://en.jpma.or.jp/information/code_compliance/news/2026/260401.html); [Medius disclosure page](https://www.medius.co.jp/company/governance/transparency/?lang=en); [JPMA Code notice](https://en.jpma.or.jp/information/code_compliance/news/2025/251001.html); [PMC case study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8761953/)). **China** requires donations to healthcare entities to follow the NHFPC 2015 Measures (not-for-profit public institutions and public-welfare social organisations only; no donations involving unfair competition or commercial bribery), layered with the July 2023 anti-corruption campaign, SAMR's **14 January 2025** compliance guidelines that flag donations, sponsorships and grants as high-risk, and the revised Anti-Unfair Competition Law in force **15 October 2025** ([Covington 2015](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2015/10/china_adopts_new_rules_on_healthcare_donations.pdf); **What this means for the platform.** A "code family" attribute (IFPMA-derived pharma, APACMed/Mecomed/MedTech Europe-derived medtech) and a per-country code-edition register (SAPI 2025, IRPMA Jan 2025, HKAPI 2022, PhAMA 2025, APACMed 2023, Mecomed Dec 2024, IMC 2020/2022, JPMA Oct 2025, MA Ed 20, MTAA Ed 14) drive country overlays. Specific encodings: Japan tags every payment with category A–E plus the two FY2027 sub-items, captures recipient at institution and department level with case counts and yen totals, applies heightened controls to scholarship donations, and stores specified-clinical-trial COI records; China restricts recipients to eligible public-welfare entities, requires a written donation agreement, evidence of the recipient's institutional review, a purpose code from the 2015 permitted list and a hard block on any link to procurement, tenders or consumables, and either keeps a China-resident instance or tracks cumulative annual personal-data exports against the 1 million / 10,000 thresholds; Korea generates the expenditure report by category with HCP/HCO identifiers, retains documents five years, produces a de-identified HIRA file and applies conference-eligibility checks (hours, credits, participant counts, booth caps, no-overlap rule); Australia cuts HCO grant, donation and sponsorship data by MA's transition periods with the next file due 31 October 2026; India requires, for any foreign-source grant, the FCRA registration or prior-permission number, validity date, the SBI NDMB account as sole payee account, a no-onward-transfer declaration, in-kind tagging as foreign contribution, and an administrative-expense check, while domestic Indian funding applies the UCPMP bans (no monetary grants to HCPs, CME only in India, research only under authorised studies); Brazil captures the physician CRM number and emits the data a physician needs for a CRM-Virtual declaration within 60 days plus a Minas Gerais export; Mexico and Colombia enforce legal-entity recipients and a "not sole funder" check for patient organisations; Canada keeps individual-level disclosure as an off-by-default switch while rolling up the three voluntary categories annually; and Mecomed/APACMed grants require a CVS reference and an attendee-selection block.

## Three enforcement waves map to four control failures the platform must hard-stop

Enforcement since 2004 falls into three waves, each exposing a different mechanism. The first was educational grants used for off-label promotion: Serono's **$704 million** (2005), Cephalon's **$425 million** (2008), Eli Lilly's **$1.415 billion** Zyprexa settlement (2009, with terms requiring website disclosure of grants), AstraZeneca's **$520 million** and Allergan's **$600 million** (2010) and Novartis's **$423 million** (2010), and the April 2007 Senate Finance Committee report that found companies had separated grant-making from marketing yet ACCME records still showed numerous cases of excessive influence over content ([Wikipedia list of settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_pharmaceutical_settlements); [Lilly 2009 10-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000059478/000095015209001897/c49534exv10w17.htm); [Senate Finance Prt110-21](https://finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/Prt110-21.pdf); [FDA Law Blog, May 2007](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2007/05/senate_finance_/)). Allergan's 2010 CIA required bi-annual website posting of medical-education grants and charitable contributions to US institutions (recipient, description, amount) and an Independent Review Organization ([Allergan CIA](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/850693/000119312510202820/dex102.htm)). The second wave was devices and copay charities: Olympus's **$646 million** 2016 settlement (reported as $623.2 million for criminal and civil components by one firm), in which a $50,000 research grant was held up until a hospital signed an equipment purchase ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/leading-scope-manufacturer-to-pay-hefty-settlement-for-alleged-kickbacks-and-bribery/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2016/03/02/468875852/endoscope-maker-olympus-agrees-to-646-million-settlement-over-kickbacks); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/olympus-to-pay-6322-million-to-resolve-allegations-kickbacks)); Merit Medical's **$18 million** (2020) for "educational" grants given only to selected purchasers to reward sales ([Plastics Today](https://www.plasticstoday.com/medical/medical-device-manufacturer-agrees-to-18-million-settlement-on-kickback-scheme)); Biotronik's **$12.95 million** for an abused training programme ([DOJ C.D. Cal.](https://justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/cardiac-device-manufacturer-biotronik-inc-agrees-pay-nearly-13-million-settle)); and the copay-foundation series described above, including Novartis's **$51.25 million** co-pay settlement alongside its **$678 million** sham-speaker-program settlement in July 2020 and the foundations themselves (PAN $4 million, Chronic Disease Fund $2 million, The Assistance Fund $4 million and a three-year Integrity Agreement) ([Novartis release, 2 July 2020](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-resolves-legacy-litigation-matters-finalizing-settlement-speaker-program-litigation-government-us-and-positioning-company-future-fully-scaling-its-next-generation-digital-engagement-technologies); [DOJ SDNY release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/acting-manhattan-us-attorney-announces-678-million-settlement-fraud-lawsuit-against); [Phillips & Cohen](https://www.phillipsandcohen.com/pharma-donations-to-patience-assistance-foundations-are-basis-for-latest-kickback-cases/); [Citeline](https://insights.citeline.com/PS141245/CoPay-Charity-Settles-Charges-It-Conspired-With-Teva-Biogen-Novartis-On-Kickbacks/)). The third wave is FCPA and UK Code cases where the vehicle was a donation, sponsorship or grant and the finding was undocumented, mis-recorded or uncertified payments.

Four failure modes recur. Grants were initiated, conditioned or steered by sales (Olympus, Merit, Medtronic's sales staff expensing a surgeon's restaurant events as "educational discussions"). Charity funds were defined narrowly enough to cover only the donor's product and donation sizing was driven by the company's own patient forecasts (Astellas asked foundations to create funds limited to Xtandi's category) ([FiercePharma, Astellas/Amgen](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/astellas-amgen-fork-over-125m-doj-s-latest-charity-kickback-settlements)). Data flowed back from charities to donors. And documentation failed: no written agreement, missing mandatory clause, no final-signatory certification, mis-booked GL category, disclosure gaps across multi-year grants (CSL Seqirus's three-year gap; Novo Nordisk's £7.8 million). The lesson for design is that nearly every 2024–2026 sanction in the UK would have been prevented by a workflow hard-stop rather than by better judgement.

**What this means for the platform.** Four non-negotiable gates: (1) sales cannot initiate, approve or be notified of grant status, grant decisions cannot reference purchasing, and the system correlates grants against sales territories and purchasing history as a compliance analytic rather than a scoring input; (2) PAP donations capture disease-fund breadth, forbid product earmarking, block any utilisation or patient-level data intake from the charity, and require a documented budget rationale independent of product forecasts with annual legal review; (3) recipient organisations are screened for beneficial owners and officers who are HCPs or public officials, and sponsorship decisions are decoupled from sales metrics; (4) agreement templates with mandatory clauses, a certification gate before payment, accurate GL coding, and an automatic disclosure feed that reconciles grants-system records against AP and transparency submissions, with multi-year grants re-validated each cycle so they do not drift out of documentation.

## Industry operating practice is already a de facto SOP the platform can standardise

Public funder portals describe a remarkably uniform process. Requests are filed through a vendor-hosted or in-house portal at least **60 calendar days** before the activity (Phathom, Apellis, AbbVie — whose portal refuses a start date inside the window — Acadia, Lundbeck, Boehringer Ingelheim, Mirum, PTC, Abbott, Novartis, Medtronic), **120 days** for ex-US organisations at Lilly, **8 weeks** at Stryker, and **90 days** for MSD patient-advocacy event grants since 1 October 2025; retroactive funding is refused ([AbbVie How to Apply](https://www.abbvie.com/science/independent-educational-grants/how-to-apply.html); [Lilly Grant Office Requirements](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/requirements); [Stryker](https://www.stryker.com/us/en/about/contact/donations.html); [MSD portal notice](https://msd.versaic.com/login); [Novartis PME grants](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/educational-grants/professional-medical-education-grants); [Medtronic FAQ](https://www.medtronic.com/en-us/our-company/governance/grants-donations/donation-faqs.html)). Scope changes must be requested 30 days ahead (argenx) ([argenx grants](https://argenx.com/grants)). Decisions come within 60 days in the US and 90–120 days ex-US at Lilly, within 90 days of a complete submission at J&J, up to eight weeks at Merck, and 15–45 days at Phathom ([Lilly FAQ](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/faq); [J&J educational grants](https://www.jnj.com/innovativemedicine/us/grants-and-giving/educational); [Merck guidelines, Feb 2025](https://www.merck.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2024/01/Merck_GrantApplicationGuidelines.pdf); [Phathom](https://www.phathompharma.com/our-science/ime-grants/)). Payment is released only after a signed letter of agreement and can take up to 45 days (Pfizer), paid only to the CME provider (Apellis) ([Pfizer Process Overview](https://cdn.pfizer.com/pfizercom/responsibility/education/process_overview.pdf); [Apellis](https://apellis.com/about-us/corporate-giving/independent-medical-education/)). Close-out requires a post-activity reconciliation within 60 days (Pfizer), 90 days (AbbVie, Boehringer Ingelheim) or 45 days with a separate outcomes form and a cheque for unused funds (Merck patient advocacy), an outcomes report within five business days of a live activity (argenx), and at Lilly monthly Level 1 and quarterly Level 2–4+ outcomes data entered in the portal ([Merck US Patient Advocacy](https://merckuspatientadvocacy.com/after-you-apply); [Lilly Requirements](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/requirements)). Pfizer's competitive RFP programme caps applications at 15 pages, allows overhead up to **28%** of budget and uses an external review panel for specific RFPs ([Pfizer CGP FAQ](https://pfe-pfizercom-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/CompetitveGrantProgram_FrequentlyAskedQuestions.pdf)); Phathom signals a soft **$15,000** cap ([Phathom](https://www.phathompharma.com/our-science/ime-grants/)). Exclusion lists are explicit: Novartis will not fund overhead, recreational components, gifts to speakers or attendees, or spouse-inclusive meals, limits patient-education grants to 501(c)(3), (c)(4) and (c)(6) organisations, bars sponsorships tied to promotional exhibits or clinical-trial awareness, and states grants are never linked to prescribing, purchasing, formulary status or reimbursement and carry no benefit beyond an acknowledgement ([Novartis FAQs](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/faqs); [Novartis Sponsorships and Memberships](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/sponsorships-and-memberships)); Abbott funds fellowships only at US teaching hospitals and universities and limits educational-grant budgets to non-transfer-of-value costs ([Abbott CV grants](https://www.cardiovascular.abbott/us/en/hcp/grants-donations/product-charitable-donations.html)); AbbVie accepts requests only from its pre-qualified IME Provider Network and Genentech only from the accredited provider ([AbbVie](https://www.abbvie.com/science/independent-educational-grants/how-to-apply.html); [Genentech IME](https://www.gene.com/good/giving/corporate-giving/imed)). Award lists are published by Merck (grants above $500, quarterly in the US via gPRIME files listing payee, city, state and type) and Lilly (a 2025 grant registry) ([Merck transparency](https://www.merck.com/company-overview/sustainability/transparency-disclosures/); [Lilly registry 2025](https://grantoffice.lilly.com/assets/pdf/grant-registry-2025.pdf)). Context numbers matter for sizing: ACCME's 2024 data report shows record total income of **$3.7 billion** with commercial support **down 5%** year on year and registration fees at 55% of revenue ([ACCME 2024 data report](https://accme.org/news/accme-releases-2024-data-report-evolving-impact-in-a-shifting-landscape/)); commercial support fell from 50.3% of CME income in 2006 to 39.2% in 2009 ([EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/cme-who-will-pay-the-tab/)); many teaching hospitals cannot accept industry funding directly because of institutional policy ([J Cancer Educ 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13187-014-0664-4)).

Requestor pain is structural rather than surveyed: a dozen-plus distinct portals with overlapping but non-identical document lists and clocks, duplicate applications for joint calls (a Bayer–Lilly joint call required two separate applications), and 2026 migrations that freeze intake (Novartis closed NGCS to new submissions on **1 October 2026** with a new platform launching **16 November 2026**; Boston Scientific moved to a new grantee experience on **20 July 2026**) ([Bayer–Lilly joint call](https://www.acehp.org/Portals/0/Joint%20Call%20for%20Grants%20Proposal_Bayer%20and%20Lilly_Tumor-agnostic%20testing_FINAL_1.pdf); [Novartis](https://www.novartis.com/us-en/healthcare-professionals/external-funding/sponsorships-and-memberships); [Boston Scientific](https://www.bostonscientific.com/en-US/corporate-responsibility/giving/grants-donations-exhibits/educational-conference.html)). A 2007 CME-provider survey already cited long decision times, no access to reviewers and inconsistent grantor policies ([MM+M 2007](https://www.mmm-online.com/med-ed-report-cmes-open-gesture/article/24765/)). Patient organisations face rising financial pressure (EURORDIS 2024 survey) while only 47.1% of UK recipient POs self-report payments, 4.5% of UK POs exceed 50% dependency, 72% of POs contributing to NICE appraisals held manufacturer or competitor funding, and 12% of US PAOs receive more than half their funding from industry ([EURORDIS](https://www.eurordis.org/publications/navigating-change-membership-survey-report/); [ScienceDirect UK PO study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168851019301915); [Ozieranski et al. 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9566.13409); [BMJ Open review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6334181/); [JAMA IM](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2598094)). A 2009 JAMA panel said societies depending on industry for more than **25%** of budget should reassess, and SVS reports about 20% industry revenue with a duty to disclose the share ([amednews 2009](https://amednews.com/article/20090413/profession/304139976/2); [SVS policy](https://vascular.org/about/policies/industry-relations-and-conflict-interest-coi/management-relationships-industry)). No funder publishes KPIs such as time to decision, approval rate or information-request rounds, and no company announced AI-assisted grant review as of October 2026.

**What this means for the platform.** The common schema is a reusable organisation profile (legal entity, tax ID, tax-exempt or accreditation evidence, COI policy, by-laws, bank details, total annual income) plus a standard application package (letter of request on letterhead, needs assessment, objectives, agenda, itemised budget with cost categories, outcomes plan, W-9/W-8) that a requestor fills once and submits to many tenants. Tenant-configurable parameters cover lead time (default 60 days; 90/120 options; a portal hard-stop on dates inside the window), seasonal or twice-yearly intake windows and RFP/call-for-applications cycles with budgets, soft and hard amount caps, overhead percentage limits, excluded cost categories, pre-qualified provider networks, scope-change notice (30 days), payment SLA after LOA acceptance, reconciliation deadline (45/60/90 days) with unused-funds return, outcomes cadence (monthly Level 1, quarterly Level 2–4+), and public award-list publication. A configurable revenue-concentration control (25% society benchmark, 50% PO dependency threshold) should use recipient-reported total income. Since no benchmarks exist, the platform instruments its own: time to decision (target ≤60 days), percentage of agreements executed before activity start (target 100%), information-request rounds, overdue reconciliations and disclosure-feed accuracy.

## AI governance, security and audit expectations define the agent's guardrails

The AI-regulatory picture as of October 2026 is clearer than a year ago. The EU AI Act's Digital Omnibus, **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, entered into force on 27 July 2026 and deferred Annex III stand-alone high-risk obligations from 2 August 2026 to **2 December 2027** and Annex I product-embedded AI to 2 August 2028, while **Article 50 transparency obligations**, GPAI duties and AI Office powers applied from **2 August 2026** (watermarking grace to 2 December 2026 for pre-existing systems) ([Orrick, July 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/); [Formalize](https://formalize.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus)). Annex III's listed areas (employment, education, essential services, credit) do not obviously include corporate grant decisions to organisations, so limited-risk treatment is the more likely reading, but no authoritative classification was found and a documented per-deployment assessment is prudent. US state law fragmented: Colorado's AI Act was repealed and re-enacted on 14 May 2026 as a narrower ADMT statute effective **1 January 2027**; Texas TRAIGA took effect 1 January 2026 without an impact-assessment regime; California's CCPA risk-assessment rules apply from 1 January 2026 with ADMT notices and opt-outs from 1 January 2027 ([Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-legal-roundup-colorado-postpones-implementation-of-ai-law-as-california-finalizes-new-employment-discrimination-regulations-and-illinois-disclosure-law-set-to-take-effect.html); [Baker Botts, Jan 2026](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/january/us-ai-law-update); [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/workplace-ai-regulation-in-2026-how-employers-can-navigate-the-changing-legal-landscape)). The most consequential document for a compliance platform is DOJ's **23 September 2024 ECCP revision**, under which prosecutors ask how a company identifies, assesses and mitigates AI risk, whether it has tools to check the accuracy of data it relies on, whether it continuously tests that technology functions as intended, and "how does the company manage the quality of its data sources and measure the accuracy, precision, or recall of its analytics models?" — with a DOJ official explicitly flagging "false approvals and documentation generated by AI" ([Cooley, 9 Oct 2024](https://investigations.cooley.com/2024/10/09/doj-focuses-on-ai-emerging-tech-in-newly-issued-guidance-updates-for-evaluating-corporate-compliance-programs/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/doj-updates-evaluation-of-corporate-compliance-programs-guidance-focused-on-ai-and-emerging-technologies/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-whistleblowers-and-data-analytics-updated-doj-compliance-guidance)). ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable AI management-system standard and NIST AI RMF plus the AI 600-1 generative profile the voluntary reference; neither establishes AI Act compliance alone ([SecureW2](https://securew2.com/blog/nist-ai-rmf-vs-iso-42001); [CASRAI ISO 42001](https://casrai.org/wp/?p=1468)). Enterprise human-in-the-loop practice warns that confidence scores alone should not control approval, that models are often most confident when they have misunderstood, that approval should be required for irreversible, costly or sensitive actions, that over-gating produces rubber-stamping, and that approval packets should show the proposed action, confidence components, evidence and policy reason ([DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/human-in-the-loop-approval-enterprise-ai-agents); [Xano](https://www.xano.com/blog/the-human-in-the-loop-isnt-a-constraint-its-how-you-trust-autonomy/); [Rioja](https://alejandrorioja.com/human-in-the-loop-ai-agents-when-to-build-an-approval-gate/)). Natural-language analytics must be approached with similar caution: raw-schema text-to-SQL scores about **21% on Spider 2.0** and roughly 55% on BIRD with curated evidence, while semantic-layer approaches report 87–98% on curated metrics ([BenchmarkList Spider 2.0](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/spider_2_0/); [Beancount BIRD note](https://beancount.io/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/06/06/bird-benchmark-text-to-sql-real-database-gap); [Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/text-to-sql-accuracy-benchmarks-semantic-layer)). LLM generation of workflows from SOPs is research-grade (JPMorgan's SOPStruct decision-tree DAGs with planner-based soundness checks; BPMN-from-SOP studies), with no production product found ([arXiv 2504.00029](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00029); [Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/bpmn-based-business-process-collaboration-modeling-using-large-l/51773402)).

Security and assurance follow pharma TPRM norms: SOC 2 Type II and/or ISO 27001 are the baseline, HITRUST is reserved for high-risk PHI vendors, and the Shared Assessments SIG Lite (140–200 questions) is the questionnaire backbone, with most enterprises accepting a clean SOC 2 in its place ([Censinet](https://censinet.com/perspectives/hitrust-certification-third-party-risk-assessment-role); [Macktez SIG Lite](https://macktez.com/university/sig-lite)). A grants system is normally non-GxP so 21 CFR Part 11 does not formally apply, but FDA's audit-trail expectations — who, what, when, why; protected from modification or disabling — are what customers ask for anyway ([FDA Part 11 scope guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/part-11-electronic-records-electronic-signatures-scope-and-application); [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/biopharma/articles/audit-trail-requirements-for-a-digitalized-regulated-laboratory-401729)). The converged tamper-evident design is an application-side hash chain anchored periodically to WORM storage (S3 Object Lock in compliance mode) with RFC 3161 timestamps, continuous verification and export-then-tombstone retention so GDPR erasure does not break the chain ([Archman](https://archman.dev/docs/data-architecture-and-persistence/data-lifecycle-and-compliance/auditing-and-tamper-evident-logs); [Gravitee tamper-evident logging](https://www.gravitee.io/corpus/gen-1025/title-44-of-the-united-states-code/tamper-evident-logging-for-content-changes.html); [DEV NestJS article](https://dev.to/elwin_ernst/building-a-tamper-evident-audit-log-in-nestjs-with-hash-chains-4l76)). Identity follows SAML/OIDC SSO with SCIM provisioning keyed on a stable externalId, a managed auth provider rather than per-customer SAML wiring, and, for pooled Postgres, row-level security on every tenant table with tenant context set per request as a safety net behind application-level scoping ([SSOJet](https://ssojet.com/docs/en/enterprise-sso/directory-sync/integrations/entra-id-scim); [Clerk](https://clerk.com/articles/microsoft-entra-id-saml-integration-for-saas-apps-2.md); [AWS Prescriptive Guidance RLS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/saas-multitenant-managed-postgresql/rls.html); [ClickHouse guide](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/multi-tenant-saas-postgres-architecture)). Integration patterns are standard: Salesforce REST/Composite, Bulk API 2.0 and Pub/Sub for events (API v66.0 as of Spring '26); DocuSign Connect JSON webhooks with HMAC-SHA256; SAP S/4HANA Cloud's API_BUSINESS_PARTNER and API_PURCHASEORDER_PROCESS_SRV via OAuth communication arrangements, with BAPI/IDoc for ECC; and iPaaS at roughly $15k–$500k+ per year depending on vendor and volume ([Salesforce REST API limits](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.api_rest.meta/api/resources_limits.htm); [Hookdeck DocuSign guide](https://hookdeck.com/webhooks/platforms/guide-to-docusign-webhooks-features-and-best-practices); [Sim.ai SAP](https://docs.sim.ai/integrations/sap_s4hana); [AutomationAtlas Boomi vs MuleSoft](https://automationatlas.io/guides/boomi-vs-mulesoft-2026-comparison/)). Accessibility and signatures: the European Accessibility Act applied from **28 June 2025**, EN 301 549 v3.2.1 maps to WCAG 2.1 AA with overlays rejected, and UK/EU e-signature law is permissive enough that simple or advanced signatures suffice for grant agreements, with QES optional ([Allyant EAA](https://allyant.com/eaa-compliance-checklist-key-steps-to-meeting-2025-accessibility-requirements/); [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/?p=11022390); [DocuSign UK legality](https://www.docusign.com/en-gb/how-it-works/legality/global/the-united-kingdom)). Data-residency constraints beyond China (India's DPDP Rules 2025 with a government blacklist approach to transfers; PIPL thresholds) were only partly sourced ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html)).

**What this means for the platform.** The AI layer is a set of named agents with intended-use statements, each limited to: completeness and document checks (W-9, COI policy, by-laws, accreditation statement), duplicate-organisation and prior-funding detection, policy-rule triage and risk flagging, drafting of summaries and committee packs, drafting of agreement clauses from templates, and SOP-to-workflow drafting into a versioned DSL for human review with soundness validation. Agents never approve, decline, pay, publish or sign; the autonomy dial is a per-tenant, per-action policy table (observe, suggest, act-with-approval, act-and-notify) keyed on reversibility and monetary or compliance impact, with confidence bands as a secondary signal and a hard floor that any consequential outcome has an evidenced human decision. Article 50 compliance means requestor-facing assistants disclose that they are AI and label AI-generated text. Each AI action is a first-class event on the same hash chain as human actions: event id, tenant, agent id, model name and version, prompt template version, retrieved sources with hashes, output or output hash, score components, policy evaluated and gate outcome, the human decision (accept, modify, reject; who, when, why) and downstream action ids. Models are versioned with documented accuracy, precision and recall testing on curated evaluation sets, drift monitoring, prompt and output logging, and contractual no-training-on-customer-data terms — the artefacts DOJ's ECCP and ISO 42001 both expect. Natural-language analytics run over a governed semantic layer exposing defined metrics with tenant and role context propagated so RLS cannot be bypassed, every question, generated query and result hash logged, and the generated definition shown to the user. The audit trail is immutable, time-stamped, user-attributed, captures prior and new values and reason-for-change, covers every state change, score, approval, screening result, document upload, payment and policy exception, supports e-signature manifestation (name, time, meaning) on attestations and certifications, and exports per-tenant signed JSON/CSV with a chain-verification manifest; retention defaults to ten years with per-tenant policy and legal hold, covering the EFPIA five-year minimum and US False Claims Act look-back. Security posture targets SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 with a HITRUST path, tenant-scoped encryption keys for documents, SSO plus SCIM for internal users, a separate B2C identity realm (magic links or passkeys with MFA step-up, no CAPTCHA-only login) for requestors, RBAC with tenant-scoped roles plus attributes (therapeutic area, country, request type, amount band) for routing and visibility, pool-model Postgres with RLS and tenant-leading indexes, and regional hosting options for EU/UK data and a China-resident option. Integrations expose canonical objects (Organisation/HCO, Payee/Vendor, Funding Request, Agreement, Payment, Disbursement Status) through native connectors (Salesforce, DocuSign/Adobe Sign, SAP OData, Coupa), outbound webhooks and an OpenAPI spec, with front-end-configurable field mapping, lookups, scheduling and retry, while tenant-specific ERP authorisations and supplier-onboarding flows are set up by professional services or iPaaS. Workflow and form definitions are versioned, tenant-scoped artefacts (BPMN or JSON DSL; JSON Schema plus UI schema) and running requests pin the version they started on; per-tenant design tokens drive white-label requestor portals that meet WCAG 2.2 AA with a published accessibility statement.

## Conclusion

The research changes the design question from "which rules apply?" to "which parameters vary?". Across every jurisdiction examined, the substantive prohibitions are the same six: no funding to individuals, no sales influence, no conditioning on business, objective and documented criteria, a written agreement before the activity, and public acknowledgement plus annual disclosure. What differs is procedural and numeric — thresholds, prior-approval authorities, lead times, filing windows, lawful bases and retention — and the 2025–2026 enforcement record (Moderna, Roche, Novartis UK, CSL Seqirus, Medtronic's Sunshine finding, Teva) shows that companies are now sanctioned for process defects a workflow can hard-stop rather than for intent a reviewer must judge. That argues for an architecture in which a small, stable global rule core is implemented as non-bypassable gates, and everything jurisdictional lives in versioned, effective-dated configuration with a compliance calendar, so that a CPI threshold change, the lapse of AO 24-02 on 1 January 2027, the Italian Sunshine Act's activation or the next EFPIA template becomes a data change rather than a release.

The second shift concerns AI. No pharma or medtech company publicly uses AI to decide grants, no incumbent vendor advertises it, and the two most relevant authorities — DOJ's ECCP question about "false approvals and documentation generated by AI" and the EU AI Act's Article 50 duties in force since August 2026 — both reward the same design: agents that triage, check, summarise and draft, never decide; every AI action recorded on the same tamper-evident chain as human actions with the human's accept-modify-reject decision attached; and model accuracy evidenced the way DOJ asks for it. Built that way, the AI layer is not a compliance risk to be mitigated but the very artefact that demonstrates an "effective compliance programme" under the March 2026 enforcement policy — provided the forty-odd verification gaps listed above are closed against primary sources before any of the expected values become rules.
